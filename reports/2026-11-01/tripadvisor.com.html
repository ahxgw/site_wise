Context

tripadvisor.com is the US/English edition of Tripadvisor, the travel-review platform founded in February 2000. IAC bought it in 2004 and spun it into Expedia in 2005, and Expedia spun it off as a public company in December 2011 (NASDAQ: TRIP). The company now calls itself "experiences-led": Viator tours drive growth, hotel metasearch shrinks, and TheFork (European restaurant bookings) is being sold to American Express for $700M. The site itself is the content engine: more than a billion reviews and opinions on over nine million places, served on 40+ country domains in 20+ languages. For an index builder the delta is the gap between crawlability and permission. HTML pages return 200 to an honest research UA and carry rich JSON-LD, including one permalink per review, and the sitemaps sit in a world-readable S3 bucket. Yet the terms of use forbid any robot or AI system from indexing the content. The only licensed path is the Terra partner API, whose own caching policy forbids storing or indexing anything but location IDs.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where tripadvisor.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Every URL embeds typed integer keys: g = geo, d = location, r = review, i = image, k = forum topic. The slug is decoration. Key on (type, id): the same d6672003 appears on 49 locale domains (hreflang) and in Terra responses. Review and photo permalinks carry their parent g and d, so joins need no lookup.

Sitemap origin outside the edge

The DataDome and CloudFront edge 302s sitemap requests to a public S3 website endpoint, so the census never touches the protected host. Parts are fixed at 50K URLs, which means a HEAD-free count is (parts − 1) × 50K plus the last part. The French site moved to a newer /sitemap/3/ pipeline served from its own domain, so expect the English set to migrate and the S3 path to disappear.

Crawlable ≠ licensed

Unlike most challenged sites in this series, the HTML is open: 200 with server-rendered reviews for an honest UA. The barrier is contractual. ToS clause (x) bans any "robot, spider, artificial intelligence (AI) system" that would "index any portion of the Services". Terra's caching policy bans storing or indexing even licensed content. A compliant index can hold IDs and links, plus whatever a feed contract grants.

robots.txt as a geo-fence for AI agents

ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot and Gemini-Deep-Research are allowed the site except for two geo-ID prefixes covering parts of Spain, the Netherlands, Switzerland and Belgium. That looks like region-specific licensing or legal carve-outs (Inferences). Google-Extended's single allowed URL (Dallas restaurants) reads as a test or showcase. Diff this file monthly.

Authoritative rating field

Take ratings from JSON-LD aggregateRating (decimal, e.g. 4.9). The og:description template reported "rated 4 of 5" for the same property, so do not parse ratings from meta or snippet text. Review dates are prose ("October 17, 2026") and need locale-aware parsing on non-English domains.

Locale fan-out

Each locale publishes its own review permalinks: the English set holds 311M and the French set ~41M (823 parts × ~50K, calculated by Site Wise). Reviews appear on the domain of their language, so a full-modal index needs a per-locale census that collapses on r-ID, not on host.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the place (d-ID), with aggregate rating, geo point and amenity facets, plus its reviews (r) and photos (i) as child objects. Geos (g) act as hub objects.
  • Primary keys & joins: d/g/r/i/k integers from URLs; BreadcrumbList for the geo hierarchy; JSON-LD geo to join with map and POI datasets; Viator product codes for tours.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Terra Feed contract (daily location, geo, photo and review files): the only path that permits storage. 2) S3 sitemaps, weekly, for the ID and URL graph and coverage counts, which are metadata only. 3) Terra Discover per-location calls for a small, display-time set, without caching. 4) Viator affiliate API for tours. HTML fetch is technically open but contractually barred; use it for single-page research samples only.
  • Recrawl cadence: review sitemap index daily (about 1 new part/day); place indexes weekly; robots.txt monthly; feeds daily within their 12-day retention.
  • Derivatives to generate: under a feed licence, per-place review-aspect summaries and multilingual review embeddings; a geo-ID → coordinates gazetteer from JSON-LD; photo embeddings from photo-s thumbnails.
  • Demo to build: a "coverage atlas" that needs no content at all. Plot English review-permalink density per g-ID from the sitemap URLs alone, showing where Tripadvisor's 311M reviews concentrate and which cities gained the most reviews this quarter.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred Open HTML is not a stable entitlement. Basis: DataDome is already deployed on every response (x-datadome: protected, Probe Log) but did not challenge ~20 paced requests. Its thresholds are tunable, and the ToS already prohibits the access, so enforcement can tighten without notice.
  • inferred Content value is being steered into licences. Basis: hotels revenue fell 23% YoY and the 10-K blames AI overviews and search changes, while the company built Terra (usage-priced API, daily feeds, an "Agentic Search" endpoint) and licensed data to Perplexity (Verified Metrics). Expect bulk review access to stay priced.

Inferences

  • The g1875*/g188* exclusions for OpenAI and Gemini agents are jurisdictional carve-outs rather than editorial choices. Basis: they apply only to live-answer AI agents (not to * or bingbot), and the matched IDs in sitemap Tourism pages resolve to Spanish, Dutch, Swiss and Belgian geos, not to a content type.
  • The English review sitemap is a subset of reviews, split by language. Basis: 311M English permalinks against "more than one billion" reviews and opinions company-wide, plus a separate 823-part French review set whose URLs sit on .fr.
