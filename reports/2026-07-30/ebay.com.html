Context

eBay is the web's largest inventory of individually described used and collectible goods — 2.5B live listings written by sellers rather than derived from a manufacturer catalog. That inverts Amazon: instead of one canonical product record with competing offers, eBay carries millions of near-duplicate, hand-written listings whose condition and provenance live in free text and photos. It monetizes a take rate on ~$80B of GMV plus $2.0B of seller advertising. For an index builder the tension is sharp: the listing detail page holds the value and is the one surface eBay refuses to serve to generic clients, while the aggregation pages above it hand out priced, imaged, structured records for free. inferred eBay wants to be cited by assistants but not learned from or transacted through. Basis: robots.txt gives training crawlers Disallow: / while Claude-User/ChatGPT-User inherit the permissive * rules, and the same file's prose bans agent-driven checkout (Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where eBay differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Two ID namespaces that do not interchange: web URLs use a bare numeric legacy item ID (/itm/117323896652), the REST APIs use v1|117323896652|0 with the third field as variation. Store both or you cannot join a crawled page to a Feed row. Strip itmmeta, hash, itmprp, _skw and var before keying — the JSON-LD emits all of them (Probe Log). Every country site is a separate namespace.

Retrieval unit

Inverted from Amazon. The listing is the unit, not the product: one physical object is one listing, so condition, completeness and photos differentiate rather than price alone. The /p/ EPID catalog covers only the mass-produced tail (21.5M entries against 2.5B listings) and collapses exactly the variation that matters for used goods.

Cross-modal joins

Everything hangs off the numeric item ID: the VIDEO sitemap keys video and thumbnail to /itm/{id}, and aggregation JSON-LD keys image and price to the same. eBay is therefore one of the few large sites where a text record, a priced offer, a thumbnail and a playable video stream join without any authenticated call. EPID joins to catalog prose and reviews; seller ID to store and feedback.

Authoritative content field

Titles are stuffed to the character limit and are the weakest field. For used goods the photo is ground truth and the description is the seller's claim about it — the reverse of catalog commerce, and why image and video derivatives carry unusual weight here. Catalog prose on /p/ describes the product line, not the item for sale, and must never merge into a listing record.

Freshness & recrawl

Two populations with different physics, and the sitemap names them: AUCTION (2.5M) expires on a clock of days, VIS/GTC (47.3M) persists for months while repricing. The ALL_ACTIVE bootstrap is a Sunday snapshot published Wednesday (Verified Metrics), so a bulk-only index carries a structural 3–10 day lag on a corpus whose fast population partly no longer exists.

Quality & spam

Near-duplicate listings from one seller across categories, titles borrowing brands the item is merely "compatible with" (visible in the sampled JSON-LD), and the counterfeit risk motivating Authenticity Guarantee. Promoted Listings make the 60-item JSON-LD slice an ad-influenced ranking, not an organic one.

Internal vs external search

eBay splits its own search surface: the sitemapped /shop/{slug}?_nkw= form is crawlable and serves structured results while the canonical /sch/i.html?_nkw= returns 403 (Probe Log). The 3.1M /shop/ pages are a purpose-built SEO layer — eBay chooses which keyword searches exist externally. Queries here run condition- and variant-heavy (model + year + "OEM", "NOS", "for parts", PSA grades), and numeric part numbers demand exact-match, not semantic, retrieval.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the listing, keyed by marketplace + numeric item ID, with photos, HLS video, price and seller attached. EPID-linked catalog prose and reviews stay a separate joined record.
  • Primary keys & joins: marketplace + itemId (store legacy numeric and v1|id|var); epid → catalog and reviews; sellerId → store and feedback; categoryId → browse tree.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Aggregation-page crawl — 23.0M /b/ + 3.1M /shop/ pages × 60 priced JSON-LD items; zero auth, and the only path yielding price without a contract. 2) VIDEO sitemap — 13.3M listings with directly fetchable HLS and thumbnails. 3) Feed API — the only true bulk path for full descriptions, gated behind Partner Network membership and lagged 3–10 days. 4) Browse API getItem for aspects, metered per call. 5) /itm/ crawl — closed; do not design around it.
  • Recrawl cadence: by sitemap class, not site-wide. AUCTION daily or not at all (most rows die before a weekly pass). GTC weekly for existence, repriced from aggregation pages far more often. BROWSE/PRP monthly. Images and video never — the CDN marks them immutable.
  • Derivatives to generate: image embeddings as the primary dedup and condition signal (photos outrank titles here); keyframe + ASR over the 13.3M videos, where sellers actually demonstrate defects and function; title de-stuffing to separate the item from "compatible with" brand noise; a legacy-ID ↔ REST-ID concordance table.
  • Demo to build: a no-auth slice builder — walk the BROWSE and VIDEO sitemaps, harvest the 60-item payload from CollectionPage.about.offers.itemOffered, pull matching HLS thumbnails, emit joined JSONL with price. It would show how much of a 2.5B-listing marketplace is indexable with no contract, and where the 403 wall starts.

Risks to the Index

  • The open window is a JSON-LD implementation detail, not a commitment. The 60 priced items sit in CollectionPage.about.offers.itemOffered while the standards-compliant ItemList is empty (Probe Log). A template refactor could remove the payload with no policy announcement.
  • Licensing forbids the obvious use. The API License Agreement bans ingesting Restricted API data into third-party gen-AI models without written consent (Verified Metrics). An index feeding a generative product needs consent or must confine itself to crawl-derived data whose own terms stay restrictive.
  • Feed API access is relationship-gated, not money-gated. Limited Release, Partner Network membership plus business-unit approval, and eligibility explicitly not a guarantee (Feed API overview). No self-serve tier to fall back to.
  • Bulk-only indexing is structurally stale. The ALL_ACTIVE snapshot reflects Sunday and lands Wednesday; auctions turn over inside that window, so a feed-only index misrepresents availability on its fastest-moving 2.5M objects.
  • inferred Agentic-commerce enforcement will tighten before crawl policy does. Basis: robots.txt was re-versioned to v29_COM_July_2026 with new prose singling out "buy-for-me agents" and citing the User Agreement, while the listing paths available to Claude-User and ChatGPT-User were left permissive (Probe Log) — the pressure is on transacting, not reading.
  • inferred eBay is building an ads-for-assistants channel. Basis: OAI-AdsBot has its own robots block with no Disallow: / and an explicit Allow: /b/*?*mkcid=32*, an affiliate/marketing-channel-tagged URL form (Probe Log). Category pages reachable by an ads crawler may diverge from what organic crawlers see.

Inferences

  • eBay wants assistant citation and affiliate traffic, not training or autonomous purchase. Basis: the robots.txt tier split (ten training crawlers at Disallow: / versus *-equivalent rules for Claude-User and ChatGPT-User), plus the prose ban on buy-for-me agents and the API terms' gen-AI clause.
  • The 2% sitemap ratio is deliberate SEO selection, not an incomplete sitemap. Basis: seven indexes hand-partitioned by listing type (AUCTION/GTC/VIDEO) and SEO surface (BROWSE/KWSRP/PRP/NGS), children uniformly packed to 39K–49K with only the last partial, and 3.1M purpose-built keyword pages — the shape of a curated feed, not a truncated dump.
  • Product reviews are crawl-excluded by a mis-specified rule, not by intent. Basis: Allow: /urw/*/product-reviews/ cannot match the live form /urw/product-reviews/{epid}, so the adjacent Disallow: /urw/ governs; the Allow's existence shows reviews were meant to be crawlable.
  • Photos, not titles, are authoritative for used inventory. Basis: sampled JSON-LD titles are stuffed to the length limit and borrow brands the item is only "compatible with", while each listing carries multiple immutable CDN photos of the specific object.
