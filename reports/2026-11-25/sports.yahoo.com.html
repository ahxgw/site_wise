Context

Yahoo Sports is the sports vertical of Yahoo, which Verizon sold to Apollo funds in 2021 in a deal worth about $5B. Yahoo says its Sports Network averages 113M US monthly unique visitors. Yahoo discloses no Sports-only revenue; the business runs on advertising plus a BetMGM sportsbook tie-in that dates from 2019. For an index builder the notable fact is that this is mostly an aggregation layer. In 12 of 12 sampled articles, the canonical URL and the JSON-LD provider point to another publisher (USA TODAY network, SI, Heavy, ClutchPoints and others), and Yahoo keeps its own URL as a second copy. The site is unusually open to a polite crawler: four sitemap trees, a rolling news sitemap, full-text RSS, and an unauthenticated HLS manifest with WebVTT captions. Page weight is the cost, at roughly 500–590KB of HTML per article. See also shutterstock.com for a walled counterpart.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Identifier & dedup

The article ID is a trailing number in the slug (-210757148.html), and the leading segment varies (/articles/ versus /{league}/articles/), so key on the numeric tail. In the 2026-11-24 sitemap, 5,924 URLs use /articles/, 2,027 use a league prefix and 3 use /article/. Dedup across the web goes by canonical URL: 12 of 12 sampled pages canonicalize to the original publisher.

Retrieval unit

One article (title, dek, body, provider, author, dates) with its lead image and caption. A video page is its own unit with its captions; a player page is a thin card keyed by Yahoo ID.

Cross-modal joins

Article to image by sitemap entry (stable per URL). Video to article by the VideoObject in the same page's JSON-LD, identifier being a UUID. Player and team to article by nfl.p./nfl.t. IDs in the page payload; whether those IDs are stable across seasons is open.

Authoritative content field

For syndicated stories, the original publisher's page wins on conflict; the Yahoo copy is a mirror whose text may be trimmed (RSS bodies ran 509–10,196 characters). provider in JSON-LD names the true source; publisher is always Yahoo and carries no information.

Freshness & recrawl

Scores and injury news decay in hours; recaps and prediction pieces are good for days; standings change daily. The rolling news sitemap plus the per-league RSS give a cheap pull. Archives before 2024-01-01 have no sitemap.

Quality & spam

Quality varies by partner bucket: wire and newsroom copy (AP, USA TODAY) next to fan blogs and betting-pick pages (the 2026-11-24 sitemap includes many "X vs Y prediction" URLs). A partner trust tier from provider is the natural first filter.

Internal vs external search

On-site search was not probed. Users reach most articles from Google News and Discover (inferred below) rather than from the site's own search box.

User query profile

Entity-plus-event queries ("Packers score", "Zacha trade"), fantasy start/sit questions and fixtures, which favor fresh, entity-linked retrieval over deep text.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: article (text, lead image, caption), video page with captions, and team/player entity cards.
  • Primary keys & joins: numeric article ID from the slug; canonical URL for cross-web dedup; provider as source; video UUID; Yahoo nfl.p./nfl.t./nfl.g. IDs for entities.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) RSS content:encoded for full text of the latest 50 per feed (no auth; about 29 feeds, window short); 2) news-sitemap-index.xml for the 48h rolling set (about 15.9K URLs, no auth); 3) daily sitemaps for back-fill to 2024-01 (about 3.4M URLs; at about 500KB per HTML page a full fetch is about 1.7TB, so prefer RSS and canonical-follow for text); 4) Fantasy API for league data (application, read-only).
  • Recrawl cadence: news sitemap every 1–2h; RSS every 15–30min in season; standings and scoreboard daily or live-window; player and team pages weekly; articles older than 7 days once.
  • Derivatives to generate: captions from HLS where present and ASR only for videos without them; OCR not justified; embeddings on title plus first 1K characters; a provider trust score.
  • Demo to build: a canonical-collapse check: take one day's sitemap, fetch each article's canonical, and measure what share of Yahoo URLs resolve to an external original and how many publishers share one story.

Risks to the Index

  • Named-bot Disallow: / already covers 43 AI and scraping UAs. A new rule naming a generic or research UA would close the open sitemap and RSS paths.
  • Partner licenses can end at any time, which would remove a partner's stories and possibly their canonicals; redirect and 404 behavior for withdrawn partner content is untested.
  • Signed fastly_token URLs on video and captions expire, so store the page and UUID, not the URL.
  • The Next.js payload and the April 2026 sitemap change show the markup is still moving; parsers need fixtures per page type.

Inferences

  • Yahoo Sports is mainly an aggregation layer, with most URLs mirroring partner content. Basis: 12 of 12 sampled canonicals were off-site, JSON-LD provider named a third party each time, and 937 partner buckets appear in one day's image URLs.
  • The 2026 growth in daily URLs reflects more syndicated intake rather than more staff output. Basis: daily URLs rose from 4,706 (2026-02-18) to 7,954 (2026-11-24) while the partner buckets dominate image URLs.
  • The news sitemap is a rolling window of about 48 hours. Basis: its oldest news:publication_date was 2026-11-23T02:21Z against a probe at 2026-11-25T02:14Z.
  • Five of the player sitemaps are truncated at 1,000. Basis: five files held exactly 1,000 entries while NBA (543) and WNBA (207) ended below it.
  • Googlebot and Bingbot are governed by the * group, so they may crawl articles. Basis: robots.txt has no group naming either bot, and the * group does not disallow /articles/.
  • Most human traffic arrives from external search and aggregators. Basis: the daily sitemaps carry a news-sitemap tree with news:publication tags, a format used for Google News.
