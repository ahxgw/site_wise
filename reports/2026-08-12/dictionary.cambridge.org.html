Context

Cambridge Dictionary is the free, ad-supported learner-dictionary property of Cambridge University Press & Assessment. Cambridge has published learner dictionaries since 1995 and put them online free in 1999. The corpus is editorial, not user-generated: lexicographers working from the Cambridge English Corpus write senses, examples and pronunciations, stacked several dictionaries deep on a single URL. The 2026-08-10 cambridge.org report treated the dictionary as the traffic-bearing half of a two-property domain; this one indexes it on its own terms.

For an index builder it is a rare thing: a large, professionally edited lexical database served as static HTML with no JavaScript requirement and no login. It is also almost entirely a duplicate of itself — the same English dictionary republished under 26 self-canonicalising locale prefixes. Crawl posture and commercial posture point opposite ways: robots.txt leaves entry pages open to *, while the edge admits almost nothing and the licensing page routes all bulk use to a sales conversation.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The slug is the only key, and it is not unique: 26 locale prefixes republish it and every copy self-canonicalises, so all 26 are separately indexable by design. Dedup cannot lean on rel=canonical here; collapse on (slug, corpus) and keep locale as an attribute. This single decision removes roughly 96% of the URL population.

Retrieval unit

The sense, not the page. A query like "what does run mean" is answered by one of 59 senses across 3 stacked dictionaries; returning the 924KB page is the wrong granularity. The site provides no sense ID, so the retrieval unit has to be synthesised — see the indexing plan.

Cross-modal joins

Headword ↔ pronunciation ↔ examples ↔ thesaurus ↔ collocations are five separate URL families joined only by parallel slug — a string match that breaks across locales. The audio join is worse: mp3 paths are partly opaque (ukb00724.mp3) and not derivable from the headword, so audio can only be attached by parsing the entry page that references it.

Authoritative content field

No conflict to resolve and no SEO distortion to discount: definitions are lexicographer-written and the meta description is a truncation of the first one. The stacking is the subtlety — cald4 (learner), cacd (academic) and cbed (business) give three register-specific definitions of the same word, and which is "right" depends on the query's register. Spam and ranking manipulation do not apply: there is no UGC and no ranking surface. The trust question inverts — this is a ground-truth corpus, useful for validating lexical data scraped from noisier sources.

Freshness & recrawl

There is no freshness signal at all: zero lastmod site-wide and a uniform changefreq/priority on every URL. Combined with an ~18K-per-year edit rate on a corpus of this size, incremental recrawl has nothing to steer on. Diff against the blog feed and a periodic full re-census instead.

Internal vs external search

The site is built to receive external search traffic, not to run discovery. /search/, /browse/, /autocomplete/ and /spellcheck/ are disallowed across all 26 locales while entry pages stay open, and the sitemaps enumerate entries with no navigational surface.

Crawl policy vs edge policy

The two disagree completely, and the edge wins. robots.txt grants * access to every entry page and even tunes crawl-delay for bingbot and Applebot; the edge then refuses both. Reading robots.txt alone would give you a badly wrong picture of what is actually fetchable.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the sense — (slug, corpus, source-dictionary, part-of-speech, sense-ordinal) — carrying its definition, guideword, examples, IPA and audio reference. Roughly 115.5K headwords expanding to a low-seven-figure sense count; the exact figure is open.
  • Primary keys & joins: synthesise a sense key from data-dataset-id + part-of-speech index + sense ordinal + guideword, since the markup provides none. Anchor everything on (slug, corpus) with locale demoted to an attribute. Use data-example-id — the one genuinely stable ID on the page — as the join and change-detection key for examples.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Licensed data set or API — the only lawful bulk route; quote-only, no free research tier, and it covers 11 bilingual pairs against the 31 published, so it under-covers what you can see. 2) Sitemaps — free, complete and structurally informative; the enumeration and census layer even if you never fetch a page. 3) Blog RSS — the only incremental signal. 4) Direct crawl — robots-permitted, edge-refused; treat as unavailable, and do not route around the challenge.
  • Recrawl cadence: the corpus is near-static, so spend nothing on periodic revisits. Re-census the sitemaps quarterly, poll the blog feed weekly, and re-fetch entry pages only when a census or feed item implicates them.
  • Derivatives: embeddings over definitions and examples at sense granularity; a locale-collapse table keyed on (slug, corpus); a slug→audio-path map extracted at parse time, since that mapping cannot be reconstructed later. No ASR — the audio is single-word pronunciation with the IPA already published as text.
  • Demo to build: a sense-level retriever over one locale's 115.5K headwords that answers register-sensitive queries ("run in a business sense") by selecting among the cald4/cacd/cbed stack — the claim being that sense-level indexing beats page-level on exactly the queries this site receives.

Risks to the Index

  • The edge is the policy, and it can tighten silently. Access depends on a user-agent allowlist that admits Googlebot and refuses bingbot, Applebot and ClaudeBot alike — nothing contractual or announced. Any pipeline that depends on fetching pages is one Cloudflare rule from zero.
  • No published TDM reservation is not permission. The absence of tdmrep.json, llms.txt and any AI-crawler line means no machine-readable reservation — but the licensing page and API terms route bulk use to a negotiated contract, and the parent domain does publish a reservation. Read the silence as unsettled, not as opt-in.
  • The licensed corpus is narrower than the visible one: 11 licensable bilingual pairs against 31 published, so an index built on what the site shows is not reproducible under a licence covering what Cambridge sells.
  • Getty-sourced images are a hard stop — the blanket third-party attribution means no image here can be mirrored or displayed, whatever a Cambridge licence covers.
  • Locale duplication inflates every estimate that skips dedup: trusting raw sitemap counts over-provisions by roughly 26× on the English corpus and misreports coverage.

Inferences

  • Cambridge sells lexical data rather than defending it from crawlers, and the edge wall is a commercial instrument rather than a crawl policy. Basis: robots.txt permits * on every entry page and publishes complete sitemaps, while the edge refuses all but Googlebot's UA and /license.html routes every bulk use to a quote; the archived API hub states outright that there is no free research access.
  • The 26 locale prefixes are an SEO surface rather than a translation surface, at least for the English dictionary. Basis: 100% slug overlap across the root, /us/ and /es/ copies of english/sitemap1.xml, combined with self-canonicalising /us/ pages and 84 hreflang alternates on a single entry — the copies are addressed separately and claim independent canonical status while carrying the same headword set.
  • The corpus is maintained on an annual editorial cycle rather than continuously. Basis: zero lastmod elements site-wide, a uniform changefreq/priority on all 25,001 sampled entries, and an Annual-Report edit rate of ~18K additions per year against a 115.5K headword corpus.
  • Sense-level structure exists internally but is deliberately not exposed. Basis: the markup carries stable numeric IDs for source dictionaries and for individual corpus examples, yet all 59 sense containers on the sampled page are bare <div class="pr dsense"> with no identifier, and the site publishes no JSON-LD at all.
