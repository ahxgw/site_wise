Context

amazon.de is Amazon's German storefront and its largest outside the US. It went live 28 years ago today, on 15 October 1998, when Amazon renamed telebuch.de, the shop of the Regensburg-based ABC Bücherdienst it had bought that April. It also serves Austria (amazon.at 302s here), and its language switcher offers Czech and Danish shoppers their own interfaces. Unlike India, Amazon may hold first-party inventory here, so retail and third-party offers compete for one Buy Box. That Buy Box is now under a German competition order (Risks). For an index builder, amazon.de sits between the two Amazon postures profiled so far. Like amazon.in it serves full detail pages to an unauthenticated client, but it closes that surface at the IP level after a short burst. Its pages carry more than amazon.in's: full review bodies, HLS product-video manifests and "bought in the last month" counters all ship in the raw HTML (Probe Log). All three Amazon reports agree on one thing: there is still no sitemap and no JSON-LD.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where amazon.de differs from the default assumptions and from the amazon.com and amazon.in profiles.

Price is a function of the fetcher

Without a currency cookie, a crawler outside the eurozone indexes converted prices, such as "31,37 USD" on a German page. The conversion uses whatever rate applied at fetch time. Pin i18n-prefs=EUR, store the currency with every price, and treat countryCode as part of the fetch context, because availability and delivery text follow the inferred address, not the marketplace.

Review provenance

The review module mixes in reviews written on other EU and EMEA stores, about a third of the sample. Key each review on its source marketplace as well as the ASIN. Otherwise a pan-EU index will store the same review once per storefront that displays it.

Language trees

The pattern differs from amazon.in: the alternate languages here are interface-plus-title translations for neighbouring markets without their own Amazon store (Denmark, Czechia). Dutch, Polish and Turkish shoppers have their own stores (.nl, .pl, .com.tr), and robots.txt closes those trees on .de. Canonicalise on (DE, ASIN) as on amazon.in, and keep the German page as the source of truth.

Crawl budget is per IP, not per path

Once the captcha trips, it applies to every product path, whatever the user agent, cookie or locale prefix (Probe Log). It persisted beyond ten minutes of slower re-tries. Liveness checks should hit a detail page, since / is always challenged, and must treat a 3.8KB 200 response as a block, not as content.

Signals absent elsewhere

"X+ Mal im letzten Monat gekauft" is a public, bucketed sales-velocity signal in the HTML. It is the closest thing to demand data on any Amazon surface profiled so far, and it is useful for ranking and for recrawl prioritisation.

User query profile

Expect German compound nouns ("Entkalkungsdosen", "Kaffeevollautomaten") in both queries and titles. Decompounding is mandatory for lexical recall. The canonical slugs show that Amazon itself strips umlauts inconsistently: Entkalkungsvorg%C3%A4nge keeps the ä percent-encoded, while DeLonghi drops the apostrophe.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: product = (DE, ASIN). Reviews are child documents keyed (source marketplace, review ID). Offers are a fast-refresh sub-record keyed (ASIN, seller, currency).
  • Join keys: the ASIN parsed from the path, never the slugged canonical; media ID for images; manifest URL for video. Austria and Danish/Czech shoppers collapse into the same DE records.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A low-rate distributed crawl of the German /dp/ tree, seeded from /gp/bestsellers and the 43 browse indexes, with an EUR cookie and an EU exit. 2) Creators API Offers V2 for authoritative EUR prices. 3) The /-/en/ tree for English titles where they exist. Danish, Czech and full review history: no bulk path.
  • Recrawl cadence: offers daily, and again around 2026-11-05 when the Buy Box rules change. Titles and media weekly. Reviews monthly, since only the rotating top 13 is reachable. Velocity badges weekly, as a recrawl-priority signal.
  • Derivatives: a DOM attribute extractor (no JSON-LD), a German decompounder, HLS keyframe and ASR extraction for product video, and per-review language ID to tag cross-border reviews.
  • Demo to build: a "same ASIN, four fetch contexts" diff. Fetch from a US exit without a cookie, a US exit with the EUR cookie, the /-/en/ tree, and the /-/da/ tree, then show which fields move (currency, availability, title language) and which stay fixed (rating, count, image set).

Risks to the Index

  • Buy Box rules change on a set date. On 2026-02-05 the Bundeskartellamt banned the price-control mechanisms that pulled high-priced seller offers out of the Buy Box or off the marketplace. It set a nine-month compliance window, ending 2026-11-05, and ordered €59M disgorged (Bundeskartellamt; deadline per Bird & Bird). Amazon said it would challenge the decision (retail-news.de). Any Buy Box-winner time series recorded before and after that date measures a policy change, not market behaviour.
  • The API pointer embedded in the site is dead. The PA-API 5 locale page now redirects to a deprecation notice, and calls return AccessDenied … Please migrate to Creators API (notice). The captcha page still advertises the old Partnernet API URL (Probe Log). Integrations that follow Amazon's own in-page pointer will fail.
  • inferred The crawl surface is open by policy but closed by rate. Basis: robots.txt permits /dp/ and the first ~14 requests returned full pages, but the same client was then captcha-walled on every product path for 10+ minutes (Probe Log). Throughput, not legality, is the binding constraint, which makes a single-IP crawl of a catalogue this size impractical.
  • inferred The DSA gives regulators data that no indexer can see. Basis: the H1 2026 transparency ZIP reports recipients per member state plus notice and moderation counts (10_AMAR.csv, 8_automated_means.csv), but it has no catalogue or listing-removal count per store. Delisted ASINs therefore surface only as 404s on recrawl.

Inferences

  • The crawl surface is rate-closed rather than policy-closed. Basis: robots.txt permits /dp/, and the captcha onset followed request volume, not path. Identical paths returned 200 with full content before onset and the interstitial after it.
  • The Danish and Czech trees exist to serve shoppers in markets without their own Amazon store. Basis: the language picker offers exactly the languages of the .de neighbours, and robots.txt re-opens Danish (no Amazon store) but not Dutch, Polish or Turkish (each with its own store).
  • No indexer-visible per-store catalogue or removal count exists. Basis: the DSA CSV set covers recipients, notices and moderation by member state, but no file breaks catalogue size or listing removals down by store host.
