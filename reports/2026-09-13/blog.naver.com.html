Context

Naver Blog launched as "페이퍼" on Jun 4 2003 and took its current name on Oct 13 2003. It is not a standalone blogging product but the user-generated supply side of Naver Search, which held 62.86% of Korean search in 2025. Blog posts are what Naver's own search — and now its AI Briefing — answers with, which is why the platform's economics run backwards from a normal blog host: Naver does not sell blogging, it sells the search results the blogs fill, and since Jun 2026 it pays 3,000 creators a month according to how often AI Briefing cites them.

The crawl posture follows from that: robots.txt names nine AI agents at Disallow: / under a header calling AI training and RAG "strictly prohibited", and blocks Naver's own crawler Yeti first of all. Meanwhile the corpus is already inside a model — blog text was the single largest source in HyperCLOVA's pretraining mix. The asymmetry, not the content, is the story for an outside index.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site departs from the default platform assumptions.

The canonical URL is the wrong URL

The default assumption is that the canonical URL serves the content. Here it serves a noindex frameset carrying the blog's title, not the post's, with the body one iframe deeper. Crawl m.blog.naver.com/{blogId}/{logNo}, take identity from og:url, and treat a 2.9KB response on a post URL as a redirect to follow, not a thin page to score.

Identifier & dedup

The key is (blogId, logNo), and logNo is a platform-global sequence — measured in the Probe Log — so it is unique on its own and orderable by creation time across the whole corpus. Three live URL forms per post (desktop canonical, m., PostView.naver) plus the retired {blogId}.blog.me alias; the reaction API's {blogId}_{logNo} is the same key underscore-joined.

Retrieval unit

Not the page. SmartEditor emits typed, UUID-bearing components — se-text, se-image, se-table, se-quotation — so the document arrives pre-segmented into passages with stable IDs. Index the component as the passage and the post as the parent; the usual heuristic paragraph splitting is wasted work here.

Cross-modal joins

Every image carries its fileSize, source dimensions and an "ai" generation flag in data-module-v2, sharing a component UUID with its position in the text — a platform labelling AI-generated media inline. Store the flag: it is the cheapest synthetic-content signal in this corpus, and it is lost if you extract images by URL scrape rather than by component.

Licensing is per blog, not per post

The badge was identical across all 10 posts of each sampled blog and differed between them: a blog-level setting stamped into every footer. Resolve it once per blogId and re-check slowly — but a change is retroactive across the whole back catalogue, the opposite of how per-post licences behave.

Freshness & recrawl

There is no sitemap and no platform-wide feed; the only incremental path is the per-blog RSS, capped at 50 items with no pagination. For a high-volume blog that is a window of days. Recrawl therefore scales with the number of blogs tracked, not with the corpus — and backfill has no feed path at all.

Discovery is the binding constraint

Post pages are open to any non-AI agent, but every surface that lists them is not: section.blog. is Disallow: / for *, the directory and popular-post endpoints are disallowed on mobile, and there is no sitemap. You may read any post you can name and may not use the site to learn its name. The global logNo sequence is the only structural way across that gap, and walking it would be exactly the bulk access the policy exists to prevent.

Quality & spam

The spam gradient runs through commerce: sponsored 체험단 review posts are written to rank in Naver Search for product and local queries, and Naver Mate now adds a second incentive to write whatever AI Briefing cites. Both pressures land in the same review categories that make the corpus valuable, so trust scoring cannot be separated from topic here.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the SmartEditor component as passage, the post as parent document, with the blog-level CCL and the per-image ai flag attached at ingest.
  • Primary keys & joins: logNo alone is globally unique and time-orderable; (blogId, logNo) for readability; {blogId}_{logNo} joins to reactions; component UUID joins passages to their images; blogId joins to licence, author and RSS feed.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A negotiated agreement — the only path licensed for an AI index, and the one Naver has an apparatus for, since it already pays creators by AI-citation count. 2) The Open API for discovery within its ceiling: 25K calls/day, 1,000 results per query, snippet-only bodies, and result links the published example returns as opaque openapi.naver.com/l? redirects. 3) Per-blog RSS for incremental updates on a curated blog list, 50 items deep. 4) The reaction API for ranking signal, free and batchable. There is no bulk path and no sitemap: the corpus is not reachable in full by any published mechanism.
  • Recrawl cadence: RSS poll per tracked blog on an activity-weighted schedule, since the 50-item window is the real constraint; reaction counts weekly for the first month then quarterly; blog-level licence monthly, because a change rewrites the rights on the entire back catalogue; post bodies once, then edit checks only — posts mutate in place with no revision marker.
  • Derivatives to generate: none for images — Naver already serves every size through ?type=, with dimensions and byte sizes in the markup. Spend the budget on Korean sponsored-post classification, on carrying the ai flag into the index as a first-class field, and on an entity resolver for the place and product names that make the review corpus worth retrieving.
  • Demo to build: a logNo clock. Fit the ID-to-timestamp line from a handful of RSS feeds, then expose date → expected ID range and ID → estimated creation date. It dates any post from its URL with no fetch, prices the corpus for any window, and shows how much a public sequence leaks from 100 samples.

Risks to the Index

  • The AI block is explicit, enforced, and names Anthropic twice. ClaudeBot and Claude-SearchBot are both at Disallow: / under a header calling AI training and RAG "strictly prohibited", and the Probe Log shows a real AI search crawler refusing the domain. There is no ambiguity to read around and no opt-in programme to join.
  • Naver trains on this corpus and pays its authors to keep feeding it. Blog text was 48.7% of HyperCLOVA's pretraining tokens on a terms-of-service basis, and Naver Mate now pays 3,000 creators a month by AI Briefing citation count. An outside index competes with a first-party pipeline holding both the data and the creators — a commercial negotiation, not a crawl problem.
  • inferred The public HTML is not Naver's own ingestion path, so it can harden at no cost — and discovery is already hardening first. Basis: the Yeti block and the closed discovery surfaces, both set out once under Inferences below.
  • The frameset is a silent failure mode. A crawler that takes the canonical URL at face value gets HTTP 200, valid HTML, a plausible title and no content — no error, no redirect, no signal. The index looks healthy and is empty.
  • Licence state is mutable and retroactive. One author's CCL toggle silently rewrites the rights on their entire archive, so an index that resolves licence at crawl time and never re-checks drifts out of compliance without any content changing.

Inferences

  • Naver Search ingests blog content from the database rather than by crawling it, so the public HTML is a third-party surface it can restrict cheaply. Basis: Yeti at Disallow: / on blog. and m.blog. while blog posts rank throughout Naver Search, plus the measured absence of UA gating — identical bytes to curl, Googlebot and Chrome.
  • Discovery closes before content does. Basis: section.blog. at Disallow: / for *, the mobile directory and popular-post endpoints disallowed, and no sitemap on any blog host.
  • CCL is a blog-level setting, not a per-post one. Basis: all 10 sampled naverofficial posts carried by-nc-nd 2.0 kr and all 10 naver_search posts carried by-sa 2.0 kr, spanning ten months of publication in each case.
  • The gap between 408M IDs issued and 330M posts published is drafts, private posts and deletions. Basis: the sequence advances on creation while the published figure counts surviving public posts, and the 81% ratio holds across the fitted 301-day span.

Open Questions

  • Cumulative post count and number of blogs ever created — Naver publishes annual flow, never stock; closeable by an Open API total reading on an unrestricted query.
  • Whether logNo increments exactly once per post creation — closeable by publishing two posts from a controlled blog a known interval apart and differencing the assigned IDs against the fitted rate.
  • Platform-wide CCL coverage — the 20/20 rate comes from two corporate Naver blogs and is not representative; closeable by sampling several hundred blogs found through the Open API and counting footer badges.
  • What share of images carries "ai": "true" — all 102 in the sample were false; closeable by extending the same markup sample across blogs in AI-heavy categories.
  • Whether the Open API link field still returns the opaque openapi.naver.com/l? redirect shown in the docs — this decides whether discovery needs a resolve step per result; closeable with one authenticated call.
  • Whether Naver licenses the blog corpus to third-party AI developers at any price, and through which channel — no public programme or contact path was found; closeable only by approaching Naver's partnership channel.
  • Video and Moment object counts and licence status — no se-video appeared in the 20-post sample; closeable by sampling video-heavy categories and reading the component metadata.
  • Hosting and CDN posture — DNS here returned unrelated address space for every blog host through two resolvers; closeable by resolving from an unfiltered network, ideally in Korea.
