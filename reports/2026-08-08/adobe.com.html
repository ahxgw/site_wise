adobe.com

Context

Adobe sells subscription creative, document, and marketing software; adobe.com is its storefront, but the domain's indexable mass sits on subdomains that are not the storefront. Four distinct corpora share the registrable domain: www (product marketing and Express/Learn SEO pages), helpx (product documentation), community (user Q&A forums), and stock (a licensed media marketplace). Each runs its own stack, its own sitemap scheme, and — the finding that matters most here — its own crawl policy. The commercially licensed corpus is the one walled against AI crawlers; the free support and forum corpora are wide open.

For a full-modal index it is therefore not one acquisition decision but four, and the valuable part is the free one: the reference corpus of error messages, version histories, and troubleshooting threads that users reach through search rather than Adobe's navigation.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where adobe.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Crawl policy splits by commercial value, not by host convention

The methodology's default is that large platforms block AI crawlers broadly. Adobe inverts the granularity: five of six subdomains carry no AI directive at all, and the single blocked host is the one selling per-asset licenses (Probe P7). The policy tracks who gets paid per object, not the corporate boundary.

The curated SEO sliver is measurable here

Stock publishes 0.054% of its catalog as crawlable asset pages and pads the sitemap with ~1.15M query-shaped pages instead (Probe P10). The sitemap is a keyword-landing surface, not a catalog export — treat its URL count as a ranking artifact, not a corpus estimate.

Locale duplication is the dominant dedup cost on www

Measured 3.1× across 12 of 91 locales (Probe P5). Canonicalize on the locale-stripped path plus rel=canonical, and read hreflang from the sitemap's xhtml:link blocks — those are absent from the page HTML (Probe P6).

Authoritative field: community answers outrank marketing copy

For the queries this domain actually wins — error codes, version conflicts, install failures — the accepted forum answer and the helpx troubleshooting article carry the signal. www product pages are conversion copy and should be down-weighted for informational intents.

Root path is a bot wall; everything below it is not

A crawler that samples / to test reachability gets a 7-byte 2019 stub and may conclude the site is dead (Probe P2). Health checks must target a content path (Probe P3).

llms.txt exists but is an editorial artifact

46 hand-picked marketing URLs with typos (Probe P6) — a curated funnel, not a corpus manifest. It is useful as a product-taxonomy seed and worthless as a crawl frontier.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: per corpus — helpx article section; community thread (accepted answer boosted); www page, locale-collapsed; stock asset (metadata only, no pixels).
  • Primary keys & joins: locale-stripped canonical path as the www key; thread slug + numeric board ID for community; helpx article path. Join all three to the Adobe product entity by parsing the product token from the path (/products/photoshop, /questions-15/, helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/) — this is the one key that spans the domain.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) community sitemaps — 1.48M threads, no auth, highest answer density per byte; 2) helpx sitemaps — 749K docs, no auth, precise lastmod for cheap incremental recrawl; 3) www sitemaps — 213K, no auth, but dedup first or pay 3×+ for duplicates; 4) Adobe Stock API under a key, metadata only, if stock coverage is required at all; 5) nothing else — Firefly and PDF Services are generation APIs, not corpora.
  • Recrawl cadence: helpx by lastmod delta (it is millisecond-precise, so trust it); community weekly for threads under ~180 days old and quarterly after that, since answers accrue early; www monthly; stock not crawled.
  • Derivatives to generate: error-code and version-string extraction across helpx + community — this is what makes the corpus retrievable for the queries it actually wins. Question/answer pair extraction from threads. Skip OCR and ASR: there is no meaningful crawlable media on the open hosts, and the host with media forbids it.
  • Demo to build: a locale-collapse harness over the 91-child creativecloud tree that reports the true dedup factor for the full tree, extending Probe P5's 12-locale sample to a complete measure.

Risks to the Index

  • The open hosts are one commit away from closing. Adobe already runs an AI-crawler blocklist on stock (Probe P7); extending those six stanzas to helpx and community is a copy-paste. The 2.2M-URL free corpus is unprotected by policy, not by decision.
  • Semrush ownership changes Adobe's incentives around crawlers. Adobe closed a $1.9B acquisition of a search-visibility company on 2026-04-28 and describes the rationale in terms of generative-engine and agentic-search optimization. A vendor selling AI-search visibility has reasons to meter what AI crawlers take from its own domain inferred Basis: the acquisition's stated rationale (Verified Metrics) sits directly against the currently unrestricted crawl policy on www, helpx, and community measured in Probe P7.
  • Community platform migrations reset URLs wholesale. The robots.txt still contains an unreplaced community.company.com vendor placeholder (Probe P7) — evidence the forum runs on third-party software whose configuration is not fully owned. A platform change would invalidate 1.48M slugs at once.
  • Edge policy can diverge from robots policy without notice. Stock already 403s a robots-permitted path (Probe P11). Any host can acquire the same behavior silently, so ingestion health must be measured, not inferred from robots.txt.

Inferences

  • Adobe's crawl policy is set per revenue model rather than per host. Basis: of six subdomains probed, the only one carrying AI-crawler blocks is the one that licenses individual assets for money; the free documentation and forum corpora, which are far larger by URL count, carry none (Probe P7).
  • The www robots.txt is maintained by accretion, not review. Basis: it still allowlists gsa-crawler-www and Atomz/1.0 — crawlers for products discontinued in 2019 and acquired in 2009 — and disallows named PDF campaign files from 2020 and 2021, while containing no rule for any crawler launched since 2022 (Probe P1).
  • Stock's sitemap is built for keyword landing pages rather than catalog discovery. Basis: 1.15M of its 1.64M URLs are most-searched-terms and most-licensed-video-terms files, against 490K asset pages covering 0.054% of the stated catalog (Probes P10, P11).

Open Questions

  • True deduplicated URL count for www.adobe.com — Probe P5 measured 3.1× duplication across only 12 of 91 locales in one tree. Closeable by walking all 91 children of each index and collapsing on locale-stripped path plus rel=canonical.
  • Modality split inside the 900M-asset Stock catalog (images vs. video vs. audio vs. 3D vs. templates) — closeable with an authenticated Stock API Search/Files call filtered by content_type, reading nb_results per facet.
  • Licensing terms for reuse of helpx and community text beyond search snippets — robots.txt permits crawling but grants nothing explicitly. Closeable by reading Adobe's Terms of Use and community guidelines against a specific reuse case, or by asking Adobe directly.
  • Adobe Stock ingest rate (assets added per day) — needed to set a recrawl cadence if stock metadata is ever indexed. Closeable by sampling asset-ID ranges over time via the authenticated API.
  • Whether Firefly's training corpus includes the Stock catalog and under what contributor consent — this decides whether the AI-crawler block on stock is a rights constraint or a commercial one, and backs the provenance claim in the Access Matrix. Closeable from the current contributor agreement plus Adobe's Firefly training-data disclosures.
