Context

icc-cricket.com is the official site of the International Cricket Council, the Dubai-based governing body of international cricket. It covers news, video, photos, rankings and stats for ICC events such as the World Cups, the Champions Trophy and the World Test Championship. The site is a promotional outlet. ICC money comes from selling media rights and sponsorship for its events, then passing the surplus to member boards. For an index builder, the site is a public window onto a Deltatre FORGE CMS. The Next.js front end reads a keyless distribution API (dapi.icc-cricket.com) that lists every story since 2008, every photo and every video record, with no depth cap. Stats and scorecards come from Sportz Interactive. The sitemaps expose only a 100-item window per tournament, while the terms forbid commercial use of site content without a licence.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where icc-cricket.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

The API is the catalog; the sitemap is a window

Every sitemap child stops at 100 URLs, so the sitemaps show only the latest slice of each tournament. The FORGE list endpoints have no depth cap. About 3.4K list requests (calculated by Site Wise: 434K objects ÷ 100 per page) enumerate the whole corpus, against ~2.8K URLs from the sitemaps. This is the same FORGE pattern as nhl.com earlier in the series.

Video dates are migration stamps

About 32.8K archived videos carry contentDate values from October–November 2023. That is the migration date, not the match date: one sampled slug covers the 2014 World Cup Qualifier and is stamped 2023-11-15. Date these videos from their match-{id}/tournament-{id} tags or from the title, and never trust contentDate inside that block. Stories kept their original dates through the same migration.

Identifiers & joins

FORGE gives two UUIDs: _entityId (stable across translations) and _translationId. Videos add a videoId UUID that appears in HLS paths. Cricket tags (player-N, match-N, tournament-N) use Sportz/ICC numeric IDs, so text, video and stats join on the same keys without entity resolution.

Machine-generated video dominates

69% of new videos are DIVA auto-clips with slugs like {player}-wicket-{team}-vs-{team}. They are short and near-duplicate, and they cluster under one match tag. Collapse them per match and ball event for the ranked index, and keep editor-made videos (named createdBy) as first-class objects.

Exposed media URLs are not a grant

The sitemaps publish HLS content_loc URLs with century-long, wildcard-path Akamai tokens, and recent API records expose full-quality mezzanine MP4s with no auth. These are delivery details meant for Google's video indexing and for the site's own player. Use the watch page as the display target and metadata as the index object; do not mirror media.

robots versus sitemap conflict

robots.txt disallows /media-zone, but sitemap.xml lists 28 /media-zone URLs. Honour robots. The 8 disallowed integrity articles remain reachable through the API, so check each API slug against robots before indexing.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the story (markdown parts, with an inline video entity), the video (title, description, duration, match/player tags, watch URL), the photo (caption and credit) and the cricket entity tag. Matches are the hub objects.
  • Primary keys & joins: FORGE _entityId for each content object; player-N, match-N and tournament-N for cricket joins; GettyImages-{id} to link photos to Getty's own catalog.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A content licence from the ICC for any commercial index (terms). 2) A one-time FORGE walk of all types at ≤1 req/s (~1 hour), honouring robots.txt per slug. 3) Daily incremental pulls of the first pages of each list, ordered by contentDate. 4) Sitemaps only as a cross-check of what Google sees.
  • Recrawl cadence: every 15 minutes during ICC events (stories and DIVA clips arrive per match), daily otherwise, plus a monthly full walk with lastUpdatedDate to catch edits and deletions.
  • Derivatives to generate: a per-match bundle of story, auto-clips, photos and scorecard link; corrected dates for migrated videos; and per-player timelines from player-N tags.
  • Demo to build: "every wicket of a player". Query FORGE videos by player-N tag, order by match, and show clip titles with watch-page embeds. It runs from API metadata alone.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The open API is incidental, not a product. Basis: dapi has no published docs, terms or key, and its CORS * and private, max-age=120 caching match a front-end backend. The nhl.com FORGE deployment showed the same shape. A gateway change (the config already names content-gateway.icc-prd.deltatre.digital) could close it without notice, so keep a local mirror of the metadata.
  • inferred Media URLs will rotate. Basis: mezzanine hosting moved from SAS-signed Azure URLs (Jan 2026 records) to media.icc-cricket.com (recent records), and the archive has none. Store videoId and the watch URL, not media links.

Open Questions

  • Scorecard, schedule and records feed: fields, depth of history and terms. This can be closed by asking Sportz Interactive or the ICC for feed terms, or by recording which /cricket/v1/* calls a rankings page makes in a browser's network panel.
  • Video-archive playability: whether pre-2024 videos still stream. This can be closed by opening 20 random watch pages from $skip ≥ 10,026 in a browser and noting playback or error states.
  • API rate limits. This can be closed by a paced ramp test (2, 5 and 10 req/s for one minute each) watching for 429s, done only with ICC consent given the terms.
  • Site traffic and audience split by country. This can be closed from ICC annual-report digital metrics (the 2025-26 report PDF on the annual report page) or a named panel estimate.
