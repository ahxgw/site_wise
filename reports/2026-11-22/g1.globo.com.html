Context

g1 is the digital news arm of Grupo Globo, Brazil's dominant broadcaster. It launched on 2006-09-18 and is fed by TV Globo's national newscasts (Jornal Nacional, Fantástico), GloboNews, and about 27 state editions run with Globo's regional affiliates. Those editions carry most of the output: in one day's sitemap, São Paulo, Minas Gerais and Rio state sections outnumber politics 17 to 1. It is free and ad-funded; the money sits with the parent, whose revenue is mostly advertising. For an index builder, g1 is unusually open. robots.txt names no AI crawler. Sitemaps run day by day back to 2003, with inline images, and video sitemaps carry title, duration and an MP4 path for every clip. The section feeds behind the homepage are a public JSON API. The difficulty is size and platform history: a modern page weighs about 1.2MB of HTML for ~500 words, pages before 2017 are a frozen legacy platform with almost no structured data, and actual video bytes need a signed playback token.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Fetch the AMP twin, not the page

Default assumption: the canonical page is the cheapest complete copy. On g1, a 500-word article ships about 1.2MB of HTML, mostly ~560KB of inline scripts, ~220KB of inline CSS and recirculation cards (44–51 image URLs per page, almost all from other stories). The /google/amp/ twin is linked from every modern page, holds the full text at a twelfth of the size, and carries the content UUID on its root tag. Crawl the AMP twin for text. Fetch the canonical page only when you need the wordCount and comment flags.

Two platforms, one sitemap

Before 2017, articles are static .html snapshots last rebuilt in June 2022, with no canonical link, no content UUID and no schema.org markup; only OG tags and a byline date. From 2017 they are .ghtml with a UUID and NewsArticle microdata. Use the URL path as the key for the legacy half and the UUID for the modern half. Do not expect the legacy half to change, and recrawl it rarely.

Video sitemaps file by air date, not upload date

The clip in the 2026/12/01 file was created 2026-10-30 with exhibited_at 2026-12-01. A Fantástico clip uploaded 2007-12-28 sits in 1978/01/02. An incremental crawler that reads only today's file misses back-dated and forward-dated clips. Diff the index for new or changed child files, and take dates from the playlist API.

Most "articles" are regional TV

State sections dominate the daily sitemap, and many clips come from affiliate newscasts (TV Morena, Inter TV), each with its own program_id and channel_id. Daily "VÍDEOS: newscast de date" pages are rundowns of clips, not articles. They lack NewsArticle markup (4 of 12 modern samples), so classify them by title pattern and index them as playlists linking clip IDs.

The feed API is the change log

The Falkor feeds give post type, publication and modification times, and kickers for ~10K posts on the homepage instance alone, with no key and no page cap short of 1,005. They are the cheapest way to detect edits (modified > publication) without refetching 1MB pages. Each section page embeds its own instance UUID.

Portuguese, Brazilian, entity-dense

All content is pt-BR (the playlist languages field, og:locale). The value is local: municipality names, state politics and police blotter. A Brazilian gazetteer (IBGE municipality codes) is the right join key. The URL's state prefix (/sp/, /am/) plus regional slug already gives location for most items.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, keyed by content UUID (modern) or URL path (legacy). Each is split into paragraphs, with the lead image and any embedded clip as linked children. The video clip is a separate unit keyed by numeric video ID, with program, affiliate, air date and duration.
  • Primary keys & joins: content UUID ↔ Falkor post ↔ AMP data-bs-resource-id; video ID ↔ playlist ↔ thumbnail path ↔ clip page slug suffix (-15266065.ghtml); program_id/channel_id → newscast and affiliate entities; URL state prefix → IBGE region.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The three sitemap indexes (20.5K files, at most ~3GB of XML: full parts measured 110KB for articles and 175KB for videos, calculated by Site Wise), then AMP twins for text. At 1 req/s, 5.1M articles take ~59 days from one IP (calculated by Site Wise), so start from 2017+ and do legacy last. 2) Video playlist API for clip metadata, paced. 3) RSS and Falkor feeds every few minutes for new items and edits.
  • Recrawl cadence: every 5–15 minutes on RSS and Falkor; daily diff of sitemap indexes; refetch an article when Falkor modified moves; legacy .html once.
  • Derivatives: entity and place extraction against IBGE codes; clip-to-article links from embedded video IDs; per-program clip timelines; poster-frame embeddings. Transcripts would need licensed ASR, so they stay out of scope until captions surface.
  • Demo to build: a "what happened in my town" search: pick a municipality and get articles, TV clips (with poster frames) and election results, joined on the IBGE code and ordered by air date.

Risks to the Index

  • Video bytes are rights-managed. Every rendition URL needs a player-issued hash and key (Probe Log). Treat clips as metadata, poster frames and embeds; any frame-level or speech index needs a licence from Globo.
  • inferred AMP twins may be retired. Basis: they live under /google/amp/, a path built for Google's AMP carousel, and Google stopped requiring AMP for Top Stories in 2021. Keep the canonical parser working as a fallback.
  • inferred The open feed API is unadvertised. Basis: Falkor is reached only through instance UUIDs embedded in page JSON, with no docs or terms page found. It could gain a key without notice, so do not build the primary census on it.
  • Sitemap counts are wide estimates. Last-part sizes vary from 1 to 1,000 within the same year (Probe Log). A full count of all 20.5K files is the only exact number, and should precede capacity planning.
