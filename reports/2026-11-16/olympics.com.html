Context

olympics.com is the International Olympic Committee's single digital property. www.olympic.org now 301s to it (Probe Log), and its video still streams from olympicchannel.com hosts. It carries year-round sports news, an athlete database covering historic and current Games, per-Games results pages, and an on-demand video library whose rights vary by country. Money is indirect: the site promotes broadcast-rights holders and TOP sponsors, which together fund the IOC. For an index, the important facts are three. The HTML is blocked to a declared research crawler. The sitemaps are open and enumerate every athlete in 12 languages. The front-end is built on Deltatre's FORGE CMS, whose distribution API is keyless — the same pattern as nhl.com and icc-cricket.com. Video is the hard modality: per-video country allow-lists, a registration wall on full replays, and tokenised HLS.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where olympics.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Open sitemaps, closed pages, open API

robots.txt invites every crawler and lists 2.8M URLs, but the Akamai edge refuses the HTML to a declared research UA. The backend the Next.js front-end reads is anonymous and CORS-open. A legitimate full-text path is therefore the API (or a crawl from an allowed network), not the pages. This is the third FORGE site in this series. Like nhl.com and icc-cricket.com, the API has an uncapped $skip, so exact totals cost about 25 requests per type.

Identifiers & joins

Content key: the FORGE _entityId UUID. It is shared across languages; _translationId differs per locale. Public URLs use the language-specific slug, which is also the API detail key. Athletes have a slug plus a FORGE entityID; tags with customentity.* sources link stories to NOCs (noc-es), disciplines and events. Video key: wonId (WO_…) plus the FORGE videoID. Pages have no hreflang, so cross-language joins must go through _entityId.

Dates by provenance

contentDate is the original publication date for imported archive stories (1928 for one olympic-importer story) but the migration date for some bp-forge-converter items. Athlete sitemap lastmods mostly record a 2025-03 bulk re-save. Use createdBy to choose which date field to trust, and treat lastUpdatedDate as edit time only.

Per-country availability is metadata

Videos carry a video:restriction allow-list in the sitemap and a countries array in page JSON. Stories carry a Countries field in the API. An index serving many regions should store these lists and filter at query time rather than assume global availability; 27% of 2024 videos were not viewable from the US.

Importer-heavy photo corpus

inferred Most of the 461K photos are pipeline imports, not editorial selections. Basis: in a 40-item random sample, createdBy values were importer or client accounts (MPX Importer, FST FST, bp-data-hub-client, olympic-importer, ioc-org-importer-client) for at least 18 of 40, and a separate single-item probe returned an EPG Importer TV-guide card. Weight photos attached to a story or album above loose ones.

Three sitemap generations in robots.txt

The live tree is /sitemap.xml → /{lang}/xml-sitemap/. The 12 /lang/ indexes still point to 302ing dynamicsitemap children with 2021 lastmods. A crawler that trusts every Sitemap: line wastes requests and may conclude the corpus is stale.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: a story (FORGE entity, one record per locale), an athlete (slug plus entityID, with medals and results), a results table row (Games, event, phase, athlete or NOC), a photo, and a video metadata card (title, description, duration, country list, thumbnail).
  • Primary keys & joins: _entityId across languages; tag slugs typed by externalSourceName for NOC, discipline and event facets; athlete slug ↔ story tags; wonId for video; img.olympics.com public ID for image dedupe.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) FORGE v2 list endpoints for stories, photos, albums, documents and tags in every locale (about 1 req/s; $limit 100). 2) The sitemap tree for URL coverage and athlete and results inventories. 3) Athlete and results pages, through an access arrangement with the IOC or archived captures, since HTML is 403. 4) Video metadata from the sitemap, through Wayback for history; no media fetch.
  • Recrawl cadence: news sitemap every 15 minutes during Games and hourly otherwise; FORGE lists sorted by update daily; athlete sitemaps weekly, with a diff after each event; sdw results once after each Games.
  • Derivatives to generate: an athlete knowledge card (medals, Games, NOC, discipline) joined to every story tagged with that athlete; a per-country availability facet for video; a locale-collapsed story cluster via _entityId.
  • Demo to build: an "athlete timeline": type a name, get medals and results by Games plus the stories and photos tagged with that athlete, in the viewer's language, with video replays flagged by country availability.

Risks to the Index

  • HTML access. Athlete and results pages, about 91% of sitemap URLs (calculated by Site Wise), are reachable only as HTML, and the edge 403s it. Without an IOC arrangement, coverage depends on archive captures.
  • Video rights. Footage is licensed to broadcast-rights holders by territory, and replays sit behind registration. An index can hold metadata and link out, but cannot embed or transcribe media.
  • Burst load. 325M users during Paris 2024 (Verified Metrics) means the news and results corpus changes fastest exactly when the edge is most protective. Plan Games-time ingestion through the API and the news sitemap, not the pages.
