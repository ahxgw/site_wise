Context

Yahoo!ファイナンス launched 1997-03-04 as Yahoo! JAPAN's stock-quote content and is now run by LY Corporation, the October 2023 merger of Yahoo Japan and LINE. It sells display advertising against the pages and a VIP倶楽部 subscription against the data the free pages withhold. What makes it worth a report is that it is an aggregator, not a publisher: the site's own footer names eight upstream suppliers — 東京証券取引所, 大阪取引所, 名古屋証券取引所, 野村総合研究所, 東洋経済新報社, ウエルスアドバイザー, リフィニティブ・ジャパン, LINE FX — and then states 「株式情報の転用、販売は固く禁じます」. So the crawl economics invert the usual arrangement. The market data is technically wide open (no AI-crawler block, no user-agent gate, full server-side rendering, an unadvertised sitemap index) and legally closed. The one corpus LY actually owns is the stock message board, which absorbed the 株式 and FX categories of Yahoo!掲示板 when that service closed in 2019 and now takes ~29M posts a year. For an index builder the asset here is the forum and the AI-derived commentary layered on top of it, not the prices.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site departs from the default platform assumptions.

The AI-crawler policy is per-subdomain and points the wrong way

The methodology's default is that big platforms block AI crawlers. Here the portal blocks Google-Extended and GPTBot in 87 bytes while the finance property, holding the far more valuable corpus, names no AI crawler at all and falls through to a four-line permissive group (Probe Log). Nothing in the enforcement layer compensates: four wildly different user-agents got the same 470,988 bytes.

One track, and it is a legal wall

The default assumes a curated SEO sliver plus a metered API. This site has neither — no allowlist, and no finance API on Yahoo!デベロッパーネットワーク — so anonymous HTML is the only track and it is the complete one. The constraint is instead contractual and upstream: LY licenses its prices and fundamentals from eight named vendors and passes through a blanket reuse ban. A crawler faces no obstacle and an index faces no permission, which is the usual ordering reversed.

URL count is not object count

181,459 sitemap URLs are 24,226 instruments times a per-tab fan-out of 7.5. Treating URLs as documents inflates the corpus 7.5× and shreds one record across a dozen pages. The fan-out is also uneven — 12.29 for JP equities, 6.0 for US, 2.13 for FX — so a naive per-URL budget over-samples Japan.

Identifiers are three different schemes

JP listings use {code}.{market} (7203.T), joining cleanly to TSE and EDINET on the four-digit code. Funds use the 8-character 協会コード (01311002), a real external key. US tickers are bare and exchange-unqualified, and 174 of them carry a leading space encoded as %20 — /quote/%20GMGT — which will silently fork into duplicate documents unless normalised at ingest.

Paywalled tables ship fabricated numbers

The trade-breakdown tab renders 1,234,567,890 across six rows dated 1234/56/78 to anonymous clients (Probe Log). These are well-formed, parse cleanly, and are entirely fake. Any numeric extractor that does not detect the VIP upsell adjacent to them will ingest garbage — this site's crawler trap is a paywall teaser, not a bot gate.

Freshness is per-field, not per-page

One quote page mixes a real-time TSE print, a ≥15-minute-delayed volume figure, an overnight-PTS block from a different venue, margin balances from the prior Friday, and AI commentary from the prior session's close. Each field carries its own timestamp in the markup. A page-level lastmod would be meaningless, which is presumably why the sitemaps omit it entirely.

Free depth is a hard cliff, not a taper

Price history stops dead at 243 rows — one trading year — and page 14 returns a shell, not a 404. Backfilling beyond one year is impossible at any crawl rate; it is a subscription purchase or nothing.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the instrument — one record per (market, symbol) assembled from all of its tabs, with the field-level timestamps preserved rather than flattened to a page date. The board post is a second, independent unit; it is the only unit here that is a document in the ordinary sense.
  • Primary keys & joins: key JP equities on the four-digit code with {code}.T as an alias — that code joins outward to TDnet, EDINET and the rehosted PDFs without a crosswalk. Key funds on the 協会コード. Key US tickers on (ticker, exchange) resolved from the profile tab, never on the bare URL segment, and strip leading %20 before hashing. Join posts to instruments on the board URL, and posts to each other on the per-board number, which the >>1611921 reply convention references directly.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) /quote/{sym}/forum — the only modality both freely crawlable and plausibly LY's to license, at 72 posts per page over a corpus adding ~2.45M posts monthly. 2) /disclosure plus the rehosted PDFs, a document corpus whose provenance is issuer-side and citable. 3) /ai-topics, valuable less for the prose than for its source lists, which are a ready-made per-ticker event-to-evidence graph. 4) Quote and profile tabs for entity attributes, ingested but flagged no-display pending a licence review. 5) Price series last — one year deep, expensive per page, least reusable.
  • Recrawl cadence: boards for the ~100 tickers on the 掲示板投稿数 ranking every few minutes during the 09:00–15:30 JST session and hourly otherwise; the long tail weekly. /disclosure daily at 15:30 and 19:00 JST, when TDnet batches land. AI commentary daily after the close. Profiles and 優待 monthly. Instrument discovery weekly from /sitemaps.xml, the only signal that a ticker was added or dropped since it carries no lastmod.
  • Derivatives to generate: a symbol normaliser across the three ID schemes, before anything else. Then a placeholder detector — numbers in the 1,234,567,890 / 1234/56/78 family, or a value grid of zeros contradicting a non-zero summary above it, dropped rather than stored. Then per-post sentiment and entity extraction over the board, which is where cross-modal value lies: linking a post to the filing or news item it reacts to, using the /ai-topics source lists as weak supervision.
  • Demo to build: the placeholder trap. Fetch /quote/7203.T/breakdown anonymously, run a naive table extractor over it, and print the six rows of 1,234,567,890 it happily returns. Two minutes, and it makes a point most crawl-trap demos do not: the defence that costs an index most is not the one designed to stop crawlers.

Risks to the Index

  • The board is now a regulated surface. On 2025-04-30 LY Corporation was designated under Japan's revised platform law (情報流通プラットフォーム対処法) for four services including the finance board, obliging faster removals and published operating disclosures. Regulatory pressure on a UGC corpus reliably shortens retention and widens deletion — the single largest threat to the one asset here worth indexing.
  • Delisted boards vanish by policy. Both transparency reports state that boards for delisted issuers move to 上場廃止・償還済み and may be deleted after a period without notice. An index of Japanese retail sentiment loses exactly the cases most interesting in hindsight — failures — unless it archives eagerly and never re-derives from live pages.
  • The reuse ban is upstream and unilateral. LY cannot grant rights it licensed from TSE, NRI, Toyo Keizai, Wealth Advisor and Refinitiv. Any vendor renegotiation changes what may be displayed without a single robots.txt line moving, and gives no crawl-visible signal that it happened. The AI layer is fenced separately and harder: the earnings-summary notice assigns rights in the generated text to LY and forbids reuse, redistribution and derivative processing outright.
  • The paywall is advancing. The VIP announcements date the trade-breakdown series to May and June 2026 and the portfolio expansion to August 2026, against a Media segment growing 2.6% with display and search advertising both down. Free surfaces are being converted while the segment that funds them stalls.
  • inferred The permissive finance robots.txt is an oversight rather than a decision, and is likely to be brought into line with the portal. Basis: www.yahoo.co.jp carries an explicit Google-Extended and GPTBot block while finance.yahoo.co.jp, holding the ~29M-posts-per-year board, names no AI crawler and omits even a Sitemap: line for the sitemap index it actually publishes at a non-default path (Probe Log, 2026-08-27) — the finance file has not been maintained alongside the portal's.
  • inferred Post numbers are per-board sequential counters, so board depth can be read without crawling it — and that shortcut breaks the moment numbering changes. Basis: page one of each of five boards showed 72 posts in a contiguous descending run (7203.T spanned 1,611,853–1,611,926, a span of 73 for 72 posts), with five distinct magnitudes across the five boards, and LY's transparency reports state each ticker board is a single non-user-creatable thread (Probe Log, 2026-08-27).

Open Questions

  • Yahoo!ファイナンス's current audience size. The last public figure is 18M monthly unique browsers claimed in 2018 and the LY media page now publishes only demographics; closeable only by a Nielsen or Similarweb panel pull with the property broken out.
  • Total cumulative posts across all 4,822 JP boards. Reading the top post number on every board settles it in one sweep, but the five sampled boards are all mega-caps and cannot be extrapolated.
  • How far back the boards actually retain posts, given that deletion is by policy rather than by age; closeable by walking one long-lived board's pagination to its floor and comparing the oldest surviving post number against 1.
  • Whether VIP's CSV export carries a redistribution licence distinct from the site's blanket reuse ban — the promotion page describes the feature and not its terms; needs the subscription agreement.
  • Whether the board accepts user-uploaded images. The FY2024 report describes generative-AI auto-deletion of obscene and violent images here, but all 45 distinct image sources on the sampled board page were site chrome or the ten default avatars.
  • Which supplier backs which asset class — the footer names all eight collectively, so licence terms on the FX and index pages cannot be separated from the equity ones without the vendor agreements.
