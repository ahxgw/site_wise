Context

Cricinfo is the sport's system of record — scorecards, careers and match reports reaching back to the 18th century, assembled since 1993 and never seriously rivalled. It matters to an index builder less as a news site than as the only comprehensive structured store of cricket facts, and the joins it publishes (player ID, match ID, series ID) are the de-facto keys the rest of the cricket web borrows.

The site is caught mid-move. After the November 2024 Disney Star–Viacom18 merger put it inside JioStar, the platform was renamed back to plain Cricinfo on 23 June 2026 and pointed at cricinfo.com. Two months later that migration is visibly half-finished, and the resulting contradictions dominate this report's retrieval notes. inferred Discovery infrastructure was not part of the cutover. Basis: the news sitemap's newest entry is 2026-06-24, one day after the rename, and it has not advanced since (Probe Log) — the incremental feed stopped at the cutover rather than following the brand.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Cricinfo differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Numeric IDs are the site's great gift: every entity carries one in its slug (ted-a-beckett-3931, …-4th-match-65196, england-1), and the slug text can change while the ID does not. Key on the trailing integer, never the slug. The dedup hazard is host, not path: the identical object exists at espncricinfo.com and cricinfo.com, both returning 200 (Probe Log).

Canonical is contested

Three signals on one page disagree. rel=canonical and og:url say cricinfo.com; JSON-LD url says espncricinfo.com; both sitemap indexes list espncricinfo.com exclusively. Follow the rel=canonical — it is the signal the operator is actively changing — but store the ID, not the URL, as the identity, so the eventual redirect cutover costs nothing.

The sitemap is not an access grant

The sharpest delta here. The methodology's usual split is curated-sliver versus metered API; Cricinfo instead advertises 2.03M URLs in robots-permitted sitemaps and then has the CDN refuse 88.8% of them. Sitemap presence carries no information about fetchability, so a crawler must probe rather than trust — and should not read the 403s as a rate limit to back off from, because they are unconditional by path.

Robots.txt is a fossil, and structurally void

The 41 Disallow rules bind nothing (Probe Log), so the file's real content is Allow: /* plus three AI-crawler groups. Their targets — /db/SUPPORT/DAWSON/, /icc_cricket_worldcup2011/, a single 2013-14 series — describe a site two rewrites ago. A careful crawler should honour the orphaned rules anyway; they are a clear statement of intent even where they are not enforceable.

Retrieval unit

Four units: the story, the match, the player, the record query. Only the first two are fetchable. Never treat a URL as a unit — teams publish exactly 6.00 URLs per entity and matches 2.77, so URL-level indexing triples the corpus and splits each entity's evidence across near-duplicate tabs.

Authoritative content field

Trust rendered HTML over structured data, inverting the usual preference. articleBody holds the headline; og:type is website on every page type; a 1999 match carries a 2016 endDate (Probe Log). The JSON-LD is reliable for identity — author URL, image, datePublished — and unreliable for content and time.

Freshness & recrawl

Inverted from a normal news site: the archive is enormous and immutable, the fresh layer small and its feed dead. With lastmod unreliable and the news sitemap frozen at 2026-06-24, recency must come from crawling /story/ listing pages, not from any published signal.

Thin-content combinatorics

1.60M head-to-head URLs are team-pair × record-type × format, including matchups never played (Australia vs St Helena). Even if the 403 lifted, most would be empty tables. Treat the records family as a query space to sample, never a document set to enumerate.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: story, match, player, team — each keyed by its numeric ID, with the multi-tab URLs folded in as faces of one document rather than separate records.
  • Primary keys & joins: trailing integer from the slug for every entity type; author/{slug}-{id} joins bylines across stories; match ID joins scorecard, commentary and report tabs; img1.hscicdn.com path joins images to both stories and players. Store host-agnostic IDs so the rebrand cutover is a no-op.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) story.xml.gz → fetch each page, take body from rendered HTML — 32,381 documents, the only large family that is both permitted and reachable. 2) overall-match.xml.gz and overall-team.xml.gz, deduped to 17,917 and 6,596 entities. 3) News-sitemap image:loc for image-to-story joins, useful despite the staleness. 4) overall-videos.xml.gz for titles and URLs only — no media metadata exists to collect. 5) /cricketers/ and /records/: excluded, edge-refused. 6) StatsGuru results: excluded, robots-disallowed.
  • Coverage ceiling, stated plainly: roughly 227K of 2.03M declared URLs (11.2%) are actually ingestible, and the excluded remainder contains the structured cricket data that is the site's whole reason for existing. This corpus is worth indexing for its journalism, not for its statistics — the statistics require a licence.
  • Recrawl cadence: /story/ listing pages daily, since no working feed exists; match pages once after the fixture concludes, then never; team and player pages quarterly if access opens; archive stories never.
  • Derivatives to generate: entity linking from story text to player and team IDs — the single highest-value derivative, because it reconstructs part of the statistical graph from the prose that is reachable. Thumbnails via CDN transform rather than local resize. No ASR: no audio is obtainable.
  • Demo to build: a fetchability auditor that reads all 60 child sitemaps, samples each path family, and produces the declared-versus-reachable table in the Verified Metrics above — turning "the sitemap lies" from an assertion into a reproducible measurement, and re-runnable to detect the day the 403s lift or the hosts finally redirect.

Risks to the Index

  • The migration will move under you. Two hosts serve one corpus with contradictory canonical and sitemap signals, and traffic is visibly shifting between them (Verified Metrics). When the redirect finally lands, every stored espncricinfo.com URL breaks at once. Key on IDs and treat the host as a mutable attribute, not identity.
  • The bot gate is a scoring model, not a rule. Access today depends on presenting a browser-complete header set, and the passing threshold is undocumented and tunable from Akamai's console (Probe Log). This is the least durable assumption in the plan; a single policy change can take the reachable 11% to zero.
  • inferred The 403s on records and cricketers look deliberate rather than incidental. Basis: they are unconditional by path — 21 of 21 samples across two families and several URL shapes — while stories and videos on the same host, same headers, same session return 200. A rate limiter or a misconfiguration would not sort so cleanly along the boundary of the site's proprietary statistical data.
  • The AI-crawler stance is unfinished and will be finished. GPTBot and Google-Extended are named; ClaudeBot, CCBot, PerplexityBot, Bytespider and Amazonbot are not (Probe Log). That silence is an artefact of a file nobody has maintained, not a grant. Meanwhile the owner has announced AI plans of its own, which cuts against third-party reuse.
  • Statistical compilations may carry database rights independent of copyright. Cricinfo's scorecards are a compiled dataset assembled over three decades, and the operator is now an Indian JV rather than a US broadcaster — a different legal posture for any bulk-extraction argument. Do not build on the records family even if the 403 lifts, without checking this first.
  • Do not build freshness on the news sitemap. It has been frozen since 2026-06-24 (Verified Metrics). Any pipeline that treats it as a change feed will silently index nothing and report success.

Open Questions

  • Whether the /records/ and /cricketers/ 403s are global or specific to this vantage point's IP range — closeable by repeating the 21-URL sample from two unrelated networks and a residential connection.
  • Whether Googlebot is exempted from those 403s by verified reverse-DNS rather than UA — closeable by checking whether those URLs appear in Google's index via site: queries, which would prove the edge serves someone.
  • Whether espncricinfo.com will 301 to cricinfo.com, and when — closeable by scheduled re-probe of the header response on a known story URL.
  • Official audience figures — JioStar has published none since taking ownership; only third-party estimates exist. Closeable by a JioStar disclosure or an audited panel.
  • Whether the compiled scorecard database attracts sui generis database rights or contractual protection under Indian law post-JioStar, and whether any partner or paid statistics feed exists — no developer portal, pricing or terms were found. Needs a licensing conversation, not a probe.
  • Whether the Hindi edition's 12,310 URLs are translations or original reporting, and whether they share numeric IDs with the English corpus — closeable by sampling Hindi URLs for ID overlap.
