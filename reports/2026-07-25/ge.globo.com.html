Context

ge (formerly GloboEsporte.com) is the sports portal of Grupo Globo, Latin America's largest media group. It launched in 2003 as EsporteNaGlobo.com.br, was renamed GloboEsporte.com in 2005, and rebranded to "ge" in July 2020. The newsroom is run by TV Globo's sports division and feeds on the group's broadcast operation — TV Globo, SporTV, Premiere, and Combate — with five regional editorial offices. It monetizes with display ads and as the top of the funnel for Globoplay/Premiere subscriptions: the video API carries an explicit subscriber_only flag per clip (Probe Log). For an index builder, ge is the opposite of the locked-down platforms this project usually meets: a single-publisher corpus of roughly 2M Portuguese sports articles, fully enumerated by dated daily sitemaps, kept fresh by per-section RSS, and backed by two JSON endpoints that answer without any token. Its live-match pages ("Tempo Real") are a distinct, high-churn object class alongside the immutable article archive.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where ge differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Canonical article URLs end in .ghtml and embed the publication date (/noticia/YYYY/MM/DD/slug.ghtml). Legacy hosts 301 cleanly to ge.globo.com (Probe Log), so dedup is trivial. Three stable ID systems coexist: the URL, Falkor post UUIDs, and numeric globovideos IDs.

Retrieval unit

Two distinct classes: the immutable article (.ghtml) and the mutable live-match page (/jogo/…), which behaves like a stream for ~3 hours and then becomes a static match record. Indexing both on one cadence either wastes budget or misses the live window.

Cross-modal joins

Article → video joins via numeric IDs embedded in page source, resolvable through the public playlist API. Falkor posts carry image originals plus a backstage content UUID; the backstage API itself refuses public connections (Probe Log), so that UUID is a dedup key, not a fetch path.

Authoritative content field

Unusually for a major news site, there is no NewsArticle JSON-LD (Probe Log) — og: tags and the Falkor JSON are the metadata sources. The Falkor payload is richer than the page markup (rights holders, crop geometry, timestamps), inverting the usual page-first assumption.

Freshness & recrawl

Daily sitemaps are append-only and close at day's end — a clean incremental-crawl contract. Anything older than a few days never needs revisiting; the entire mutation load concentrates in live-match pages and the homepage (cache-control: max-age=10, Probe Log).

Quality & spam

Single-publisher editorial corpus: no UGC spam surface in the crawlable pages (comments sit behind login). Trust tiering is uniform; the only sponsored surface is the robots-excluded /publieditorial path (Probe Log).

AI-crawler stance

The block list is narrow rather than hostile: only CCBot, GPTBot, and Google-Extended are refused; ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and newer agents inherit near-full wildcard access (Probe Log) — the opposite of the default assumption that AI UAs are broadly named and blocked.

User query profile

Team/player/match queries in Portuguese with extreme time-sensitivity ("resultado", "escalação", "onde assistir"). News value decays in hours, but the retro-dated archive (1958 World Cup days in the sitemap) serves a durable historical-lookup intent.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: article (.ghtml) as the primary unit; live-match page as a separate high-churn class that freezes into a match record; video metadata records joined to articles.
  • Primary keys & joins: canonical URL (date-embedded) as primary key; Falkor post UUID for feed dedup; numeric globovideos ID for article↔video joins.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) daily sitemaps — free, no auth, complete census back to 2010 plus retro archive (~2.3M URLs); 2) per-section RSS — free, latest 100 items per section, sub-hour freshness; 3) Falkor CDA JSON — free but undocumented, richest metadata (rights holders, images, timestamps); 4) globovideos playlist API — free, per-ID video metadata. All four ran unauthenticated in this sample.
  • Recrawl cadence: today's sitemap hourly; RSS every 15 min; live-match pages every minute during scheduled matches (the fixture list says when); closed sitemap days never.
  • Derivatives to generate: Portuguese ASR for free-tier video clips, team/player entity linking against a football knowledge base, structured match-record extraction from frozen Tempo Real pages.
  • Demo to build: a one-day ingester that reads today's sitemap + the Falkor feed, joins articles to video IDs via the playlist API, and emits joined JSONL — proving the whole corpus is reachable without a single token.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The two JSON endpoints are internal infrastructure, not products, and can close without notice. Basis: neither falkor-cda.bastian.globo.com nor api.globovideos.com has public documentation or terms, and the sibling backstage API they reference already refuses public connections (Probe Log). The sitemap+RSS plan must survive their loss.
  • inferred The AI-crawler allowance is one robots.txt edit from disappearing. Basis: Globo already blocks CCBot/GPTBot/Google-Extended wholesale (Probe Log); extending the same Disallow: / to ClaudeBot et al. is a one-line change, and the current omissions look like list staleness, not policy.
  • Image rights are per-photo, not per-site: the rightsHolder field names clubs and agencies per image (Probe Log) — a bulk image mirror inherits third-party agency claims, not just Globo's.
  • Video is migrating off-host: ge tv (Sept 2025, Verified Metrics) puts flagship live sports on YouTube and Globoplay, so the on-host video corpus may increasingly be clips-only while long-form lives on other platforms' terms.

Inferences

  • The Falkor and globovideos endpoints are closable internal infrastructure rather than committed public APIs. Basis: no documentation or terms found; discovered only via page source; the sibling backstage host already refuses public connections.
  • The narrow AI-crawler block list reflects staleness, not deliberate allowance. Basis: the three blocked UAs (CCBot, GPTBot, Google-Extended) are the 2023-era first wave; no UA that emerged since appears anywhere in the 23-line file.
  • Pre-2010 sitemap days are retro-dated archive content, not contemporaneous publication. Basis: the portal launched in 2003, yet the index carries populated 1958 World Cup days while sampled 2005–2009 days return empty.
