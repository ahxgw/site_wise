Daily Site Profile

dmm.co.jp

dmm.co.jp is FANZA: 1.6M sitemap-listed product pages that open fully for a Googlebot user-agent string and age-gate everyone else.

Adult Digital Commerce Video Publishing Japan

Evidence: verified inferred (must state basis) open — conventions in the methodology.

Context

DMM began in 1998 as a Japanese video rental business and now runs 60-plus businesses off one account system. In March 2018 the adult division was carved out into a separate company, Digital Commerce, and rebranded FANZA that August — leaving an awkward artifact for anyone mapping the web. The corporate domain dmm.com serves the all-ages catalogue; the older, higher-traffic dmm.co.jp serves FANZA: adult video, doujin works, ebooks. They share one login, one point balance, one affiliate API. What makes the site worth a report is not its subject matter but its access design, which is unusually legible: a very large, well-marked-up catalogue is published in full through Google-format sitemaps, then hidden behind a single user-agent check. The catalogue is not protected — it is curated for one audience, and here that arrangement is cheap to measure.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site departs from the default platform assumptions.

The wall is a string, not a system

The methodology assumes the SEO sliver is a genuinely curated subset. Here it is the entire catalogue, and the approval check is a substring match on the user-agent: the bare token Googlebot/2.1, with no URL and no reverse-DNS possibility, opened the same 411,948-byte page as the full string (Probe Log). No IP verification is performed.

Stated policy and enforced policy point opposite ways

Only Google-Extended is excluded in robots.txt. GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, CCBot, Applebot-Extended and Amazonbot go unmentioned on all three hosts and fall through to the permissive * group — then get stopped in practice by the age gate rather than by policy.

Identifier & dedup

The content ID is the spine, exposed as sku in JSON-LD, as ?id=/cid= in URLs, as content_id in the API, and inside every CDN asset path. But its form is not stable: the API separates content_id (15dss00145) from product_id (15dss00145dl), the embed player uses a zero-stripped third variant (15dss145), and one work can appear under different IDs per floor. Normalising these is the first real engineering task.

Cross-modal joins

Unusually good, and free. One sitemap entry ties page URL, MP4, thumbnail, publication date and safety flag together; the page's JSON-LD adds performers, genres, price and rating; the image sitemap ties every still back to the same product URL. Joins that normally need an authenticated API are pre-computed here in crawlable files.

Safety labels use two vocabularies

hasAdultConsideration appeared on 3 of 3 doujin pages and 0 of 4 video pages (Probe Log); the video sitemaps carry video:family_friendly=no instead. Two floors of one host, two schemes — an index must derive its own label rather than trust either field's presence.

Freshness & recrawl

cache-control: no-cache, private on detail pages means no conditional-request savings — every recrawl is a full ~400KB fetch. Sitemap lastmod is the only economical change signal and it is per-file, not per-URL: cheap to detect that something in a 50,000-URL shard moved, expensive to find out what.

Discovery requires walking sibling hosts

Two of the apex's four declared sitemaps are dead (Probe Log), while the two largest indexes — video and ebooks — are declared only in their own hosts' robots.txt. Trusting the apex file alone would miss 60% of the corpus.

Objects published, queries withheld

On-site search is comprehensively closed to crawlers, and the sitemaps are the deliberate substitute. That is the reverse of the usual arrangement, where the query interface is open and the object list is not.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the product (one content ID), with its sample images, preview clip reference, performer/circle links and rating attached as facets. Reviews are a child unit on the ebook host only.
  • Primary keys & joins: key on (host, floor, content_id) and carry the product_id and zero-stripped embed variant as aliases, because the same work is reachable under all three. Join media to products on the CDN path segment (the content ID is literally in it), and products to entities on the API's numeric performer/series/maker IDs — the entity sitemaps give names, the API gives the IDs that survive renames.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The video sitemaps — free, no auth, and alone they deliver title, description, thumbnail, publication date and safety flag for 373K titles without fetching a page. 2) The doujin detail + image sitemaps — 656K products and ≈3.3M stills at the same cost. 3) The ebook product and review sitemaps — 379K products plus the only enumerable review corpus. 4) The Web API, for the numeric entity IDs, rating counts and price fields the HTML omits; three registrations, a 990–999 affiliate ID, and every query sharded under the 50,100 ceiling. 5) Page fetches last — 400KB each, uncacheable, worth it only for JSON-LD the sitemaps lack.
  • Recrawl cadence: video sitemaps daily (publication dates land same-day); doujin index daily for lastmod with shard re-pull only on change; ebook products weekly; entity sitemaps monthly; the broken live and mono sitemaps never.
  • Derivatives to generate: a content-ID normaliser across the three ID forms — nothing joins without it. Then perceptual hashes over the doujin stills to collapse works republished across floors, and a derived safety label, since the site's own two vocabularies disagree.
  • Demo to build: the user-agent gate itself. Fetch one detail URL under six UA strings, tabulate response size and final URL, show 412KB against 34KB. A two-minute measurement that demonstrates, more cleanly than any large platform does, that "closed to crawlers" and "closed" are different claims.

Risks to the Index

  • The gate can be tightened in an afternoon. Reverse-DNS verification of Googlebot is a standard, documented control that DMM has not applied. If it is, every path in this plan except the API disappears at once — the sitemaps stay readable, the pages behind them do not.
  • The API gate is an affiliate contract, not a developer one. Access depends on an affiliate account in good standing, and the 990–999 suffix rule means credentials are provisioned per-programme rather than per-application. A policy change on the affiliate side removes API access with no API announcement.
  • Adult-platform regulation is the live legal variable. Payment-network pressure and age-verification statutes have repeatedly forced catalogue removals and hard age walls on this category of site. Either would convert the UA check into a real credential check.
  • Ownership is already split. FANZA runs under Digital Commerce while DMM runs the systems, and DMM's published revenue footnotes the entities it consolidates — Digital Commerce is not among them. An index treating dmm.co.jp and dmm.com as one property mis-attributes both traffic and liability.
  • inferred The regional block is likely to expand to dmm.co.jp. Basis: www.dmm.com/ returned 403 and affiliate.dmm.com/api/ redirected to special.dmm.com/not-available-in-your-region/ for our UA on 2026-08-27, while dmm.co.jp served the same client normally — the infrastructure for geo-fencing is deployed and already applied to two of three sibling surfaces.
  • inferred Preview video is deliberately treated as the one gated asset class. Basis: stills on awsimgsrc.dmm.co.jp returned 200 to an anonymous no-Referer request while the MP4 named in the same sitemap entry returned CloudFront 403 both with and without a Referer header, and the affiliate programme routes preview playback exclusively through the /litevideo/ embed (2026-08-27).

Open Questions

  • Whether the preview-MP4 403 is a regional block, a signed-URL expiry or hotlink protection — closeable by requesting the same content_loc from a Japanese egress within minutes of pulling the sitemap.
  • How many audio works exist and whether any path enumerates them separately — closeable with an authenticated FloorList call, the only endpoint that would name an audio floor.
  • Whether review text for video and doujin products is retrievable at all — the API exposes only count and mean, and no review sitemap exists outside the ebook host; needs a credentialed sweep for an undocumented endpoint.
  • The API's request-rate limit — the reference documents paging ceilings but no per-hour or per-day figure; only a credentialed client hitting 429 reveals it.
  • Whether Googlebot and Bingbot actually index what the gate lets them see — closeable by comparing sitemap URL counts against site: result counts per floor.
  • Traffic split between dmm.co.jp and the all-ages dmm.com — no official figure separates them, and the shared account system means member counts cannot be apportioned.
  • Whether the mono (physical goods) storefront has a working sitemap under another path — its declared one 404s, so no census of that floor was possible.
