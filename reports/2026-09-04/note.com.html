Daily Site Profile

note.com

note.com is Japan's 82M-article creator commons: its sitemap shows five days, its unauthenticated API returns full text, and AI access is being priced.

Evidence: verified inferred (must state basis) open — conventions in the methodology.

Context

note.com is Japan's dominant long-form publishing platform: 2.41M creators, 82.09M published articles, roughly a quarter of them priced. It is the 13th most-visited site in Japan and, per an Ahrefs study the company cites, the second most-cited Japanese domain in AI search answers. That second statistic is the whole strategy. note inc. is not defending its corpus from AI — it is building the toll booth: a NEDO-commissioned RAG database (GENIAC) that licenses publisher content to AI vendors and pays holders on reference history, 630K auto-generated product pages engineered to be cited, and a creator opt-out with revenue sharing attached. Google, NAVER, KADOKAWA and Nikkei sit on the cap table.

inferred note keeps free crawl open as marketing for that paid channel. Basis: robots.txt carries zero AI-crawler directives (Probe Log) while comparable platforms block a dozen or more, and the same company meters AI access through GENIAC and stamps every note with an exclude_ai_learning_reward flag (Probe Log).

The governing tension: the richest path into this corpus — a 120-field unauthenticated API that hands over full article bodies — is the one path robots.txt tells every crawler to stay out of. The paths robots.txt permits are deliberately thin: a five-day sitemap, a 25-item RSS cap, a search API stopped at 1,000 results. An index built here is either robots-compliant and shallow, or complete and out of policy. There is no third option short of a GENIAC licence.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where note departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & retrieval unit

The note key (n + 12 hex) resolves without the creator segment, so /api/v3/notes/{key} works even when the canonical URL sits behind a note pro custom domain that would otherwise redirect through a session handshake. Creators carry a stable numeric id alongside the mutable urlname in their path — key on the number. The retrieval unit is the whole article: median 1.6K characters is a coherent single-topic essay and p90 at 5.6K stays inside most context budgets, so chunking costs more than it returns.

Paywall boundary

Unusually honest. The API returns the free preview and declares the withheld remainder as remained_char_num and remained_image_num, with a separator UUID marking the cut. You can measure exactly how much of the corpus you cannot see, per article, without paying — worth exploiting for coverage accounting.

Freshness & recrawl

The sharpest delta. The sitemap is a publication feed, not an update feed (50/50 probe), so edits to the 82M back catalogue emit no signal at all. In a corpus where ~40% of articles are still read a year later, staleness accumulates silently in exactly the documents that matter. Drive freshness off sampled dateModified drift, never off the sitemap.

AI opt-out is invisible to crawlers

note gives creators an AI-refusal setting and an opt-out from its paid AI-learning programme, but publishes neither as a machine-readable signal — nothing in robots.txt, meta tags, ai.txt, or the creator API. A crawler reading HTML cannot tell an opted-out article from any other. The one visible trace, exclude_ai_learning_reward (true for 3.3% of a 60-article sample), tracks the revenue-share programme rather than the refusal setting.

Machine-generated entity layer

630K AI Context Network pages are auto-generated from product feeds and populated with aggregated fan reviews, explicitly so that AI answer engines cite them. Genuinely useful as an entity spine — but they are synthetic pages built to be retrieved, and they share the /hashtag/ namespace with organic tags. Separate the two before treating either as evidence.

Language & spam profile

Overwhelmingly Japanese. note began auto-translating in May 2026 and 48.3% of sampled articles advertise translation targets, generated on demand rather than stored as separate URLs — index the original. Spam pressure points at the paywall, not at ranking: the incentive is to sell a ¥980 note, not to farm views, and preview quality versus body quality is unverifiable by definition. Seller reputation (follower count, note count, account age, all in the API's user object) is the only available prior.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, keyed by note key. Store free bodies in full; store paid previews with their declared remained_char_num as an explicit coverage gap.
  • Primary keys & joins: note key → creator numeric id → hashtag id → magazine key. The hashtag graph doubles as the entity layer, so tag → work/product is the cross-modal join that matters here.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Daily sitemap sweep — robots-clean, free, catches ~76.7K new articles/day, but expires after five days, so a missed week is lost permanently. 2) Hashtag enumeration at 50/page — cheapest backfill at ~1.6M requests for the corpus, reaching the ~87% of articles that carry tags. 3) Creator enumeration at 6/page — complete, but ~13.7M requests for 82.09M articles and it needs the creator list first. Both API paths violate Disallow: /api/*. 4) GENIAC licence — the only path both complete and sanctioned, and the only one that reaches paid bodies. Price unpublished.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemap daily and non-negotiably; the window is unforgiving. Back catalogue on a sampled schedule keyed to dateModified drift since no update signal exists — start at 90 days for articles with traffic, never for the tail. Hashtag entity pages weekly.
  • Derivatives to generate: Japanese-tuned embeddings on full articles (the unit is already the right size). OCR on inline images only, not decorative eyecatches. Skip ASR entirely — audio is effectively absent. Build a tag-cooccurrence graph from relatedHashtags: free, weighted, and better than anything inferred from text.
  • Demo to build: a five-day sitemap tail-latency monitor. Snapshot the sitemap daily, diff against the previous snapshot, and chart what fraction of newly published articles a crawler on a 2-, 3- or 7-day cycle would miss. It turns "the window is five days" into the number that decides crawl budget.

Risks to the Index

  • The API closes. Undocumented, unauthenticated, robots-disallowed and returning full bodies — every property of an endpoint that gets locked down without notice. Keep the robots-clean sitemap sweep independently viable.
  • GENIAC makes free crawl commercially awkward. note is selling reference-metered access to text a crawler can read today. Once vendors pay per reference, tolerance for unpaid bulk collection should drop — and the shareholder register (Google, NAVER, KADOKAWA, Nikkei) makes that enforceable.
  • Creator opt-outs are unobservable. Building on this corpus means holding text whose creators may have refused AI use, with no way to detect it from the crawl. The exposure grows with corpus size and cannot be remediated retroactively without a note-side signal.
  • Five days is the entire margin for error. Unlike a paginated archive, note's sitemap forgives nothing: a week of pipeline downtime is a permanent hole recoverable only through paths policy disallows.

Inferences

  • The 82.09M corpus is overwhelmingly text. Basis: 60/60 sitemap-sampled articles and every article surfaced across six search queries returned type: TextNote; 0/60 carried audio (Probe Log).
  • The five-day sitemap window is a crawl-budget decision, not a technical limit. Basis: note ships an unbounded magazine sitemap (45,782 entries) from the same generator run in the same minute, so unbounded sitemaps are within its capability (Probe Log).
  • The measured 76.7K articles/day exceeds the ~63.3K/day implied by official growth (82.09M at +39.2% YoY as of 2026-05-31, calculated by Site Wise). Basis: the gap fits three further months of acceleration plus deletions netting against the month-end "live articles" definition — the sitemap counts publications, the company counts survivors.

Open Questions

  • What do note's terms permit for automated access and redistribution? The ToS host returns 403 to every user agent tried (Probe Log). Closing it needs a browser-rendered fetch of terms.help-note.com or the ToS text from note's IR filings.
  • What does a GENIAC licence cost, and does it include paid-article bodies? The Q2 deck names the mechanism and partners but no price, per-reference rate, or scope. Closing it needs the partner enquiry channel or a later disclosure.
  • How many articles are AI-refused by their creators? exclude_ai_learning_reward tracks the revenue-share programme, not the refusal setting, and the refusal setting has no external surface. Closing it needs note to publish an aggregate or expose refusal in the API.
  • What share of the corpus is non-text? Company reporting gives one undifferentiated content count; probe sampling found only TextNote, which bounds the image/sound/movie share below the sampling floor but does not measure it. Closing it needs a type histogram over a much larger sitemap sample.
  • Is there an undocumented API rate limit? 12 sequential requests drew no throttling and no rate-limit headers (Probe Log) — far too small a burst to find the ceiling. Closing it needs a deliberate, disclosed ramp, which is not appropriate to run unannounced against a production API.
  • How fast does the back catalogue churn? With no update feed, edit and deletion rates are unmeasurable from outside. Closing it needs a repeat probe: re-fetch today's 383,570 sitemap URLs in 90 days and count 404s and dateModified changes.
