Context

ikea.com is the single global storefront for the IKEA home-furnishing franchise. The brand and the site are held by Inter IKEA Systems B.V., worldwide franchisor and the site's copyright holder; the retail operations behind most country storefronts belong to Ingka Group, largest of 13 franchisees and 87% of IKEA retail sales. Franchisees pay Inter IKEA 3% of net sales. Revenue is product sales — there is no ad business whose incentives would shape the crawlable surface. For a full-modal index ikea.com is the clearest example yet of a large commerce site that has not closed down: no AI-crawler restrictions, byte-identical HTML to every user agent tested, and product text, image sets, direct MP4 files, pre-written video transcripts, review excerpts and downloadable 3D models all on ordinary public paths. Its search backend sits on a separate host whose robots.txt allows everything. The counterweight is licensing, not access: nothing in the published legal pages grants any reuse right, and the assortment is a per-market catalogue replicated across 91 locales, so most of the apparent corpus is translation rather than new objects.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where ikea.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

The AI-crawler default does not hold

The methodology assumes AI crawlers are blocked and a curated sliver goes to allowlisted search bots. Neither applies: no AI-crawler rule exists, and five user agents including ClaudeBot and GPTBot got byte-identical responses (probe log). The one named block is AmazonProductDiscoverybot — IKEA is defending its assortment data from a retail rival, not its text from model training. The ~120 Disallow lines that do exist target crawl-budget waste and account surfaces, excluding no product, category, editorial, image, video, 3D or PDF path: SEO hygiene, not access control.

Two namespaces, three notations

ART (single articles, 8 digits) and SPR (combinations, s+8) — 11,995 and 17,139 in en-GB. One article appears undotted in the URL slug (20503887), dotted in JSON-LD mpn (205.038.87), and as itemNo/itemNoGlobal in the search API; normalise by stripping dots. SPR pages reuse their components' photography, which is most of the 1.75× intra-market image duplication.

The sitemap is the only source of the locale join graph

Product sitemaps carry 58.2 hreflang alternates per product — 1.7M in en-GB alone — while the product HTML carries none. A crawler fetching only pages cannot tell which 91 URLs are the same object; one that parses sitemaps gets the whole mapping free. This inverts the assumption that sitemaps are merely URL lists.

lastmod is a build stamp, not a change signal

All 29,134 en-GB products share one lastmod and one changefreq, so recrawl scheduling must ignore both. The live signal is the search API, which returns current salesPrice and availability while the HTML sits behind a 30-day edge TTL — poll price and stock there rather than scraping pages.

Derivatives arrive pre-made

Two expensive pipelines are unnecessary: transcripts ship as written prose in the page's embedded JSON, so ASR is redundant, and image alt-text arrives as descriptive sentences in the search API, giving captions without a captioning model. OCR is worthless — assembly PDFs are wordless vector diagrams whose only text is step numerals, which is how one manual serves all 91 locales.

Quality and spam

No UGC ranking surface exists to game: the catalogue is first-party and centrally authored. The single third-party corpus is reviews, of which 1.7% is crawlable. The real problem is the inverse of spam — near-duplicate text at scale, since ~2.5M product URLs describe tens of thousands of articles across 91 locales.

User query profile

Queries lean on IKEA's invented product names, globally unique tokens and near-perfect retrieval keys: YUPPIENALLE identifies one article in every market. The failure mode runs the other way — descriptive intent ("narrow shelf for a rented flat") must route through dimensions, category path and room-context imagery, none of which the product name carries.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article in a market — (itemNo, country) — with locale-language pages as translations of one object, not separate documents. Editorial guides index as pages; PDFs and 3D models attach to the article.
  • Primary keys & joins: itemNo normalised to undotted 8 digits (or s+8) is the spine. Join media by asset ID ({assetId}_{pe|ph|fe}{n}) rather than URL, since the same asset is re-pathed per locale — that collapses 123,028 image references to 70,229 assets, then collapses again across markets. Join locales through the sitemap hreflang set; join taxonomy through breadcrumb and categoryPath keys.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Product sitemaps — one 50MB fetch yields URLs, image sets, MP4 locations and the locale graph together; best yield per request on the site. 2) Search API — no key, no pagination cap, 30 fields plus alt-text and live price. 3) Product page GET for JSON-LD, the 20-review window, the 3D block and transcripts — unavoidable for those four payloads. 4) Direct asset GET for JPEG/MP4/GLB/PDF, deduped by ID first. 5) Partner API (api.ingka.ikea.com, currently 401) — only for full review corpora and authoritative stock.
  • Recrawl cadence: ignore lastmod. Diff sitemap URL sets weekly for assortment changes; poll the search API for price and availability on commercially important articles; re-fetch product HTML monthly, matching the 30-day edge TTL beyond which nothing newer can be served; fetch assets once, since asset IDs and content-hashed 3D filenames change rather than mutate.
  • Derivatives to generate: embeddings over product text and room-context photography, since descriptive queries must reach dimensions and visual context rather than names. Generate nothing IKEA already ships (transcripts, captions). Skip OCR on manuals; if those diagrams matter the job is diagram understanding, not text extraction. Do build a canonical-object table mapping every locale URL to one (itemNo, country) — the artefact that makes ~2.5M URLs behave like tens of thousands of articles.
  • Demo to build: a locale-collapse probe — take the en-GB product sitemap, resolve the 1.7M hreflang alternates and the asset IDs, and show live how many distinct articles, photographs and MP4 files sit behind the URL count. It makes the most consequential number here auditable and re-runnable.

Risks to the Index

  • The openness is undefended, so it can close in one commit. No AI-crawler rules exist today, but nothing is committed either — and the site already runs Cloudflare with a worker in front (probe log). Adding managed bot rules is a configuration change, not a re-architecture. Snapshot the sitemap-derived corpus while the current posture holds.
  • The keyless search API is the most valuable and least durable path. An unauthenticated endpoint returning complete result sets with no pagination cap is almost certainly not intended as a bulk interface. Any key, rate limit or size ceiling would remove the only live source of price, availability and image alt-text. Do not make it load-bearing.
  • Access is granted, reuse is not. Every modality fetches cleanly and none carries a reuse licence; the only IP statement located is a bare copyright notice. Photography and 3D models are brand-critical assets, so a permissive robots.txt is not permission to mirror them. Index text and metadata freely; gate asset storage on an explicit rights review.
  • Locale replication inflates every corpus estimate. Counting URLs rather than articles overstates this site by roughly two orders of magnitude, distorting cross-site comparisons, dedup budgets and crawl scheduling alike. The ~2.5M figure is a URL count and must never be reported as a product count.
  • Franchise fragmentation is a structural ceiling. One host presents 50 countries, but the retail operations behind them belong to 13 franchisees, of which Ingka is 87%. Assortment, price and availability are franchisee-scoped, and a market can join or leave coverage without any change to the brand or domain.

Inferences

  • The AmazonProductDiscoverybot block is competitive rather than editorial. Basis: it is the only Disallow: / for a named agent in the file, it targets a retail rival's product-discovery crawler, and no AI-training crawler is named anywhere — the file discriminates by commercial threat, not by content use.
  • Product sitemaps are generated as one batch job per market rather than incrementally. Basis: all 29,134 en-GB products carry the identical lastmod of 2026-08-19, and five of six files terminate within 12KB of a 49.95MB cap.
  • The empty second-hand, optimera, agec and contentgallery sitemaps are global scaffolding rather than dead features. Basis: the same 19 sitemap types are emitted for all 91 locales (91 files each), yet several return a bare urlset with zero entries for en-GB — the generator emits the full type list per market regardless of whether that market runs the feature.
  • The video:restriction country codes are search-engine metadata, not access control. Basis: the en-GB sitemap declares relationship="allow" with GB, yet the same MP4 returned 200 to an unrestricted fetch with no geo challenge.

Open Questions

  • Global ikea.com visit volume — no absolute figure is published; only IKEA U.S. discloses one (457M). Closed by an annual summary stating total web visits, or a named third-party panel estimate.
  • Official size of the IKEA range — no FY25 article count located; the 29,134 en-GB URLs are one market's assortment. Closed by an Inter IKEA range statement, or by intersecting itemNoGlobal across all 91 locale sitemaps.
  • Rate limit and terms of the sik.search endpoint — it served a 1.5MB full result set with no key and no observed cap. Closed by a controlled ramp test plus a request to Inter IKEA for the endpoint's status.
  • Whether any published term prohibits scraping or text-and-data mining — the UK terms index and global legal page carry no such clause, and the terms-of-use document 404s at the path linked from the index. Closed by locating the current terms-of-use document for a major market.
  • Total review corpus and whether it is reachable — 20 exposed per product against 1,198 on the sampled article, with the Bazaarvoice routes robots-disallowed. Closed by Bazaarvoice API access under IKEA's account.
  • Whether product MP4s carry audio tracks — the clips were not decoded. Closed by ffprobe on a sample of /pvid/ files to count audio streams.
  • The category taxonomy dictionary — keys like fu002 and 700417 appear with no published mapping. Closed by enumerating the cat-* sitemaps across locales and pairing each key with its localised name.
