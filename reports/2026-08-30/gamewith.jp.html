Context

GameWith is Japan's largest game-strategy media property: per-title wikis of walkthroughs, character ratings, tier lists and drop tables, written by in-house editorial desks rather than a user wiki community. It is operated by a listed company (TSE Standard, 6552) that earns most of its money selling advertising against those pages, with esports talent management and a consumer ISP resale line alongside. For an index builder the defining property is that GameWith is a derived-data site: almost every fact it publishes is an observation about somebody else's game, restated as a table. That makes it unusually high-value for structured extraction — 71 tables on a single character page (Probe Log) — and unusually constrained legally, because the art and numbers belong to game publishers while the wrapper belongs to GameWith. The robots.txt is one line and blocks nothing; the terms of use prohibit commercial reuse of any part of the service. That gap, not any technical wall, is the binding constraint here.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where gamewith.jp departs from the default platform assumptions.

Two URL forms, one article space

Articles appear as either /<game>/article/show/<id> or /<game>/<id>, and the two ID sets do not intersect at all across 227,487 articles. inferred The short form is the newer convention rather than an alias. Basis: median ID is 506,302 for the short form against 304,427 for the long form, and the sets are perfectly disjoint (census probe) — an alias scheme would produce overlap, a cutover produces exactly this. Practical effect: dedup by (game, id), and do not assume the missing form redirects.

The sitemap is a trustworthy change feed

The methodology's default is to distrust lastmod. Here it is exact: the sitemap timestamp for the sampled article matched its JSON-LD dateModified to the second, all 227,883 entries carry one, and five random URLs were live. Ignore changefreq and priority, which are template constants on 99.9% of rows.

JSON-LD is a trap here

The Article object looks complete — it even includes articleBody — but that field holds ~2% of the page bytes and omits about 65% of table-cell strings. An indexer that takes the structured field and skips the HTML would index the introduction and discard the tier list, which is the part users came for.

Authorship is a desk, not a person

The author field is typed Person but holds a team name (原神攻略班). Treat it as a per-game editorial-desk identifier that joins to the game, and never as an author entity. There is no individual byline anywhere on the sampled pages.

Freshness is bimodal, not decaying

Live-service titles regenerate constantly while finished games freeze: 24.2% of the corpus moved in 30 days, yet the median object is 271 days old and the tail reaches Dec 2015. Recrawl by title activity, not by a global half-life — the Genshin sitemap alone shows 57% of its URLs touched in the current month.

Quality risk is provenance, not spam

There is no UGC ranking surface to game. The trust question is upstream: in 2018 GameWith apologised after guide maps were found traced from a competitor's site, and the company now describes generating articles from primary strategy inputs with an internal AI system. Both mean a passage retrieved here may not originate here.

Search is scoped per title

The WebSite JSON-LD on a game hub declares a SearchAction at /<game>/search/results?query= — there is no site-wide search action. Users pick a game first, then search inside it, which matches a query profile of "<game> <character> 評価" that arrives from Google already disambiguated.

The throttle has a search-engine carve-out

A high-volume crawl triggers a 403 dressed as a maintenance page, and during that window a Googlebot or bingbot user-agent string is served normally from the same IP (Probe Log). inferred The exemption is UA-string matching, not verified reverse DNS. Basis: the exemption applied to a UA string sent from an ordinary residential path with no Google-owned address and no verification round-trip.

Indexing Plan

How Site Wise would build this index.

  • Retrieval unit: two levels. The article (227,487 units) for navigational queries, and the table — each <table> extracted with its nearest preceding <h2>/<h3> as a caption — for the factual lookups that dominate here. Sampling suggests tens of tables per guide page, so the table index is the larger of the two by object count.
  • Primary keys & joins: (game_slug, article_id) is the spine, with numeric game_id from data-game-id as the title-level key. Join to comments by article_id through the comment API, to thumbnails by the predictable /article/thumbnail/rectangle/<article_id>.png path, and to body media by md5 filename — which gives free cross-article image dedup, since the same asset reused in fifty guides is one hash.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The 137 child sitemaps as a daily change feed — complete, accurate, free, and the only bulk enumeration that exists. 2) Serial HTML fetch of changed URLs at low concurrency; the ceiling is politeness, not quota, and exceeding it costs a five-minute lockout. 3) The comment API for the rolling 10-comment window plus replies, cheap enough to poll on the same schedule as the parent article. 4) The YouTube Data API for the video business, which does not live on this host. There is no fifth path: no dump, no RSS, no partner feed, and api.gamewith.jp answers 405 to every GET.
  • Recrawl cadence: drive entirely off sitemap lastmod, which is reliable here. Poll the 137 child sitemaps daily and refetch only changed URLs — that is roughly 15K URLs/day at the observed 1-day churn rate, against 227K for a naive full sweep. Game hubs are stamped Hourly and genuinely move; dormant titles need no scheduled visit at all.
  • Derivatives to generate: table-to-record extraction is the whole game — normalise each table into typed rows keyed to the game and article. Embed table captions plus headers rather than raw cells. Skip OCR: alt text is present on 100% of sampled body images and is descriptive Japanese written by the editor. Skip ASR entirely; the inline video is silent and captionless.
  • Demo to build: a change-feed monitor over the 137 child sitemaps that diffs lastmod hourly and reports which titles are churning — it validates the one claim this index depends on (that lastmod is exact), costs 137 requests per poll, and surfaces live-service update cycles as a side effect.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms forbid the use case outright. Prohibited act (19) bars commercial use of any part of the service by any method, and act (17) bars reproduction, republication and even analysis. A permissive robots.txt is not a licence; nothing in the site's machine-readable surface reflects this, so a crawler that reads only robots.txt will conclude it has permission it does not have. Any index built here needs an explicit licensing decision, and realistically a commercial agreement, before it ships.
  • Media rights are not GameWith's to give. Article images and video are game publishers' screenshots and art, carried under the tolerated conventions of game media. Even a negotiated deal with GameWith would not clear the image layer, which is the visually distinctive part of the corpus.
  • The Googlebot carve-out is the wall's shape. Bulk access is gated by a volume throttle rather than by robots.txt or auth, and the exemption tracks a spoofable UA string today. Hardening that check to verified reverse DNS — a routine change — would convert "crawl slowly" into "no bulk path," with no announcement and no robots.txt edit to detect it.
  • Provenance is contested and increasingly synthetic. The 2018 copying allegations and the company's own AI article-generation programme both mean retrieved passages may be derived from elsewhere. For a full-modal index that plans to answer from this text, that is a citation-correctness problem, not a ranking problem.
  • The corpus follows a volatile catalogue. Coverage is per-title and concentrated — the top 20 games are 51.4% of URLs — so a title's shutdown freezes thousands of articles at once and a new hit adds thousands. The 137-title set is itself the unit of change; watch the sitemap index for additions and removals, not just the URLs inside it.
  • The segment funding this corpus is flat. Media revenue grew 8.9% while operating profit fell 2.3% in FY2026/5, and PV was roughly flat; company growth came from esports and ISP. inferred Editorial output is being defended rather than expanded. Basis: segment profit declined on rising revenue while the deck attributes the revenue gain to PV unit-price measures rather than traffic growth.

Open Questions

  • How many tables and rows exist corpus-wide? The single sampled page held 71 tables and 1,333 cells; multiplying that out is not defensible across guide types. Closable by extracting <table> counts during a stratified fetch of a few hundred articles across title tiers.
  • How many distinct images and videos does the corpus reference? The md5 filenames make exact deduplicated counting possible, but only after a full pass over article HTML.
  • What is the comment API's real pagination contract? Seven parameter names returned an identical 10-item payload, which may mean no pagination or a CDN normalising query strings. Closable by reading the gds-article-comment bundle at img.gamewith.jp/gds-packages/article-comment/8.2.13/ for the client's own call signature.
  • Where is the throttle threshold? A 40-request burst did not trigger it; a 137-file, 44MB census did. Closable by a stepped-load probe measuring bytes and requests per minute against the 403 boundary — worth knowing precisely, since it sets the ingest schedule.
  • Does GameWith cover more titles than the 137 with published sitemaps? The homepage links only 68 game hubs, and the company has described a 1,000-title multilingual ambition. Closable by crawling the hub navigation and comparing the slug set against the sitemap index.
  • What share of articles is AI-generated, and is it labelled? The company describes an internal generation system, but no sampled page carried a generation disclosure. Closable only by a company statement or a labelled field in the markup.
  • Is any commercial licensing programme available for this content? No developer or partner documentation was found on either gamewith.jp or gamewith.co.jp; closable by a direct enquiry to the company.
