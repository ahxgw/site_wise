Context

Wise is a cross-border payments company that prices transfers as an explicit fee plus the mid-market rate, and earns roughly half a percent on volume. Its marketing engine is one of the largest programmatic SEO corpora on the consumer web: the sitemap declares ~13.0M URLs, a cross-product of about a dozen templates (currency converter, SWIFT/BIC, IBAN, US routing numbers, cost of living, stock tickers) times ~18–30 country locales. For an index builder the site runs opposite to most platforms — the HTML is enormous and thin, while the data under it is served by unauthenticated JSON endpoints Wise points crawlers at on purpose. inferred Wise treats its pricing and comparison corpus as a marketing asset to distribute, not a moat to defend. Basis: robots.txt carries the comment "Allow bots to access API endpoints" above two explicit Allow: rules for the comparison endpoints (Probe Log); the endpoint answers with no auth and is documented publicly.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where wise.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Three external keys — ISO 4217 code, BIC11, ABA routing number — mean this corpus joins to the rest of finance with no proprietary namespace. Dedup is the dominant cost: strip ?amount= (4.17×), then collapse the locale prefix, a presentation variant rather than a distinct document. No template sampled emits hreflang, so locale equivalence must be derived from the path.

Retrieval unit

Not the page. The useful units are the currency pair, the BIC, the routing number and the corridor quote — one row each, carried by pages of 443KB–958KB. Indexing at page granularity buys hundreds of megabytes of chrome per megabyte of fact.

Authoritative content field

Inverted from the usual case: the JSON endpoints are authoritative and the HTML is the derivative. A converter page's rate renders the same number /rates/live returns in 68 bytes. Where they disagree the endpoint wins — the page may be edge-cached, the endpoint is timestamped.

Freshness & recrawl

Four regimes and no sitemap signal to separate them: rates churn per second, comparison quotes lag 0–4 days, bank directories move monthly, marketing copy is static. With lastmod absent everywhere, a crawler either re-fetches 13M pages blindly or polls the endpoints for the volatile half and crawls the rest on a slow fixed cycle.

Crawl policy

The sharp delta from the default assumption: robots.txt names no AI crawler at all — no GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended or Applebot-Extended line in either direction. The blocklist is a legacy download-manager list plus AhrefsBot and SemrushBot. AI crawlers are permitted by omission; the API endpoints are allowed by name.

Quality & query profile

No user-generated content, so no spam surface; the risk is near-duplicate thin pages at scale, with 19 amount-variants of one USD→EUR answer competing. Queries split head-and-tail with little between: conversions ("500 usd to eur") and identifier lookups ("BARCGB22XXX swift code"). Both are answerable from a row, which is why this corpus feeds answer boxes rather than getting read as pages.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the fact row — (pair, rate, ts), (BIC11, bank, branch, country), (routing number, bank, state), (corridor, provider, fee, markup, dateCollected) — with the rendered page kept only as a citation target.
  • Primary keys & joins: ISO 4217 code, BIC11 (XXX-padded), ABA routing number, help-centre article ID; locale as an attribute, never part of the key.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) /rates/live and /rates/history — no auth, the entire rate corpus for kilobytes. 2) /v3/comparisons — no auth, explicitly allowed in robots.txt, and the only free source for rival pricing. 3) api.wise.com/v1/currencies — the namespace, one request. 4) Crawl of the deduplicated SWIFT/IBAN/routing/cost-of-living slice, paced against the sitemap throttle. 5) Converter pages — skip entirely; path 1 supersedes them. 6) Wise Platform API — OAuth, partner-gated, and carries no public corpus.
  • Recrawl cadence: rates on a polling interval set by your own freshness SLA; comparisons daily (quotes lag up to 4 days anyway); bank identifiers monthly; blog and help weekly by content hash, since no lastmod exists to diff against.
  • Derivatives to generate: a canonical-URL map collapsing ?amount= and locale variants; a BIC11↔bank↔country table; a corridor price panel keyed on (source, target, amount, provider, dateCollected); content hashes standing in for the missing lastmod.
  • Demo to build: a sitemap deflator — walk the 27 families, apply the two measured collapses (query-param, then locale), and report declared URLs versus true documents alongside the bytes saved. The 443KB-page-versus-68-byte-endpoint ratio is the headline it should print.

Risks to the Index

  • The open endpoints are undefended, which is also the risk. No auth means no contract: /rates/live and /v3/comparisons can acquire a key, a quota or a CAPTCHA without deprecation notice, and an index built on them has no entitlement to fall back on.
  • Republishing rival pricing is Wise's risk to take, not yours. The comparison payload names 16 competitors with fees and markups collected up to four days earlier. Displaying it under your own brand transfers the accuracy exposure to you, with no licence text to point at.
  • No lastmod makes incremental crawl impossible. 13M URLs with no change signal means either blind re-fetch or content hashing; budget for the latter from the start.
  • Sitemap throttling caps census throughput. 53 of 77 SWIFT children returned empty under retry (Probe Log). Any coverage claim built from a single fast pass will silently undercount — this report's own first census undercounted the SWIFT family by 3.9×.
  • inferred The AI-crawler silence is unmaintained rather than deliberate. Basis: the blocklist is a 1990s-era download-manager list (Teleport Pro, Go!Zilla, EmailSiphon) with AhrefsBot and SemrushBot appended, and the .well-known/security.txt alongside it expired on 2026-08-29 — both signs of a file that is not actively revised. Treat the permission as revocable.
  • Reporting basis changed with the listing. FY2026 is the first year reported in USD on a US basis after the 11 May 2026 Nasdaq debut; figures before FY2025 are not directly comparable to the ones in Verified Metrics.

Inferences

  • Wise distributes rather than defends its pricing corpus. Basis: robots.txt Allow rules for *comparisons*sourceCurrency=* under the comment "Allow bots to access API endpoints"; the endpoint returns 200 with no auth and is officially documented.
  • The converter sitemap is deliberately query-param inflated, not accidentally duplicated. Basis: 38,323 of 50,000 URLs in one child carry ?amount= drawn from exactly 19 round values, each resolving to a page whose rel="canonical" points back to the bare path.
  • The SWIFT family republishes one shared BIC universe per locale — roughly 210K distinct BICs, not 3.8M. Basis: 3.81M declared URLs across the 18 locales seen in served children, with 12.0–81.4% pairwise BIC overlap between locale children and 49,202–50,000 distinct BICs within each.
  • Structured data here is SEO decoration, not a data channel. Basis: JSON-LD appears on 5 of 10 templates and only as FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, BlogPosting and Organization; the rate, BIC and routing number never appear in any JSON-LD block.
  • Help-centre article IDs are the stable key; slugs are decorative. Basis: a fabricated slug on a real article ID returned 200 and rewrote rel="canonical" to the true slug.

Open Questions

  • Whether the open /rates/live and /v3/comparisons endpoints enforce any rate limit — closeable by a ramped request test from one IP until a 429 or challenge appears.
  • How often comparison quotes are recollected per corridor — closeable by polling one corridor hourly for a week and tracking the dateCollected distribution.
  • How Wise signals locale equivalence given no hreflang on any template sampled — closeable by checking for HTTP Link: rel="alternate" headers or an xhtml:link sitemap variant.
