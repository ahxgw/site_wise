Context

Investing.com launched in 2007 as Forexpros, rebranded in late 2012, and was bought by Hong Kong private-equity firm Joffre Capital in April 2021 for $500M. It runs on advertising, broker referrals, and the InvestingPro subscription launched May 2022. What makes it worth a report is not the data but the packaging: the same instrument corpus is published 31 times over, once per language edition, on subdomains that self-canonicalise and carry no hreflang — nothing in the markup tells an index the copies are copies. Two rendering stacks split the corpus: equities, ETFs, bonds and indices ship a 1.5MB Next.js page whose embedded JSON carries an ISIN, while the 112K funds and certificates pages run a legacy jQuery template with no embedded JSON at all. The newsroom is largely machine-written — 62% of the current news window is earnings-call transcripts, and bodies carry an explicit AI-generation notice. The retrieval problem here is deduplication and provenance, not discovery.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site departs from the default platform assumptions.

The edge gate is backwards, and it is the whole access story

The methodology expects robots.txt to carry the AI-crawler stance. Here robots.txt names no AI crawler, and the real policy lives at Cloudflare — which 403s Chrome, Googlebot, GPTBot, Bingbot and curl alike while admitting a bare ClaudeBot string with no IP verification (Probe Log). Rejecting a spoofed Googlebot from a non-Google IP is correct; granting an unverified ClaudeBot the same way is not. Observed access here proves nothing about permitted access — the door that opened is a misconfiguration, and the terms behind it prohibit storage regardless.

31 editions, self-canonical, zero hreflang

The defining defect. The same ~329K URLs are published on 31 subdomains, each canonicalising to itself, with no hreflang anywhere (Probe Log). Nothing signals that de.investing.com/equities/apple-computer-inc and the www copy are one object. Crawl the sitemaps naively and 96.8% of the corpus is duplicate. The only reliable cross-edition key is the ISIN inside __NEXT_DATA__, so dedup costs a megabyte of JSON parsed per page.

Two stacks, two identifier schemes

Equities, ETFs, bonds and indices give up an ISIN from embedded JSON. Funds and certificates — 112,461 URLs, 34% of the corpus — have none, so their identifiers come from the page title (ISIN) or an HTML attribute (Morningstar 0P… secid). One extractor will not cover the corpus, and the larger half is the poorer-instrumented one.

Sitemaps enumerate the wrong axis

The census lists ~1.2 URLs per instrument while 14 populated tabs per equity are reachable by suffix and almost entirely unlisted (469 -historical-data URLs against 82,591 equity slugs). The sitemap simultaneously overstates the corpus 31× through edition mirroring and understates it ~14× through tab omission. Neither error is visible without probing.

No lastmod, on a site whose value is recency

Zero lastmod, changefreq or priority across all 53 sitemaps (Probe Log). Only the news sitemaps carry dates, over a rolling two-day window. Elsewhere there is no incremental-crawl signal at all, and cache-control: no-store removes conditional requests too — recrawl must be scheduled blind, on a corpus averaging 1.5MB a page.

Pagination wraps instead of ending

Forum page 1175 returns page 1's comments rather than a 404 or empty shell (Probe Log). A crawler that walks until it sees an empty page never stops, and every page past the floor duplicates page 1 under a fresh URL. Termination must be detected by falling comment IDs, not by emptiness.

The newsroom is largely machine output, and says so

62% of the current news window is earnings-call transcripts, bodies carry an explicit "generated with the support of AI" notice, and the NewsArticle schema declares a Person author with an empty name (Probe Log). Provenance must be assigned at ingest rather than read off a byline. The two-year expires value is the only retention hint published.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the instrument, one record per ISIN (or 0P… secid for funds), assembled from its overview plus the tabs worth having — never one document per URL, which inflates the corpus ~31× through editions before any tab fan-out. The comment and the article are the secondary units that are genuine documents.
  • Primary keys & joins: ISIN first — identical across all 31 editions, and joins outward to any security master without a crosswalk. Fall back to (exchange, symbol) from __NEXT_DATA__; use the URL slug only as a last resort, since the slug is shared across editions and is the thing being deduplicated. Funds key on the Morningstar secid from the title. Comments key on the global comment ID, which is site-wide monotonic and so doubles as a timestamp proxy and a completeness check.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) News sitemaps — the only dated discovery surface and the only corpus with a plausible first-party licence claim, though AI provenance must be recorded. 2) Forums via {slug}-commentary, the one corpus Investing.com owns, with full retention to 2013. 3) Instrument records from __NEXT_DATA__ on www only, ingested but flagged no-display pending a licence the current terms do not grant. 4) Economic-calendar events, small and well-structured. 5) Nothing under /pro/ — 16,956 URLs of empty shell, wasted budget despite being explicitly robots-allowed.
  • Recrawl cadence: news sitemaps every 15 minutes, since a two-day window at 314 articles/day is all that stands between you and permanent loss. Active-ticker forums hourly during market hours, the long tail monthly. Instrument records weekly, accepting that no lastmod and no conditional requests make this guesswork. Sitemap index weekly to detect added or delisted instruments — the only signal available.
  • Derivatives to generate: an edition-collapsing key over ISIN before anything is stored, or the index is 97% duplicate. Then a shell-and-placeholder detector — the ~41KB /pro/ bootstrap and the 45 aaaa aaaaa runs on every Next.js page must be stripped before text extraction, or every document inherits the same gated-widget noise. Then provenance tagging on news, since the byline will not supply it.
  • Demo to build: the duplication meter. Fetch one instrument slug from all 31 editions, show 31 self-canonical pages with zero hreflang, and print the single ISIN identical across all of them. The point lands in one screen: the dedup key exists, but it is buried a megabyte deep in a JSON blob instead of in the HTML attribute built for the job.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms forbid storage, not merely redistribution. "It is prohibited to use, store, reproduce, display, modify, transmit or distribute the data contained in this website without the explicit prior written permission of Fusion Media and/or the data provider." Indexing is storage. Unlike a display-only restriction, this one has no compliant read-and-discard reading, and it is the governing fact for everything except the forum corpus.
  • The rights are not Fusion Media's to grant. Terms reserve all intellectual property to "the providers and/or the exchange providing the data," so any licence negotiation runs to exchanges and vendors, not to Investing.com. Vendor terms can change with no crawl-visible signal.
  • The edge allowance is a bug and will close. An index built on the current ClaudeBot pass-through is one Cloudflare rule edit from total loss of access, with no deprecation window and no warning. Any plan that depends on it is not a plan.
  • Personal data sits inside the one indexable corpus. Forum comments carry real display names, stable user IDs and profile links, retained unbroken since 2013. That is the asset and the liability in the same object, and erasure requests reach a 13-year archive with no deletion signal published anywhere.
  • inferred The forum corpus is in structural decline and its recent volume no longer justifies aggressive recrawl. Basis: global comment-ID issuance measured from dated deep pages fell from 19,742/day across 2020-09 → 2022-05 to 6,416/day across 2025-01 → 2026-08, a 68% drop, while the ID counter itself confirms no renumbering (Probe Log, 2026-08-28).
  • inferred The /pro/ robots allowlist is maintained for a rendering path crawlers do not get, so those URLs will stay empty rather than fill in. Basis: robots.txt hand-lists ~35 exact /pro/* Allow patterns including per-metric explorer paths, and the sitemaps publish 16,956 matching URLs, yet all five sampled return the identical ~41KB SPA bootstrap with html { display: none; } and no content (Probe Log, 2026-08-28).

Inferences

  • The ClaudeBot allowance is an unverified user-agent string match rather than a deliberate, authenticated grant. Basis: the exact token ClaudeBot returns HTTP 200 from an ordinary residential IP while claudebot, Claude and anthropic all return 403, and a spoofed Googlebot from the same IP is correctly rejected — so the rule keys on a case-sensitive substring and performs no reverse-DNS or IP verification (Probe Log, 2026-08-28).
  • Funds and certificates are served by an older, separately maintained application rather than a variant of the Next.js template. Basis: those pages are ~330KB against ~1.1–1.7MB, contain no /_next/ asset references, no __NEXT_DATA__ and no JSON-LD, and carry jQuery plus pair_id HTML attributes that appear on no Next.js page (Probe Log, 2026-08-28).
  • Comment IDs are a single site-wide sequential counter, not per-forum. Basis: comments timestamped within the same hours on the Apple, Tesla, EUR/USD and S&P 500 boards all carry IDs in the 47.47M range, while Apple's own deep pages descend monotonically with date to ID 52,368 at 2013-01-09 (Probe Log, 2026-08-28).
  • Two further inferences — that the forum corpus is in structural decline, and that the /pro/ allowlist targets a rendering path crawlers never receive — are stated with their bases in Risks to the Index.

Open Questions

  • Whether real Googlebot and GPTBot traffic from their published IP ranges is admitted while the same user-agents are blocked from other addresses. Closeable only by observing from a verified crawler IP, or by an Investing.com statement of its bot policy.
  • Whether the 30 edition subdomains carry translated instrument records or only translated chrome over one English record. Closeable by diffing __NEXT_DATA__ instrument objects across editions field by field; this report sampled only ISIN equality.
  • Total live comment count as opposed to IDs issued. The 47.5M counter includes deletions and any non-forum comment surfaces sharing the sequence; closeable by summing paginated floors across the ~22.8K forum URLs.
  • What share of published articles is AI-generated today. Wikipedia cites reporting of more than 50% for the first half of 2025; the on-page notice appears per article, so a census over one full news-sitemap window would give a current first-hand figure.
  • Whether the studios corpus is video and where it is hosted — 814 URLs, no VideoObject, no video sitemap extension, no media host identified in this sample.
  • Whether any licensed bulk or API path exists at a price. No public API, oEmbed endpoint or developer portal responded on this host; only direct commercial contact with Fusion Media would settle it.
