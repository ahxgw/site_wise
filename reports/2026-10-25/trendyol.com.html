Context

Trendyol is Türkiye's dominant multi-category marketplace: fashion, electronics, cosmetics, home and groceries sold by third-party sellers alongside own brands such as TrendyolMilla. Demet Mutlu founded it in Istanbul in 2010. Alibaba bought about 85% in July 2018, and its latest annual report calls Trendyol "by far the leading e-commerce platform in Türkiye in terms of GMV in 2025". Revenue comes from seller commissions, own-brand retail and ads (the AdSense ads.txt line and gads=true robots carve-outs). For an index builder it is the deepest structured catalogue of Turkish-language product data: brand, category tree, variant group, price, seller and reviews on every product page. It is also multilingual. One numeric product ID is shared by the Turkish page, eleven European/global locale paths on trendyol.com and the Gulf storefronts trendyol.sa and trendyol.ae. The catch is access: the sitemaps and image CDN are open, while HTML and JSON gateways on trendyol.com refuse non-browser clients outright.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The product ID after -p- is the key; the brand and title slugs are cosmetic and differ by locale. The same ID appears under up to 15 locale URLs on three domains, so dedup on ID and keep locale titles as aliases. productGroupId (in state and JSON-LD) groups colour/size siblings that each have their own product ID; collapse on it for a "one result per item" view.

Retrieval unit

The product group with variants underneath. Each variant carries price, stock and seller (merchantListing). Brand, category and seller store pages are navigational hubs and secondary retrieval units.

Cross-modal joins

Images join to products only through the sitemap or JSON-LD: CDN paths are UUIDs with no product ID. Image captions are just the slug plus brand, so the image text is no better than the title. Category and brand IDs (c{id}, b{id}) are shared across all locales and both Gulf domains.

Authoritative content field

Turkish is the source language; other locales' titles are translations. On .sa the JSON-LD description is a boilerplate template ("With Trendyol.sa/en, you will be able to purchase…"); the real attributes are in additionalProperty and the state blob. Do not index the JSON-LD description.

Freshness & recrawl

All 2,560 children are rewritten nightly, but nothing carries a per-URL lastmod. Change detection means diffing the ID sets between days. The Gulf storefronts can be recrawled for price changes where they overlap.

Quality & spam

"genel-markalar" (generic brand) is the largest brand slug in a TR file (230 of 23,909): unbranded goods with keyword-stuffed titles. The 2023 competition ruling found Trendyol manipulated its own ranking, so its internal sort order is a commercial signal, not a neutral one.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: product group (productGroupId) with variants, each keyed by product ID; locale titles attached as aliases.
  • Primary keys & joins: product ID ↔ locale URLs (hreflang) ↔ image UUIDs (sitemap image:loc) ↔ brand b{id} / category c{id} / seller m{id}.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Daily sitemap pull at about 1 request per 3s: every product URL, title slug, images and hreflang, for zero auth; about 14GB gzip per full pass (calculated by Site Wise: 2,437 product files × ~5.7MB, the TR file size). 2) Gulf HTML for the shared subset (34% of TR products in one file have an .sa alternate) to get price, attributes, variants and rating aggregates. 3) A Turkish-egress browser crawl of trendyol.com for the TR-only remainder; untested, and the robots/terms posture needs review (see Risks).
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemap set diff daily; price/stock on Gulf HTML weekly for head products; taxonomy monthly; images on first sight only (immutable).
  • Derivatives to generate: CLIP-style image embeddings from the first image of each product (the titles are slug-quality, so images carry real signal); OCR on images, since product infographics often hold the specs; Turkish↔locale title pairs as a free parallel corpus for multilingual query matching.
  • Demo to build: a daily sitemap diff that reports new and removed product IDs per locale, plus first-image embeddings for the new ones — "what entered Trendyol today" with zero HTML fetches.

Risks to the Index

  • The .com wall is IP/fingerprint-based and also blocks the Googlebot UA from this network, so any crawl from cloud egress gets 0 HTML. The Gulf storefront header already says unverified ip, and it could flip to blocking at any time.
  • Sitemaps and the CDN are the only open surface. They are served from the same Ceph origin behind a Cloudflare rate rule that trips at about 80 fast requests, so an aggressive full pull can lose the one free path.
  • Alibaba is restructuring its international holdings (the Trendyol GO sale in FY2026), and an IPO has been discussed since 2022. A change of control could change data posture; watch the filings.

Inferences

  • The product sitemap is a curated sliver of the catalogue, not the full ID space. Basis: 8.1M TR product URLs against a top ID of 1.22B, IDs spread across the whole range rather than concentrated in recent ones, and Trendyol's own "1B+ items sold" scale.
  • The trendyol.com block keys on network reputation, not user agent. Basis: identical 403/blocked malicious bot for six UAs including Googlebot strings, while trendyol.sa on the same Trendyol proxy (dr-router, ty-lb-*) answers the same client 200 with unverified ip.
