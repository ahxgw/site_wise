Context

CNN is the original 24-hour cable news network, now a digital news site with a metered paywall over a free ad-supported core. It matters to an index builder for one reason: it is a paywalled publisher that still emits the complete article body as machine-readable articleBody in JSON-LD on every article page — the Google "flexible sampling" bargain made unusually literal. The question is not whether CNN's text is reachable but what it costs to reach: ~4MB of single-page-app HTML per 470-word story. Meanwhile the corporate ground is moving: owner Warner Bros. Discovery agreed in February 2026 to a Paramount Skydance takeover, shareholder-approved in April and still in litigation, so any contract signed with "CNN" today may be administered by a different company within a year.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

The JSON-LD is the product

On most paywalled news sites you scrape prose out of HTML and hope. Here the full body arrives pre-extracted with wordCount, articleSection, and both timestamps in one script block. Parse it and ignore the DOM — the 4MB around it contains nothing the JSON-LD lacks.

Bandwidth is the paywall

CNN meters humans with subscriptions and machines with page weight: ~273 bytes transferred per byte of text. A 327K-article backfill is roughly 260GB of transfer for well under 1GB of prose. That ratio, not any credential, is what makes ingestion expensive.

Two CNNs, one brand

The modern SPA and transcripts.cnn.com are different internet eras on one domain. The transcript archive is ~1,000× more text-efficient per byte, spans 2001 to today, and is untouched by the robots.txt banning every AI crawler on the main host. Best corpus CNN exposes, least defended.

Paywall markup is not a reliable signal

Treat isAccessibleForFree: false as a blanket template default, not a per-object access statement. inferred Basis: the live-blog page carries the flag while CNN Business's own launch article lists live blogs among the paywall exclusions.

Freshness has no incremental path

With RSS dead and no API, the only way to learn what changed is diffing sitemap lastmod values. news.xml (90 URLs) is the cheap high-frequency poll; the article index gives month-granularity change detection for the rest.

Geo-conditioned serving

Crawl identity and location change what you get. inferred Basis: 451 vs 200 on the same URL from two egress points, plus the city-level geoData and 20-slot FastAB cookies observed in the header probe.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, from JSON-LD, not the HTML page. For live blogs, the individual nested BlogPosting update. For broadcast, the transcript segment — CNN already splits shows into segment URLs, which is close to the right chunk size for retrieval.
  • Primary keys & joins: canonical URL is the key and it encodes /YYYY/MM/DD/section/slug, so date and section come free without parsing. Join video to article via thumbnailUrl and the CMS instance ID visible in player_loc. Transcripts key on (show_id, date, segment) — note there is no join from a transcript to the video of the same broadcast, which is the biggest structural gap in CNN's data.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) transcripts.cnn.com — best text-per-byte, 25 years deep, no crawl restrictions; 2) news.xml polled every few minutes for breaking news, 90 URLs and cheap; 3) sitemap-lastmod diffing against the 1,728-child article index for backfill; 4) video sitemaps for metadata only — take title, description, thumbnail, skip the 162MB MP4s unless a clip earns transcoding. There is no fifth path: no API, no dump, no working feed.
  • Recrawl cadence: live blogs every 2–5 minutes while active; news.xml every 5 minutes; published articles once at ingest, then only when lastmod moves; transcripts daily; ticker pages never — buy market data from a vendor rather than crawl 5.5MB pages for stale numbers.
  • Derivatives to generate: ASR is largely unnecessary — CNN hands you human transcripts. Spend that budget aligning transcript segments to video clips by title and timestamp, creating the cross-modal join CNN itself does not publish. Generate w_800 thumbnails, not originals.
  • Demo to build: a JSON-LD-only article extractor that fetches a CNN URL, pulls articleBody, and reports bytes-transferred vs. bytes-of-text — making the ~273:1 ratio visible and doubling as the argument for prioritizing the transcript host.

Risks to the Index

  • Ownership change mid-flight. The Paramount Skydance acquisition is shareholder-approved and in litigation (Verified Metrics). New owners routinely revisit licensing posture, and a pipeline built on today's open surfaces has no contractual protection.
  • The JSON-LD body could be truncated tomorrow. Publishing full articleBody on paywalled pages is a choice made for Google, not for us. Emitting only a snippet is a one-line CMS change that would eliminate the cheapest text path on the site.
  • The transcript host is an orphan. A 2001-era subdomain with no robots.txt running beside a modern SPA is a decommissioning candidate. Archive it early rather than planning to crawl it steadily.
  • Archive.org_bot is in the banned block. Blocking the Internet Archive leaves no third-party historical mirror — if CNN removes or rewrites something, that version is simply gone.
  • Wire-photo rights are not CNN's to grant. Getty and Reuters imagery dominates the article corpus; no image licensing conversation with CNN can cover it.

Inferences

  • Object counts for articles, video, and galleries are extrapolations. Basis: child-sitemap sampling described in the Probe Log; the article sample of 24 spans counts from 0 to 517, so the true figure could move materially.
  • CNN's crawl posture is "search engines yes, AI no, archives no." Basis: robots.txt lists ~90 AI and scraper agents under a single Disallow: / while leaving Googlebot and Bingbot unrestricted, and includes Archive.org_bot in the banned block.
  • The paywall is enforced client-side, not at the origin. Basis: the full articleBody is returned to an unauthenticated curl on pages declaring isAccessibleForFree: false, and hasPart names a CSS selector — a rendering-layer instruction.
  • RSS is abandoned rather than broken. Basis: five feeds return HTTP 200 with well-formed XML but content frozen at 2023-04, HTTPS was never fixed on the FeedBurner CNAME, and /services/rss/ now falls through to the SPA catch-all.
  • Ticker pages are rendered client-side from an XHR. Basis: a 5.5MB page whose only JSON-LD is a bare WebPage with no price fields, while /api/ is disallowed in robots.txt.

Open Questions

  • Exact article count — replace the ~327K estimate by fetching all 1,728 child sitemaps and counting <loc> directly (roughly 1,728 requests, a few hundred MB).
  • Does edition.cnn.com serve a distinct corpus or alias www? Measure: compare sitemap trees and canonical tags across the two hosts.
  • What triggers the 451 seen from Anthropic egress — ASN, geography, or UA? Measure: request one article from several egress points and UA strings and tabulate status codes.
  • How many transcript segments exist in total? Measure: enumerate /date/ pages from 2001-06 to present (~9,200 requests) and count segment links.
  • Is a paid CNN content-licensing tier available to index builders? Measure: direct inquiry — no public API, rate card, or partner documentation surfaced in any probe.
