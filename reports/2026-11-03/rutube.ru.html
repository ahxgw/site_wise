Context

Rutube is Russia's state-aligned video host. Two Oryol entrepreneurs founded it in 2006, Gazprom-Media took control in 2008, and Gazprom-Media became sole owner on 2020-12-28 (Wikipedia). It mixes UGC, shorts, live streams and catalogue from Gazprom-Media and partner broadcasters (TV Tsentr, Soyuzmultfilm, film and series channels), funded by advertising and paid subscriptions. The 2024 YouTube slowdown in Russia was its growth event: Rutube shipped a YouTube-to-Rutube transfer tool that August, and its monthly audience now sits in the low 80 millions (Verified Metrics). For an index builder, it is the most openly machine-readable large video platform profiled so far. A complete per-URL sitemap tree, an unauthenticated Django-style REST API and public SRT captions are all served without challenges. The constraints are jurisdictional: rights-holder geo-allowlists, Russian law, and an owner whose content policy follows the state.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where rutube.ru differs from the default assumptions.

The full catalogue is listed, not a curated sliver

Unlike the SEO-sliver default, the sitemap tree enumerates video IDs back to 2007, including zero-view shorts, in time-ordered 15K parts. Discovery is solved: a census is 23,157 gzip fetches (~7.5GB compressed at ~320KB per part, calculated by Site Wise). Incremental sync is the daily feed plus the last few parts of the main tree.

Two sitemap trees, one undisclosed

robots.txt advertises only the Yandex tree and forbids Yandex from /sitemap-google/. The Google tree has the same part count and is reachable by anyone. Use the Yandex tree as the published path; the Google tree is useful for cross-checking counts only.

Robots gives Google privileges nobody else gets

Only Googlebot may fetch query-string URLs, oEmbed, embeds and /api/play/options/. Every other UA, including Yandex, is barred from them. A non-Google crawler that respects robots should take metadata from page JSON-LD and the /api/video/ path (not disallowed for *). It should skip play/options, and with it captions and stream manifests.

Metadata is global, playback is not

Geo-locked licensed titles still return full metadata, comments and thumbnails to a US client, but no stream. An index built outside Russia sees the licensed catalogue as text-only. Store restrictions.country.allowed with each record, and treat the region-pseudo codes AB and OS as opaque strings, not ISO 3166.

Two clocks per video, and imports rewrite one

created_ts is ingest time and publication_ts is go-live time. Two sampled shorts ingested on 2024-09-03 carry publication dates in 2018 and 2023, so for imported items publication_ts looks like the original off-platform date. Rank freshness on publication_ts, but use created_ts for "new on Rutube". The sitemap lastmod follows neither exactly.

Search API is not a discovery surface

/api/search/video/ returned zero hits for common queries to an anonymous client, and /search/ is robots-disallowed. Rutube's own lexical retrieval cannot be used as an oracle; build recall from the sitemap census.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the video, keyed by its 32-hex ID (the same ID serves /video/ and /shorts/). Caption cues are child units keyed (id, cue start ms), and comments are children keyed by comment id. Keyframes are derived from the 1280×720 thumbnail only, unless rights allow stream access.
  • Join keys: author.id → channel/profile; category.id (42 values); tv_show_id + season + episode for broadcaster catalogue; caption sub_id. origin_type separates native from imported uploads.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Yandex sitemap tree for the ID census, then the daily and hot feeds. 2) Page JSON-LD for metadata, which stays within robots for *. 3) /api/video/ and comments for the richer fields, pending clarity on API terms. 4) Captions only after the robots question is resolved for non-Google agents.
  • Recrawl cadence: the daily feed every day. Hot-feed items every 6h for hits and comments. The back catalogue gets a quarterly is_deleted/is_hidden sweep, and channel profiles a monthly pass.
  • Derivatives: Russian ASR where captions are missing (~92% of the sample), a category classifier for the 42-value taxonomy, YouTube-import detection from the timestamp gap, and a geo-availability map per title.
  • Demo to build: a "same title, two worlds" viewer. Take a hot-feed title and show what a RU exit and a US exit each get (stream vs blocking_rule), alongside the full metadata both receive. Add a timeline of created_ts vs publication_ts that exposes imported back catalogue.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred A large share of the corpus is low-signal or migrated. Basis: the median of ~30 hits in the random sample, and shorts whose publication date precedes ingest by up to 6 years (Probe Log), consistent with the August 2024 YouTube transfer tool (Wikipedia). Expect heavy duplication against YouTube; dedupe on perceptual hash and title before spending ASR budget.
  • inferred The is_licensed flag is not a rights guarantee. Basis: a 1993 Hollywood feature on a third-party film channel carries is_licensed: true with a 32-country allowlist (Probe Log). The flag reflects Rutube's own rights claim, so do not inherit it as a reuse licence outside the allowlisted territories.
  • Openness has failed catastrophically before. The May 2022 attack wiped much of the platform before the Victory Day stream (Wikipedia). IDs that 404 after such an event are losses, not moves; keep tombstones.
  • Jurisdictional exposure for the indexer. The owner is Gazprom-Media Holding, whose head is UK-sanctioned (Interfax, 2022-03), and Apple pulled the app in August 2024. Any licensing or paid-data relationship needs a sanctions review first.

Open Questions

  • Whether third-party use of /api/ is permitted. Closeable by reading Rutube's user agreement (linked from the /info/ sitemap tree) and asking partner support.
  • True caption coverage and generation lag. Closeable by sampling 1K IDs across sitemap parts and re-polling recent uploads hourly for captions[].
  • Total stored hours. Closeable by summing duration over a stratified 10K-ID API sample and scaling by the per-part counts.
  • The deletion/hide rate in the back catalogue. Closeable by re-fetching a fixed 5K-ID panel monthly and counting 404s and is_deleted/is_hidden flips.
  • Why the search API returns zero to anonymous clients: auth, region or a different endpoint. Closeable by repeating from a RU exit and diffing the web client's search XHR.
  • Whether any Gazprom-Media entity that owns Rutube appears on the OFAC SDN, UK or EU consolidated lists. Closeable by querying each list for "Gazprom-Media", "Ruform" and "Rutube".
  • Where the ~1.7M channels missing from the sitemap went (empty, private or excluded). Closeable by probing profile IDs between consecutive sitemap channel IDs.
