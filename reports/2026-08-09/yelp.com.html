Context

Yelp is a US local-business review platform that monetizes through performance advertising against high-intent local searches. Its corpus is one of the largest human-written review archives on the web, and essentially all of it is unavailable to any crawler Yelp has not individually named.

The closure is product design, not defensive posture. The methodology assumes big platforms block AI training crawlers while tolerating general crawl; Yelp inverts both halves. User-Agent: * is Disallow: /, there is no sitemap anywhere on the domain, and a DataDome edge wall enforces the policy independently of the robots file. The same corpus is then sold through the Yelp Places API by the thousand calls and licensed wholesale to OpenAI — Yelp reviews began powering ChatGPT's local experience during Q2 2026. Blocking the crawl is what makes the license worth buying.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where yelp.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

The curated SEO sliver is the entire crawlable surface

The methodology expects an SEO sliver alongside a metered API. Yelp keeps both and deletes the middle: 2,857 sitemap URLs domain-wide, none a business page (Probe P7), and Disallow: / for everyone unnamed. General public crawl is not "being actively closed" here — it closed.

Star ratings are computed over a filtered subcorpus

48.1M of 300.3M on-page reviews are not recommended: reachable behind a link, excluded from the star rating, re-classified continuously, and exposed by no API field. An index treating page-visible text as the rating basis disagrees with Yelp by 16% of volume, and the disagreement moves.

Freshness is a licensing tier, not a crawl cadence

Partners get Reviews Webhooks; everyone else gets 3 excerpts per paid call. At the 150K-calls/month self-serve ceiling, one pass over 8.39M claimed locations takes ~4.7 years (calculated by Site Wise) against a corpus growing 60.6K reviews/day. This is a buy-or-skip decision, not a scheduling problem.

Identity-verified walls, and unreliable status codes

DataDome 403s a Googlebot UA string exactly as it 403s curl (Probe P4), against the usual pattern where declaring a search-engine UA reaches the SEO sliver. Nor do status codes help: /spk/index returns 200 carrying a "can't find the page" body while /spk/ hard-404s (Probe P9). Ingestion health must be measured against the API, and dead-page detection has to read the body.

The AI-permitted surface is the least structured one

The six user-triggered AI agents may read /article/ and the blog. Those pages carry no JSON-LD, no canonical and no Open Graph (Probe P5), while the JSON-LD-rich city and marketing pages are closed to them. Whatever comes through the permitted door needs derived structure.

International hosts are dark to everyone

yelp.co.uk and fr.yelp.com place User-Agent: * in the same blocked group as Bingbot with no Googlebot exception (Probe P6). Non-US Yelp content is not a smaller crawl target — it is zero, and non-US coverage must come from the API.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the business location, with rating, attributes and up to N review excerpts attached. The individual review is the natural unit and is not obtainable in bulk, so do not design for it.
  • Primary keys & joins: Yelp's opaque business_id plus the human-readable alias that forms the /biz/{alias} path — both API-returned, and the alias is what external links carry. Join outward through /businesses/matches, which exists to reconcile name-plus-address against Yelp's graph; that endpoint, not the URL, is the bridge to other local corpora.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) negotiated data license — the only route to the review corpus at scale, and demonstrably available given the OpenAI deal; 2) Places API Enhanced or Premium for a targeted subset, budgeting ~$55K in overage for one 3-excerpt pass over 8.39M claimed locations and accepting a ~4.7-year wall clock at the self-serve ceiling; 3) Open Dataset for offline modeling only — 2.1% coverage, educational license; 4) /article/ and blog.yelp.com by ordinary crawl, ~3.1K documents, the only free ingest. There is no fifth path, and building one means defeating DataDome.
  • Recrawl cadence: moot for reviews without a license. Weekly against blog lastmod for the free editorial corpus; Reviews Webhooks rather than polling on a licensed feed; quarterly attribute re-fetch on claimed locations, accepting staleness between.
  • Derivatives to generate: the structure missing from the permitted surface — entity linking from /article/ listicles to business_id via /businesses/matches, converting free editorial text into graph edges against paid records. Skip OCR and ASR: photos cap at 3–12 per business and video has no access path.
  • Demo to build: a coverage-and-cost calculator that takes a target city, counts claimed locations, and prints the API spend, call count and calendar time for a 3-excerpt versus 7-excerpt pass — turning Probe P8's caps and the published price sheet into the buy-or-skip number governing every decision above.

Risks to the Index

  • The licensing counterparty is the competition. Yelp reviews now power ChatGPT's local experience and Request-a-Quote integration is next, per the Q2 2026 10-Q. A licensee building local search on Yelp data rents an input its supplier is simultaneously renting to the largest assistant on the market, at terms it cannot see.
  • Nothing in the free tier is load-bearing, which cuts both ways. The 2,857 crawlable URLs and the /article/ grant could vanish in a one-line robots edit and no API-based index would notice — but any index that does lean on the free surface leans on the one part Yelp has no revenue reason to keep open.
  • Price and schema are the same lever. Attributes, photos and review excerpts are all tier-gated (66/23/base, 12/3/0, 7/3/0). Yelp can degrade a competitor's index quality by moving a field between tiers, without changing any access rule — a risk with no technical mitigation.
  • The rating basis is not reproducible from retrievable data. 48.1M reviews are excluded from star ratings by a classifier that re-runs continuously and exposes no flag, so any independently computed rating drifts from Yelp's displayed one, unauditably.
  • Demand is shrinking while the corpus grows. All three traffic metrics fell in FY2025, paying locations fell 1% in Q2 2026, and advertising fell 3% while other revenue grew 98%. The commercial logic of tightening access strengthens as the ad business weakens inferred Basis: the 10-Q attributes other-revenue growth specifically to "our Yelp Places API program" and the OpenAI partnership, in the same quarter advertising declined — the two lines move in opposite directions.

Inferences

  • The crawl lockdown exists to make the data license saleable, not to reduce server load. Basis: the same corpus barred to every AI crawler in robots.txt (Probe P2) is sold per-thousand-calls through the Places API and licensed to OpenAI, and the 10-Q names the Places API program as a driver of 98% other-revenue growth in the quarter advertising revenue fell 3%.
  • Yelp's robots.txt is maintained by hand, rule by rule, rather than generated. Basis: it enumerates 50 US cities twice over for /biz_photos/ Allow and /log Disallow, allowlists one single business page in Lost Springs, Wyoming, carries three bare numeric path blocks, and includes Asimov's Three Laws as comments (Probes P1, P10).
  • OpenAI holds a materially different crawl position than the other AI vendors. Basis: OAI-SearchBot is the only agent given its own robots group and the only one granted /spk/ (Probe P2), while Claude and Perplexity agents share a group limited to /article/ — and the 10-Q discloses a live OpenAI partnership with none named for the others.
  • The business.yelp.com group containing GPTBot is a configuration defect, not a permission. Basis: that group lists 12 user-agents and then terminates with no Allow or Disallow line before the Sitemap directive (Probe P6); a rule-less group is a no-op, and every other Yelp host blocks GPTBot explicitly.

Open Questions

  • Whether a commercial license permits indexing full review text, or only the same excerpt counts as the self-serve tiers. This is the single fact that decides whether Yelp is indexable at all, and nothing public states it. Closeable only by requesting terms from Yelp's data-licensing team.
  • Total business listings including unclaimed pages — 8.39M is the claimed count, and unclaimed listings carry reviews too. Closeable by sampling /businesses/search across a dense geographic grid with an API key and measuring the claimed fraction of returned records.
  • Platform-wide photo and video counts, and the video access path if one exists. Closeable by counting photos array lengths across an API sample and asking Yelp whether any endpoint returns business video, since /biz_photos/*?select_video= implies objects with no documented route.
  • The GraphQL daily points allotment per key, which sets the real bulk ceiling — the docs give point costs but show the allotment only inside an authenticated dashboard. Closeable by registering a key and reading the Manage App page.
  • Whether the AI API early-access allowance is 20 or 100 calls per day — the plan-details doc and the pricing page disagree as of 2026-08-09. Closeable by reading the RateLimit-DailyLimit response header on a live key.
  • What /spk/ is, given that it is granted to OAI-SearchBot alone and resolves to soft 404s (Probe P9). Closeable by re-probing over time, or by watching for a ChatGPT local surface citing a yelp.com/spk/ URL.
