Context

Shutterstock licenses stock photos, vectors, illustrations, footage and music from contributors, mostly by subscription. A second business, Data, Distribution and Services, sells the library and its metadata as AI training data. That business explains the crawl posture: the bots that would harvest training data are named and refused in robots.txt, and every HTML page, sitemap and even llms.txt returns a DataDome challenge to a non-browser client. Blocked visitors who reach the old sitemap path are shown a "Did you know we have an API?" page. 2026 has been hard. Getty Images walked away from the planned merger on 2026-07-07, the dividend was suspended, and Q3 revenue fell by a third. For an index builder the site is the mirror image of freepik.com: the pages are closed, but the media layer is a flat, unauthenticated S3 namespace keyed by one global integer ID.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

One integer keys everything

The asset ID is a single global sequence, shared by URL slug, thumbnail, preview, API record and the ID printed in the watermark footer. Slugs and type segments are cosmetic: the CDN ignores them, and so should dedup. Canonicalize to shutterstock:{id}, and treat the 20 hreflang paths and image./www. thumbnail hosts as aliases. Watermarked copies scraped onto other sites can be traced back from the printed ID with OCR, a cheap provenance join.

The sitemap is a 2018 fossil

Default assumption: big sites give search engines a selective sitemap. Here the public sitemap stopped growing in August 2018 (images) and December 2018 (video). It was served unchanged until mid-2023, then 404'd, then went empty, and is now walled. Nothing after ID 1.157B was ever listed. The bingbot group disallowing /sitemaps suggests whatever replaced it is not for Bing either. A sitemap-led crawler sees at most the pre-2019 catalogue.

The CDN is the census

With pages and sitemaps closed, an existence probe on the thumbnail path is the only anonymous way to measure the catalogue: hit or miss, plus an upload timestamp. The 33% hit rate means most IDs are not public images. They may be video, music, rejected submissions or removals; that split is open. Pace the probe: this run used about 1 request per second with an abort on 403.

Two preview generations

Assets up to about Dec 2023 have the 1500px watermarked comp on the legacy display_1500 path. Newer assets do not, so their large previews come only from the URLs the API returns. Any index that relies on the 1500px comp for embeddings silently loses everything from 2024 on.

Robots.txt splits training from retrieval

The file draws the line the company sells along. Training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Meta) get Disallow: /, while answer-engine crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot) get normal access. Yandex can fetch images but no asset pages. DataDome overrides all of it for any client that cannot prove it is a browser, so the robots.txt allowances are not usable without IP verification.

The query is the product

Users search concepts ("woman hiking with kids", "seamless mandala vector"), filtered by orientation, colour, people and licence type. Captions are contributor-written (the archived sitemap captions are short literal descriptions), so pair them with an embedding of the thumbnail rather than ranking on caption text alone.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the asset, keyed by integer ID, carrying title or caption, keywords, contributor ID, media type, upload date, a thumbnail embedding and a deep link to the asset page.
  • Primary keys & joins: asset ID (global and sequential), contributor ID (in API records and legacy thumbnail paths), the CDN upload timestamp. ID order is a usable proxy for upload date: 2.40B is Dec 2023, 2.70B Nov 2025, 2.84B Sep 2026.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The keyed API: search plus /v2/images/updated for change feeds. A free key reaches only the Free library; full coverage needs an Enterprise agreement. 2) For a catalogue backbone, the archived 2018 sitemaps (212M image and up to 12.9M video URLs, with captions) from Wayback, about 89K gzip files. 3) Thumbnail-CDN existence probes, only to measure growth and date ID ranges, not to mirror.
  • Recrawl cadence: the API change feed every 30 minutes; a monthly probe of the ID frontier; no recrawl of archived sitemaps.
  • Derivatives: image embeddings from the 260px thumbnails; watermark detection and ID OCR for provenance joins across the web; keyframes from preview MP4s.
  • Demo to build: a watermark tracer that OCRs the ID from a Shutterstock comp found elsewhere on the web and resolves it to the dated thumbnail via the open CDN.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The open CDN is an oversight, not a licence. Basis: every HTML and sitemap path is DataDome-protected while S3 media paths are not (Probe Log). The company also earns data revenue from selling exactly this catalogue (Verified Metrics). Expect the media layer to be signed or walled if bulk fetching grows; keep any mirroring to thumbnails for display with a link back.
  • inferred The editorial collection may change hands. Basis: the CMA's merger remedy required divesting the whole editorial business, and the Q3 release reports a goodwill impairment and cost cuts. A divestment would move editorial assets, IDs and licensing to a new owner.
  • The data business is volatile. Data, Distribution and Services revenue fell 76% year on year in Q3 2026. If the company leans harder on data deals, expect tighter API terms and narrower free tiers.
  • inferred The frontier may have moved. Basis: no thumbnail was found above 2.843B even though the 2.840–2.842B range carries Last-Modified dates up to 2026-11-04 (Probe Log). New uploads may have moved to another path or ID range, as the 1500px preview path did after 2023. Re-run the frontier probe before relying on it for freshness.

Inferences

  • Images and video share one ID sequence. Basis: a Nov 2018 video clip carries ID 1,020,226,309, inside the image sitemap's 2018 ID range, and only 33% of sampled IDs resolve to image thumbnails.
  • The CDN openness is an oversight rather than policy. Basis: the HTML, sitemap and media layers are treated differently (Probe Log), against data licensing as a revenue line.
  • Bing gets no sitemap. Basis: Disallow: /sitemaps appears only in the bingbot group of robots.txt.
