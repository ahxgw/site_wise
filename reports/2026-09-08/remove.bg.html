remove.bg

remove.bg publishes 410 documents as 14,350 URLs across 35 locales, most untranslated — while its real corpus, user uploads, is deleted after processing.

AI Utility Canva-owned Freemium API Zero Corpus Locale Inflation

Evidence: verified inferred (must state basis) open — conventions in the methodology.

Context

remove.bg removes the background from an uploaded photo in one click. Kaleido AI built it in Vienna in 2018; Canva bought it in February 2021 and the entity is now Canva Austria GmbH, with unscreen.com and designify.com as siblings. Revenue is credits: free accounts get 0.25 MP output, paid plans 50 MP, the API meters calls beyond 50 per month. For an index builder the site is a study in the gap between published surface and corpus. The surface is marketing and support — 410 documents fanned out to 14,350 locale URLs, carrying no structured data at all. The corpus is user photos and their cutouts: never published, never given a URL, deleted after processing. What is left to index is the documentation of a tool, plus the tool itself behind an API key.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where remove.bg departs from the default platform assumptions. Largest delta: the default two-track model — an SEO sliver plus a metered API over one corpus — does not apply, because the two tracks point at entirely different things.

Identifier & dedup

The URL is the only ID and the main source of noise: 14,350 URLs resolve to 410 documents. Dedup must run on body text, because the locale prefix carries no information — /de/f/blue-background is a real translation while /de/b/car-photography-tips is verbatim English, and nothing in the sitemap or markup distinguishes them. Only fetching both and diffing does.

Canonical signals are load-bearing and inconsistent

Three behaviours coexist: self-canonical (blogs, features, templates), hub-canonical (all 2,450 help URLs), and absent (the malformed set). A crawler honouring canonicals drops the entire help center — 63 genuinely useful support answers — because the site says to; one ignoring them indexes 350 template-leak URLs. Neither default works, so the help center needs an explicit override.

Authoritative content field

With zero structured data, the title tag and first paragraph carry the whole signal. Help articles need special handling: each renders ~90 words of real answer inside ~460 words of sibling-article navigation and footer chrome, so naive extraction returns a link list. Stripped to the answer block they are the site's highest-value retrieval units.

Freshness & recrawl

lastmod is worthless — it reports sitemap generation, so all 13,965 non-template URLs claimed to change today (Probe Log). The usual fallback fails too: max-age=0, private, must-revalidate on every HTML response means no shared cache and no edge age to read. Schedule on body content hashes.

Query profile and the retrieval ceiling

Demand is overwhelmingly navigational — one query, "remove bg", is worth 6.6M monthly visits, roughly 15% of all organic traffic (Verified Metrics). The intent is to run a transform on the user's own image, which no text index can satisfy: the answer is not a document. Ranking the tool page is the whole job; the 410 documents serve a small informational tail.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the document, not the URL — 410 of them, clustered across locale variants. Help articles are sub-units: extract the ~90-word answer block as its own passage.
  • Primary keys & joins: locale-stripped path as the cluster key (/de/b/x and /b/x → b/x), plus a body hash to decide whether a variant is a translation or a duplicate. Do not use rel=canonical as the cluster key — it is correct in four sections, hub-collapsing in one, and missing in the malformed set.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Sitemap index → fetch the 410 English URLs only: complete coverage of unique content for 2.9% of the full crawl cost, avoiding a 35:1 fetch-to-document waste factor. 2) Locale spot-check — fetch /de/ once per document, diff, keep the ~124 that differ; re-run quarterly. 3) Nothing else. There is no feed, no llms.txt, and no unauthenticated API — api.remove.bg 403s every request and blocks all crawlers (Probe Log).
  • Recrawl cadence: weekly for /help/a/* (pricing, credits and feature answers track the product), monthly for blog and feature pages, quarterly for case studies and templates (templates.xml has not moved since 2024-07-01). Drop the 385 malformed and dead URLs permanently rather than retrying.
  • Derivatives to generate: no OCR or ASR — there is no media corpus. Generate the two things the site omits: publication dates and authors for the 166 blog posts, absent from the markup entirely, and a canonical-cluster map, which the site's own canonicals cannot produce.
  • Demo to build: a locale-inflation detector. Feed it a sitemap, group URLs by locale-stripped path, fetch one non-default variant per group, and report published URLs against distinct documents. On remove.bg it should output 14,350 → 410 distinct → ~124 with genuine translations, and it generalises to every enterprise site fanning one CMS across dozens of locales.

Risks to the Index

  • Consolidation into Canva. A standalone brand inside Canva Austria GmbH whose core feature already ships in Canva's editor. A merge retires the domain and its 410 documents at once — nothing here is anchored to an institution that needs this hostname.
  • inferred The published surface is marketing collateral, not reference material. Basis: 35 of the 36 off-domain sitemap entries carry utm_campaign=internal+enterprise+referrals, the sitemap includes four live giveaway campaign URLs, and 83% of blog posts are served untranslated to 34 locales (Verified Metrics). An index weighting it as documentation will over-rank it.
  • inferred Nobody is maintaining the crawl surface. Basis: 350 template-leak URLs and a hard 500 on /g/smart-brush are actively submitted; the help center canonicalises itself out of the index; hreflang is declared 502,250 times in the sitemap and zero times in HTML; /b/feed.xml 500s (Probe Log). These are silent misconfigurations — expect them to persist, and to change without notice in either direction.
  • inferred The open crawl policy is accidental, not granted. Basis: robots.txt names only Googlebot and Googlebot-image and has no User-agent: * group (Probe Log). AI crawlers are unrestricted because nobody wrote a rule, not because access was extended — while sibling api.remove.bg carries a full Disallow: /. One added line closes the whole site.
  • The training-use statements conflict. The help center says uploads are not used for training; the privacy policy says media uploads may be analysed to train models (Probe Log). Whichever reading is right, an index quoting remove.bg's privacy posture is quoting a document its sibling contradicts — cite both or neither.

Open Questions

  • How many images remove.bg has processed, and at what rate — no figure appears on the site, in Canva's disclosures, or on the press page. Closeable only by a Canva or operator statement.
  • Whether uploads are used for model training. The help center says no; the privacy policy says media uploads may be analysed to train models, and it also covers three sibling brands, so the conflict may be one of scope. Closeable by a GDPR Art. 15 request or written clarification from Canva Austria GmbH.
  • How long "shortly after" is for upload deletion — the policy gives a duration for server logs (3 months) but none for image files. Closeable by the same request.
  • Whether Googlebot, the only agent robots.txt names, receives different content from other agents. All probes here used the default curl agent and one custom UA; closeable by re-running the structured-data and canonical samples from a reverse-DNS-verified Googlebot source.
  • Why the estimators disagree on India's share (20.49% of all visits per Similarweb vs 45.1% of search visits per Ahrefs) and on organic volume (43M measured vs ~32.6M implied) — closeable by a third panel, or by reconciling search-only against all-channel denominators.
  • Whether the 2,450 help-center URLs are indexed by Google despite canonicalising to /help — closeable by a site:remove.bg/help/a/ query against a search API, or by Search Console coverage data if the operator published it.
