news.yahoo.co.jp

Japan's largest news portal is an aggregator: 16.5B monthly page views on articles it licenses, then hard-deletes when partner contracts expire.

News Aggregator Japan Ads Ephemeral Corpus

Evidence: verified inferred (must state basis) open — conventions in the methodology.

Context

Yahoo!ニュース launched on 22 July 1996 and turned 30 ten days before this report. It originates almost nothing: 470 partner companies supply 720 contracted outlets' worth of articles, which Yahoo re-hosts under its own domain, wraps in editor-written Topics headlines, and monetizes with advertising inside LY Corporation's Media segment. A paid placement product also injects press releases into the same article namespace at ¥30,000 per placement (Web担当者Forum). For an index builder this is the rare top-100 platform whose article surface is genuinely open — no user-agent gating, complete JSON-LD, sitemaps and RSS covering the full publisher roster (Probe Log). The catch is on the other axis: because Yahoo licenses rather than owns, every article carries a contractual expiry and is deleted when it lapses. inferred The corpus is optimized for distribution, not permanence. Basis: the operating policy states retention is set per partner contract, sitemaps carry no lastmod and no tombstones, and the retention probe found live back-catalogues ranging from six days to six months across ten publishers.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The canonical /articles/{40-hex} hash is opaque — it encodes nothing. But the internal ID leaked in twitter:app:url decomposes into publish date, serial, outlet code and slot, so one fetch yields provenance and age without any lookup. The real dedup problem is upstream: a single wire story reaches Yahoo through several outlets and lands under several unrelated hashes. Outlet code plus near-duplicate text detection is the only way to collapse them.

Retrieval unit

Two units, not one. The article is the content; the Topic (/pickup/{id}) is a separate editor-curated object that points at one or more articles and carries the headline users actually see. Indexing only articles loses Yahoo's editorial judgement, which is the site's distinguishing signal.

Cross-modal joins

The outlet code is the strong key: the same token appears in the sitemap path, the RSS path and the internal ID. It is the only stable join to the originating publisher — because JSON-LD publisher.name and news:publication both always say Yahoo!ニュース. Trust author.name and the outlet code; never the publisher field.

Authoritative content field

The article body wins. The headline appends the outlet in parentheses, and the Topics headline is an editorially rewritten compression to at most 15.5 characters — informative about salience, actively lossy about content. Treat the two headlines as different fields with different jobs.

Freshness & recrawl

The defining delta. Deletion here is scheduled, not incidental: retention is a contract term per partner, and the measured spread across ten outlets was roughly 6 days to 180. Sitemaps offer no help — no lastmod, no tombstones, and every listed URL was live. An index must learn each outlet's expiry class empirically and snapshot before the cliff.

Quality & spam

Quality attaches to the outlet, not the URL, since Yahoo hosts 720 outlets under one domain and one design. Paid press releases enter the same /articles/ namespace in the same format for ¥30,000, so commercial content is not separable by path — only by outlet code.

Internal vs external search

An unusual inversion: the crawlable article surface is wide open to conventional agents, while the site's largest original corpus — 308K comments a day — is deliberately invisible to every external index. On-site search is the only route to it.

User query profile

Japanese-language breaking news, entertainment and regional stories, with the 15.5-character Topics headline effectively defining the vocabulary users arrive with. Recency dominates; an index that ranks on authority over freshness will mismatch demand here.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, plus the Topic as a separate curated record joined to the articles it points at.
  • Primary keys & joins: article hash (canonical); outlet code (join to publisher, present in sitemap path, RSS path and internal ID); internal ID date+serial (age and provenance without a lookup); Topic ID.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) the 662 per-outlet RSS feeds — unauthenticated, ~40 items each, complete roster coverage, the cheapest way to catch ~11K articles/day. 2) The 714 publisher sitemaps for backfill — a 274K floor, capped at 1,000 per outlet, so deep back-catalogues need feed history instead. 3) Plain crawl of /articles/ — no UA gate and complete JSON-LD, so full-text is available without rendering. 4) Comments — no legitimate path; design the index to live without them.
  • Recrawl cadence: feeds every 10–15 minutes (~8 new articles per minute at measured volume); sitemaps daily for gap-filling; and a per-outlet expiry sweep driven by learned retention class rather than a site-wide interval.
  • Derivatives to generate: an outlet-code concordance table; near-duplicate clusters collapsing the same wire story across outlets; per-outlet retention half-life estimates that schedule snapshots ahead of deletion; and Topics-headline ↔ article-headline pairs, which are a ready-made Japanese extreme-summarization corpus at a fixed 15.5-character budget.
  • Demo to build: a retention-class profiler — walk each outlet's sitemap, extract internal-ID date prefixes, and emit per-outlet expiry curves so a crawler can archive each article before its contract lapses.

Risks to the Index

  • Scheduled deletion, not decay. Retention is set per partner contract and articles may also be pulled early at either party's discretion (operating policy). An index that treats a 404 as a fetch error rather than an expected lifecycle event will silently bleed coverage, fastest on exactly the outlets with the shortest terms.
  • Rights sit with 470 companies, not the host. Anything beyond linking depends on 470 separate licensors, so no single negotiation opens this corpus — and any partner departure removes its whole back-catalogue at once.
  • inferred AI retrieval is excluded by name while conventional crawl stays open. Basis: robots.txt observed 2026-08-01 places 30 AI and scraper agents — ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, GPTBot, OAI-Searchbot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended among them — under one Disallow: /, while User-agent: * retains access to /articles/; the separate 50+ AI content-partner programme shows the intended route is a licence, not a crawler.
  • The comment wall is doubled, so treat it as permanent. A robots.txt Disallow plus a page-level noindex,nofollow plus no API is three independent refusals; budget no roadmap for this modality.
  • inferred Openness is a policy choice that could reverse cheaply. Basis: the article surface has no UA gating, no JS requirement and no challenge (probe of 2026-08-01), yet /api/ already returns 403 and the AI-crawler block is granular and actively maintained — the machinery to close the article surface is in place and pointed elsewhere.

Inferences

  • The corpus is optimized for distribution rather than permanence. Basis: contract-set retention in the operating policy; sitemaps with no lastmod and no tombstones; measured back-catalogues of ~6 to ~180 days.
  • AI retrieval is excluded by name while conventional crawl stays open. Basis: 30 named AI agents at Disallow: / against an otherwise permissive User-agent: *, plus the separate 50+ outlet AI content-partner programme.
  • The current openness is a revocable policy setting, not an architectural one. Basis: no UA gating or challenge on article pages, while /api/ already 403s and the AI-crawler blocklist is granular and maintained.
  • Site-side AI derivatives are not crawlable. Basis: AIまとめ and ヤフコメまとめ appear zero times in the server-rendered HTML of an article and its comments page, despite being named in the operating policy.

Open Questions

  • Total live article count beyond the 274K sitemap floor — closeable by accumulating per-outlet RSS history over a full retention cycle, since 139 sitemaps truncate at 1,000.
  • Per-outlet retention terms — the measured 6-to-180-day spread is a ten-outlet sample; a full map needs internal-ID date sweeps across all 714 codes, repeated to catch the deletion boundary.
  • Video article volume and whether video files have any documented reuse path — no video sitemap, feed or API exists; would need an LY disclosure or a media-partner agreement.
  • Whether the measured ~11K articles/day genuinely exceeds the official ~9,000 average or reflects sitemap double-counting across outlets carrying the same wire story — closeable by near-duplicate clustering on a single day's full capture.
  • What share of the 16.5B monthly PV lands on Topics versus article pages — no public breakdown; would need LY panel data.
  • Whether the 50+ AI content partners receive a bulk feed distinct from the public RSS and sitemap surface — the programme's terms are unpublished.
