Context

Steam Community is the social and user-content side of Valve's Steam platform; the store lives on store.steampowered.com (covered in the 2026-06-26 Steam report). The community host serves user profiles, groups, per-game hubs (discussions, reviews, screenshots, artwork, videos, guides), the Workshop for mods and in-game items, and the Community Market, where users sell tradable items for Steam Wallet funds. Valve earns from the market through a per-sale fee and from Workshop item sales through a revenue share. For an index builder, the important fact is that every UGC type lives in one numeric publishedfileid space at one URL pattern, /sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=. The host offers no sitemap and no structured data beyond OpenGraph. Its robots.txt allows almost everything, but a per-IP rate limit makes bulk crawling slow in practice. Several legacy XML and JSON endpoints still answer anonymously.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where steamcommunity.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Open policy, closed rate

This deviates from the default two-track pattern. robots.txt allows every crawler, AI crawlers included, and there is no curated SEO sliver: no sitemap, no JSON-LD. The real gate is an undocumented per-IP 429 that kicks in after a few dozen pages and lasts more than 20 minutes. A crawl budget has to be planned in requests per IP per hour, not in robots rules.

One ID space, many object types

Screenshots, Workshop mods, artwork, videos, guides and collections all share publishedfileid and one URL. The type appears only in <title> (HTML) or file_type (API). Sort objects by type before choosing an extractor. Most issued IDs are screenshots, so a naive ID walk spends its budget on low-value images.

Identifiers & joins

The keys are publishedfileid (UGC), appid (game, shared with the store), steamID64 (user; vanity /id/ names change, /profiles/{id64} does not), groupID64, 18-digit discussion gids, and the market key (appid, market_hash_name) plus classid. Reviews have no public ID in the URL; key them on (steamID64, appid).

Dates need a year

Stat blocks print current-year dates without a year ("Nov 10 @ 3:36pm"; every 2026 item in the sample). Parse them against the fetch date and pin l=english, or read Unix timestamps from the API.

The store review JSON has closed

The unauthenticated appreviews endpoint, which the June Steam report called the best review path, now redirects anonymous clients. Treat any open Valve JSON as revocable. The keyed Web API is the only surface with documented terms.

The market is the freshest object

Market listing counts and prices move by the minute, and the market has the tightest throttle. Index market items (name, game, item art, classid) as slow entities. Keep prices as a separate time series sampled for a short list of items, not refreshed across all 546K.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the published file (title, description, type, app, creator, preview image, tags, stats). Other units: the discussion topic (first post plus replies), the review (steamID64, appid), the market item (appid, market_hash_name), and the group.
  • Primary keys & joins: publishedfileid; appid joins everything to the store; steamID64 joins creators, reviewers and group members; classid joins market items to inventory assets.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The Steam Web API with a free key: QueryFiles per app (cursor), GetPublishedFileDetails in batches, within 100K calls per day. 2) market/search/render JSON per app for the item catalogue. 3) Hub HTML for discussions, reviews and guides at ≤1 request per 10s per IP. 4) Never walk raw IDs. The ID sample shows a sparse space dominated by screenshots and hidden items.
  • Recrawl cadence: Workshop by time_updated daily for apps you track; discussion lists hourly on hot forums; the market catalogue weekly, with prices only for a watchlist; profiles only on demand.
  • Derivatives to generate: type-routed extraction (images get embeddings; guides get passages); mod dependency graphs from collections; a market item-art image index keyed by classid.
  • Demo to build: a per-app Workshop explorer that hydrates one app's items through the keyed API and shows its type and visibility mix and update cadence, with a live 429-budget meter.

Risks to the Index

  • The throttle is the policy. The 429 window (Probe Log) is not documented and sanctions retries by extending the block. A shared-IP crawler can lock itself out of the whole host, market included, for more than 20 minutes.
  • inferred Open endpoints are being closed one by one. Basis: the store appreviews JSON answered anonymously on 2026-06-26 and redirects on 2026-11-13 (Probe Log). The XML profile and market search endpoints are the same vintage and could follow.
  • Privacy is per object and changes. The four visibility values (Verified Metrics) mean a public item can turn friends-only or private. Re-check visibility before display, and drop items that turn hidden.
