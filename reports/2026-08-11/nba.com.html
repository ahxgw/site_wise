Context

NBA.com is the official site of the National Basketball Association, launched November 3 1995 and operated by NBA Media Ventures, LLC with NBA TV, LLC and NBA Properties, Inc. The league is a private joint venture of 30 franchises and publishes no accounts, so the durable public numbers are audience figures and media-rights terms, not revenue. Money reaches the site indirectly: national media agreements running 2025-26 through 2035-36, sponsorship, League Pass, a Fanatics-run store, and betting-data licensing through Sportradar and Genius Sports. The domain matters to an index builder for one structural reason — it is two products on one host. The league property at /game/, /player/, /watch/ and /stats/ is a Next.js app behind Akamai Bot Manager that blocks nine AI crawlers outright and serves its own video from a 403-only S3 bucket. The 30 team subsites at /hawks/, /celtics/ and so on are a WordPress-backed network whose sitemaps publish 226,795 directly downloadable MP4 URLs, 183,794 dated news articles and ~1.03M gallery images. One robots.txt governs both; the enforcement does not.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where nba.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier, dedup & joins

The keys are unusually clean because they are the league's operational IDs, not web artefacts. The 10-digit game ID decomposes: 00 league, one digit game type, two season, four sequence — so 0022500507 is regular-season game 507 of 2025-26, parseable without a lookup table. Player ID templates the headshot; team ID templates the team path. The URL slug is not a key: the 5,487 player IDs produce name collisions (three distinct charles-smith) and one game URL has lost its team abbreviations entirely. Canonicalise on the ID; treat the slug as a label.

Retrieval unit

The game, not the page. A single game ID fans out to a league /game/ page, a team /{team}/game/ page, several highlight videos, a gallery and multiple articles across both teams' subsites. Indexing pages independently produces five near-duplicate answers to "what happened in Celtics–Magic"; indexing the game and attaching the rest produces one.

Cross-modal joins

Game ID ↔ team ID ↔ player ID all close for free. The join that fails is video → game: no video sitemap entry, and no VideoObject, carries a game ID. The only bridge is the title string — inferred reliably parseable for the automated classes, unreliable for the rest. Basis: league video titles follow a fixed "{Away} vs. {Home} - {N} Quarter Highlights" template with the date restated in video:description, while team-site titles sampled in the Probe Log are free-form editorial prose with no date at all.

Authoritative content field

Inverted from the usual case. On team pages the VideoObject beats the sitemap, adding duration and embedUrl the sitemap omits. On league player pages the JSON-LD is the worst source — invalid lowercase type, non-ISO birth date, full name in givenName — and __NEXT_DATA__ is what the page renders from.

Freshness & recrawl

Only the 39,245 league video entries carry lastmod; all 515,774 team URLs carry none, so change detection on the largest corpus has to come from HTTP validators. Against that, the news archive is 98% pre-2026, so cadence should track the season calendar rather than the corpus: hourly on game days, weekly on the back catalog, never on 2008 galleries.

Quality & spam

There is no spam surface — no UGC, single publisher, retired community paths blocked. The manipulation risk points the other way: this is the definitional source, so errors matter more than abuse. The concrete trap is the league soft-404, which mints an unbounded set of HTTP 200 pages with self-referencing canonicals; a crawler that trusts status codes will index "Page Not Found" as content.

Internal vs external search

Disallow: /search plus no OpenSearch document and no feeds: inferred the site is built to be entered from external search on entity pages, not searched. Basis: the sitemaps enumerate 5,487 players, 19,595 games and 30 teams as individual URLs while /search, /api/* and /amp/ are disallowed, and there is no feed of any kind to subscribe to.

User query profile

Entity-plus-time, and overwhelmingly recent: "warriors score", "tatum stats", "who won last night". inferred the league's own SEO priority is exactly that head, not the archive. Basis: the AI-crawler allowlist admits only /standings, /schedule, /players, /team/* and four static reference pages — the live-state surface — while the 183,794-article archive stays behind Disallow: /.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the game (keyed by the 10-digit ID) for anything match-related; the article for editorial text; the player for biography and career queries. Videos and galleries attach to a game or player rather than standing alone.
  • Primary keys & joins: game ID (type/season/sequence parsed from the digits) as the spine; player ID → headshot template and roster; team ID → 30 subsite path prefixes. Build a title-parser for video → game and record its match rate as a stored confidence, not a silent assumption.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Team sitemaps — 515,774 URLs, no auth, and the only complete news archive and video back catalog on the domain; requires hand-repairing the comma-joined <loc> values and URL-encoding the Trail Blazers space. 2) League sitemaps — 84,105 URLs; the only authoritative game and player entity list, plus the only lastmod values. 3) Page-level JSON-LD — the cheapest way to get durations, embed URLs and publication dates; skip the league player entity and read __NEXT_DATA__ instead. 4) Licensed stats feed (Sportradar / Genius Sports) — the only path that clears clause 9 for structured statistics at scale. 5) Direct media download — technically trivial for 12.3TB of team MP4s, contractually prohibited; ingest thumbnails and embeds, not files. 6) cdn.nba.com liveData and stats.nba.com — treat as unavailable; both are blocked, and the second returns 200 with HTML, so a naive pipeline stores garbage rather than erroring.
  • Recrawl cadence: game pages hourly on game days and frozen a week after; standings and schedule daily in season; team news sitemaps weekly with HTTP validators standing in for the missing lastmod; player pages at each transaction window; galleries once, never again.
  • Derivatives: embeddings over article text and video titles; a game-ID decoder table; a video-title → game-ID resolver with per-class confidence; an alias table folding /profile onto the canonical player URL. No ASR and no keyframes — the MP4s that would justify them are the assets the ToS most clearly forbids holding.
  • Demo to build: a sitemap-integrity linter. Point it at a sitemap index and have it report what is unreachable and why: comma-joined <loc> values, unencoded whitespace, non-sitemap entries inside <sitemap>, children that answer 403, declared content_loc media that does not resolve, and pages that return 200 while titling themselves "Page Not Found". On nba.com it would find every defect in this report's Probe Log — and it turns "the sitemap says 600K URLs" into "600K are reachable and 28,644 declared MP4s are not."

Risks to the Index

  • Clause 9 forbids the deliverable, not the crawl. A comprehensive, regularly updated statistics database is prohibited by name without express consent. Any index that normalises NBA box scores into queryable structure is the thing the clause describes, however politely it was fetched — and the licensed alternative is an enterprise contract, not a rate limit.
  • The ToS restricts linking itself. Clause 1 lists "linked to" among the prohibited uses; clause 6 confines even search engines to unframed word links to the home page or a team home page. Deep-linking a result to /game/bos-vs-orl-0011900039 — ordinary search-product behaviour — sits outside what the terms permit.
  • The 12.3TB is a temporary accident. Team MP4s are reachable because the sitemap publishes the file URL and the CDN checks nothing. The enforced posture already exists on the league side; it simply has not reached the WordPress network. inferred the team tier will converge on the league tier. Basis: both sit behind the same Akamai edge and the same robots.txt, yet 28,644 league content_loc URLs return 403 while 226,795 team ones return 200 with no gate at all.
  • The pre-2026 league video catalog has already vanished from discovery. All 52 /sitemaps/ files 403, so a crawler starting today enumerates 2026 highlights and nothing older through league paths. Whatever survives, survives in the team sitemaps.
  • Two silent failure modes corrupt a pipeline before they break it. stats.nba.com answers 200 with HTML where JSON belongs, and league /news/ answers 200 for URLs that do not exist. Both need content-level validation; neither shows up in a status-code dashboard.
  • The AI stance is self-contradictory, so an interpretation of it is not safe to cache. GPTBot and Google-Extended each appear twice — once with an allowlist, once as a bare Disallow: / — and the allowlist's player pattern matches nothing the sitemap advertises. Re-read and re-resolve rather than storing a verdict.

Inferences

  • The team tier's open media will be closed to match the league tier. Basis: identical Akamai edge and robots.txt across both, yet 28,644 league content_loc URLs return S3 403 while 226,795 team ones return 200 video/mp4 with no auth, referer or cookie check.
  • Video → game joins are parseable for automated highlight classes, not editorial ones. Basis: league video titles follow a fixed "{Away} vs. {Home} - {N} Quarter Highlights" template with the date repeated in video:description; sampled team titles are free-form prose carrying no date and no ID.
  • The site is designed to be entered from external search rather than searched. Basis: Disallow: /search, no OpenSearch document and no feed at five tested paths, while the sitemaps enumerate 5,487 players, 19,595 games and 30 teams as individual URLs.
  • The league's SEO and AI priority is live state, not archive. Basis: the GPTBot and Google-Extended allowlist covers only /standings, /schedule, /players, /team/* and four static reference pages, leaving the 183,794-article archive under Disallow: /.
  • The AI allowlist is not a tested configuration. Basis: /player/*profile$ matches 0 of 10,974 sitemap-advertised player URLs; the /profile alias it does match canonicalises to a URL the same group disallows; and both agents are declared twice in one file with contradictory rules.

Open Questions

  • Size and reuse terms of the pre-2026 league video catalog — the 52 /sitemaps/ files that would enumerate it all return S3 403. Closeable only by a restored sitemap or a partner feed.
  • Whether the team sitemaps' 226,883 video entries overlap the league's 39,245 for 2026, and by how much — closeable by resolving both sets to game IDs and intersecting, which needs the title-parser this report only specifies.
  • Episode-level podcast objects: count, transcripts and licence. sitemap_podcast_series.xml stops at 12 series pages.
  • Whether page content actually mutates without a lastmod — measurable by re-fetching a fixed sample of 500 team URLs weekly and diffing etag and last-modified, which would replace this report's assumption that the archive is near-immutable.
  • Whether Applebot and Bingbot receive the curated allowlist that GPTBot and Google-Extended get — neither is named in robots.txt at all, so their treatment is a Bot Manager policy invisible from outside.
  • What Akamai Bot Manager actually keys on, given that a UA string alone passed every fetch in this report — closeable by replaying identical requests across TLS fingerprints.
  • nba.com's own traffic: no official figure exists. The league publishes viewership and social views but no site sessions or page views, and third-party panel estimates are not treated as official here.
  • Whether any NBA entity has licensed content for AI training, and on what terms — nine crawlers are blocked and OAI-SearchBot is allowed, but no agreement has been announced either way.
