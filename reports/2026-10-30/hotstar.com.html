Context

hotstar.com is the web front of JioHotstar, India's largest streaming service. Star India launched Hotstar on Feb 11 2015; after Reliance's Viacom18 and Disney's Star India merged into JioStar in Nov 2024, Disney+ Hotstar and JioCinema were combined as JioHotstar on Feb 14 2025. The brand moved, the domain did not: jiohotstar.com 301s back to www.hotstar.com (Probe Log). Revenue is subscriptions plus advertising, driven by cricket (IPL) and Star's regional-language TV serials. For an index builder the delta is geography. The catalogue is fully enumerated in public sitemaps for India, Canada, the UK and Singapore, yet a request from any other country (the probes ran from the US, where Hotstar closed on Nov 30 2021) gets the same 9.5KB static Disney-bundle page on every path, including /in/… and /llms.txt. Page metadata is only reachable from an in-region vantage point or from archives.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where hotstar.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Key on the numeric contentId, the last integer in every URL (e.g. /shows/{slug}/{showId}/{episodeSlug}/{contentId}/watch). The same ID appears under up to four region prefixes and as both a detail page and a /watch page (MOVIE vs MOVIE_WATCH_PAGES), so one title can surface as up to eight URLs. Episode URLs embed the parent showId, which gives the show → episode join for free from the sitemap alone.

Geography is the access control

The default "logged-out crawl is unpersonalised" assumption undersells this site: outside four countries the crawl gets no catalogue at all. Every path, including India paths, returns the same static page, so a naive crawler would index 1.7M copies of a Hulu advert. Detect it by size and ETag, not status (all 200s). Region availability is itself data: 116K India-only IDs mark India-exclusive rights.

Authoritative metadata field

There is no JSON-LD, so window.APP_STATE is the record. It is richer than typical schema.org markup: licence startDate/endDate, content provider, studio, parental rating and DRM flag. Timestamps are Unix seconds set to IST midnight boundaries. Treat the subtitle-language list as a global taxonomy, not per-title availability; it includes camera-feed names such as "Birdseye" and "Insiders".

Sitemap hygiene

The indexes under-report. Parts that fell out of the index after a regeneration are still served (Probe Log), so probe N+1 past the last listed part until a 404. India lastmod values are regeneration stamps; use them for change detection, never as a publish date. feed.xml is a 388MB, heavily duplicated subset of the sitemaps and adds nothing; skip it.

Crawler policy vs edge policy

robots.txt is permissive (no AI-crawler rules) and blocks only personalised rails, search and /play/. Enforcement sits at Akamai: geo routing plus Bot Manager cookies on every HTML response. Whether named crawlers get past the geo-wall from outside the four regions is open.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the catalogue ID (film, episode, clip, highlight, replay), with shows and live channels as parent entities.
  • Primary keys & joins: contentId; showId from the episode URL; trailer ↔ title through trailerObjs; artwork by asset ID; region availability as a set-valued field from sitemap membership.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Four regional sitemap trees plus unlisted N+1 parts, daily: IDs, slugs, show joins and region sets with zero page fetches. 2) In-region (IN first) page fetch for APP_STATE, paced and only for new or changed IDs. 3) Wayback id_ captures to backfill metadata where no in-region vantage exists. 4) Artwork from img1.hotstarext.com. 5) Playback, captions, audio: partner licence only.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemaps daily, diffing ID sets to catch additions and removals; detail pages once on discovery and again at endDate; live-channel lists weekly.
  • Derivatives to generate: title and synopsis embeddings; artwork image embeddings; a rights calendar from startDate/endDate; per-title region masks.
  • Demo to build: a "where can I watch it" region map: union the four sitemap ID sets, show which titles are India-only versus global, and colour by licence expiry from sampled APP_STATE.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred Regions can vanish wholesale. Basis: /us already serves a sunset page and the sunset page still lists /id hreflang with no Indonesian sitemap (Probe Log). If CA, GB or SG close the same way, their IDs drop out of discovery; keep tombstones rather than deleting records.
  • inferred Titles leave on a schedule. Basis: per-title endDate licence windows in APP_STATE (Probe Log). An index without them will keep serving dead watch links; expire records at endDate.
  • The brand may move domains. jiohotstar.com currently redirects to hotstar.com (Probe Log); a reversal would rewrite every URL while contentId stays stable. Key on the ID, never on the URL.

  • Out-of-region requests are answered by one static object regardless of path. Basis: 6 different paths (two region roots, four title pages) returned the same AkamaiNetStorage ETag and 9,467-byte body, and /llms.txt the same size (2026-10-30).
  • Unlisted sitemap parts are leftovers from larger earlier generations. Basis: no URL in any unlisted part has a lastmod after Oct 5 2026 (most stop in 2025 – May 2026; median dates 2022–2026), whereas the listed parts' medians fall in 2026, and the numbering continues the listed series (2026-10-30).
