Context

Yahoo Finance is the largest free finance destination in the US, launched January 1997 and now owned by Apollo Global Management (90%) with Verizon holding 10% after the $5B Verizon Media sale closed September 2021. It earns money from advertising against quote pages and from three consumer subscription tiers. For an index builder the site splits cleanly in two. The news half is a syndication surface: a random sample of 12 articles returned 12 third-party providers — Motley Fool, Zacks, 24/7 Wall St., Simply Wall St. — and four of the twelve were press wires (Business Wire, PR Newswire, GlobeNewswire, CNW Group). That corpus is mostly duplicate content whose canonical home is elsewhere. The quote half is the genuinely unique asset: a global multi-asset instrument catalog spanning equities, futures, indices, mutual funds and foreign listings, keyed by exchange-suffixed ticker. inferred Yahoo treats that catalog as an SEO asset rather than a public dataset. Basis: the same history URL returns HTTP 200 with a full year of OHLCV to Googlebot, bingbot and Slurp but HTTP 404 to a Chrome user agent (Probe Log), and no official API has existed since 2017.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Yahoo Finance differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The exchange-suffixed ticker is the whole key: AAPL, CDIS.L, 0P0001HOK1.TO, ZWT=F, ^HTL, PEAI.PVT. It is stable and printed in every URL, which makes it an unusually good cross-site join. Canonicalize to the trailing-slash form, since the bare path always 301s to it.

Retrieval unit

Two units, not one. Instrument for the quote layer, with an instrument-day sub-record for price history. Article for news — but an article here is a copy, so the useful unit is the (provider, original headline) pair used for dedup rather than the Yahoo URL.

Cross-modal joins

The ticker joins everything: article bodies auto-link /quote/TICKER/, the news sitemap's news:keywords names companies, and earnings filenames embed the symbol. This is the site's real contribution — an entity-tagged news stream over a global instrument namespace.

Authoritative content field

For news, Yahoo is not the authority: JSON-LD provider names the real publisher, and a third of the sample were press releases, i.e. paid placement rather than reporting. Trust the provider field over the publisher field and over og:site_name, which both say Yahoo Finance.

Freshness & recrawl

The 3-hour news-sitemap window is the binding constraint and the tightest Site Wise has measured. No archive sitemap sits behind it, so anything missed loses its discovery path. Meanwhile the quote sitemap's lastmod has been frozen since December 2022, forcing content-hash change detection.

Quality & spam

The manipulation surface is wire-service placement rather than user spam: anyone can push a press release onto the domain with its authority attached. An index equating domain authority with content quality will systematically over-rank paid small-cap announcements.

Internal vs external search

The gated tabs mean Google's index, not the site's own visitors, is the intended reader of the fundamentals corpus — optimised for a reader allowlisted by user-agent string alone. The five hand-picked /compare?comps= URLs are the same instinct applied to comparison pages.

User query profile

Overwhelmingly navigational-by-ticker plus dated history lookups — queries with one correct answer, so namespace coverage and freshness matter far more than ranking sophistication.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: instrument (with instrument-day price sub-records) for the quote layer; article for news, carrying a provider-dedup key.
  • Primary keys & joins: exchange-suffixed ticker as the universal key; article joined to instruments via in-body /quote/TICKER/ links and news:keywords; earnings objects via symbol + fiscal period parsed from the filename.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) news-sitemap.xml polled every ≤2h — the only open, complete, legitimate feed, ~6.9K articles/day, but it yields duplicate third-party content. 2) /quote/TICKER/ root pages — 200 to normal agents and carrying the summary stats; ~590K pages, so a full sweep is the real cost driver. 3) feeds.finance.yahoo.com RSS per ticker — cheap change-detection for watchlist instruments, headlines only. 4) Robots-allowed earnings filenames for the 2025–2026 window. 5) Fundamentals and history tabs — no legitimate path; reaching them requires impersonating an allowlisted crawler, so design the index without them.
  • Recrawl cadence: news sitemap ≤2h (hard requirement); quote roots tiered by liquidity — daily for large caps, monthly for the long tail of funds and foreign listings; ignore sitemap lastmod entirely and diff on content hash.
  • Derivatives to generate: a provider-normalisation table mapping the 84 observed syndicators to canonical publishers; a press-release classifier to demote wire content; ticker-mention extraction to build the entity-tagged news graph; instrument-namespace concordance (ticker ↔ ISIN ↔ exchange) since the suffix scheme already exposes it.
  • Demo to build: a news-sitemap window monitor — poll news-sitemap.xml on a timer, persist every URL seen, and plot the eviction rate against the 3-hour window to show exactly how much of the corpus a slower crawler loses. It turns the report's central freshness claim into a live measurement.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms name this use case explicitly. Yahoo's ToS §2(c)(x) prohibits using its content to create a "database, archive… data feed… or aggregated data source that competes with or constitutes a material substitute for the Services" — a finance index is precisely that. This is a stronger, more specific bar than the generic anti-scraping clause in §2(c)(ix).
  • No machine-readable licence exists to rely on. Yahoo joined the RSL Collective when it launched in September 2025, but as of Aug 15 2026 finance.yahoo.com publishes no rsl.xml, ai.txt, llms.txt or robots License: directive (Probe Log). There is nothing to negotiate against programmatically.
  • The unauthenticated query1 endpoint is not a plan. It answers today with no key, but it is undocumented, robots-disallowed on its own host, and was already withdrawn once in 2017. Any pipeline depending on it inherits an unannounced deprecation.
  • inferred The crawler allowlist is a soft wall that will harden. Basis: gating is by user-agent string only — a Googlebot UA from an ordinary non-Google IP got 200 while Chrome got 404 on the same URL (Probe Log). Adding reverse-DNS verification is a small change that would close the fundamentals corpus completely, so treat any access obtained this way as temporary as well as non-compliant.
  • inferred Ad-funded ownership pushes content behind subscriptions, not toward licensing. Basis: three paid tiers up to $479.40/year gate 40+ years of downloadable data (Probe Log) while 44 AI and aggregator agents are blocked outright in robots.txt and no bulk path is published — the monetisation path runs through end users, not data customers.
