Context

e-Devlet Kapısı (the "e-Government Gateway") is the single sign-on front door to Turkish public services. Ministries, universities, municipalities, water utilities and some regulated private firms (electricity and gas distributors, telecoms, insurers) plug their services into it. A citizen logs in once with an e-Devlet password, mobile signature, e-signature, ID-card chip or internet banking, and then queries their own records: social-security service history, traffic fines, tax debt, court files, vehicles. It has no business model: it is state infrastructure, run by the Presidency's Cyber Security Directorate and built and operated by the state-owned Türksat A.Ş. For an index builder, nearly all of the value is personal records that must never be indexed. What can be indexed is a catalog of ~9K service descriptors (one page per service: what it does, which agency, how to authenticate), a directory of ~1.1K institutions, a small set of anonymous public-record query forms, and an unusually good official statistics series. The interesting problem is entity resolution — "which agency, which service, which URL" — and that is harder here than it looks, because the sitemap is both stale and incomplete.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where e-Devlet differs from the default platform assumptions.

Open door, empty room

Unlike most platforms profiled here, nothing is withheld from crawlers: robots.txt allows everything, every UA gets the same bytes, and pacing at 1 req/s drew no blocks. The wall is the login, not the crawl policy. The anonymous surface is a catalog about the services, and the top 10 services, which carry half of all usage, are all gated. An index answers "where do I check my traffic fines?", never "what are my fines?".

Sitemap is not the corpus

The sitemap is a poor seed in both directions: a large share of its URLs are dead municipal services, and it lists only a third of the services the category pages link (Verified Metrics). Discovery should come from the 14 category pages and the A–Z institution index, with the sitemap used only as a cross-check. The sitemap gives no lastmod, so change detection has to come from re-crawling the category pages.

Identifier & dedup

No canonical tags and no numeric service ID in the URL (suffixes like -7890 appear on only some slugs). Duplicate institution slugs exist (milli- vs mill-savunma-bakanligi). inferred The numeric suffixes are IDs from an upstream service inventory, attached when a slug was generated automatically. Basis: the suffixed slugs are the ones with dropped Turkish letters (bavurusu for "başvurusu"), a pattern consistent with machine slugging, while hand-made slugs transliterate correctly. Dedup key: (agency, normalized service title), not URL.

Retrieval unit

The service descriptor: title, agency, one-paragraph description, login methods, and whether it is single-step ("tek aşamalı") or multi-step. Gated stubs often have only the title and agency: their meta description falls back to the site-wide default. The search result line "Agency/Service" is the cleanest label pair the site emits.

Duplicated services across municipalities

Municipal services are templated: the same "Arsa Rayiç Değeri Sorgulama" (land value query) or "Borç Sorgulama" (debt query) exists once per municipality, with the municipality name prefixed to the slug and often to the title. 43% of sitemap URLs are municipal. A retrieval system should collapse them into one service type × N municipalities and route by the user's location; otherwise one query returns hundreds of near-duplicates.

User query profile

Queries are task names in Turkish ("adli sicil belgesi", "vergi borcu", "ne zaman emekli olabilirim"), often colloquial and often with the agency left implicit. On-site search answers them well and is server-rendered with facets, so it can serve as a relevance oracle: compare its ranked results with an external index for the same query.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: service descriptor (agency, title, description, auth methods, gated/open, single/multi-step, URL); institution record (name, type, website, phone, address, service list).
  • Primary keys & joins: slug (dedup by agency + normalized title); institution slug ↔ official website domain (from the institution page) to join e-Devlet services with the agency's own site; municipality ↔ province from the breadcrumb ("Aydın Belediyeleri").
  • Ingestion paths, ranked:
    1. 14 category pages (~3.9MB total) → 3,364 service slugs, then fetch each stub (~16KB). A full pass is ~7.4K pages ≈ 120MB, about 2 hours at 1 req/s (calculated by Site Wise).
    2. A–Z institution index → 1.1K institution pages; JS-render the municipal ones to recover their service lists.
    3. Sitemap as a cross-check only; drop URLs that 302 to /404.html.
    4. Statistics workbook once a year.
  • Recrawl cadence: category pages and /yeni-eklenen-hizmetler daily (cheap, catches additions); stubs monthly; institution pages quarterly; a full re-verification of live/dead status quarterly, given municipal churn.
  • Derivatives to generate: service-type clusters across municipalities; a Turkish task-phrase → service mapping (from search queries and titles); an English gloss for each service type, since the site offers no anonymous English version.
  • Demo to build: "Which service, which agency?": a Turkish/English task finder over the deduplicated catalog, returning the service type, whether it needs login, which login methods it accepts, and the user's municipality's own URL. Score it against on-site /arama results for the same queries.

Risks to the Index

  • Dead links in the seed: following the sitemap alone would publish a large share of dead links, mostly municipal. Verify status before display, and treat 302 → /404.html and the soft-404 template as "gone".
  • Personal-data boundary: anonymous forms include document verification (barcode → document) and per-person lookups. Bulk-querying them would harvest personal data under KVKK. Index the form's existence, never its results.
  • Phishing adjacency: a public index of e-Devlet service URLs is a high-value target for look-alike domains. Show only turkiye.gov.tr URLs and verify the host before display.
  • Silent re-slugging: with no canonicals and no IDs in most URLs, a renamed service looks like one deletion plus one addition. Match on agency + title to carry history across.
