Context

tv.apple.com is the web front end of the Apple TV app. It does two jobs. First, it sells and plays Apple's subscription service, which was called Apple TV+ until Apple dropped the "+" on 2025-10-13. The service carries Apple Originals, MLS and, from 2026, Formula 1 in the US. Second, it is a catalogue of third-party film and TV that points viewers to Peacock, Prime Video, Globoplay and other apps. The catalogue is far bigger than the service: in the sitemap, Originals are under 0.1% of titles and episodes (Verified Metrics). For an index builder, the site has three useful properties. One opaque ID (umc.cmc.*) holds steady across about 170 storefronts and every language. Every title page carries typed JSON-LD. And Apple's own trailers stream anonymously over HLS with WebVTT subtitles in dozens of languages. The constraints: the site's edge depends on DNS geography, its sitemaps carry no lastmod, and Apple's Media Services terms ban automated measurement and AI training.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where tv.apple.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Key everything on the umc ID. Storefront (about 170), slug language and ?l= are presentation only, so 23.1M storefront URLs collapse to about 4.3M objects. Episode URLs add ?showId= and clip URLs add targetId/targetType; strip both. showId and seasonId are on the episode record.

Retrieval unit

Visible page text is mostly shelf labels and related-title lists, so it makes poor body text. Index the JSON-LD and synopsis instead (Indexing Plan).

Cross-modal joins

umc joins page, artwork, trailer HLS (via og:video), cast (umc.cpc) and Wikidata. Through Wikidata a title reaches IMDb or TMDb IDs. That bridge is strong for Originals (58–74%) and thin for the third-party long tail (1.7–13.5%). playables[].externalId carries provider-side or iTunes IDs (e.g. 1813006619 on the iTunes channel), which is a possible join into iTunes Search.

Authoritative content field

Use JSON-LD description and name. og:title adds "Watch … - Show - Apple TV", and the meta description adds marketing copy ("winner of two Emmy® awards"). JSON-LD datePublished is the premiere date, not a page date. Do not trust the site-level sameAs to Q270285, which is the hardware.

Freshness & recrawl

With no sitemap lastmod, change detection means diffing ID sets between censuses. Provider availability moves fastest; synopses and cast barely move.

Internal vs external search

Search is excluded from crawl (*/search?) and needs the UTS API, so on-site search is not an index path. inferred The third-party catalogue exists mainly to rank in web search for "where to watch X". Basis: 143.6K third-party show pages carry indexable JSON-LD and a single Provider block, while Similarweb lists organic search as the second-largest source.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: one record per umc title. Children: episodes (for shows), availability by storefront and provider, cast, artwork, and the trailer with its subtitle tracks.
  • Primary keys & joins: umc.cmc and umc.cse as primary keys. Use umc.cpc for people and Wikidata P9586/P9751/P9750 to reach external film IDs. The storefront is a dimension, not an identity.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The sitemap index for the ID inventory: about 2.2GB gzipped per full pass, and robots-sanctioned. Sample the US storefront to keep it small. 2) HTML JSON-LD from the Akamai edge at about 1 request/s, which is enough for Originals and sports daily and the long tail monthly. Pin the rest-of-world edge, or a China-resolving fleet sees only 404s. 3) Wikidata SPARQL dumps for the external-ID bridge (CC0, free). 4) The UTS API only if a licence is agreed: it is robots-disallowed and covered by the measurement ban.
  • Recrawl cadence: sporting events and clips daily in season. Originals weekly, plus on Apple TV Press release dates. Third-party availability monthly. Full ID-set diff of the sitemaps monthly.
  • Derivatives to generate: embeddings from trailer subtitle text (multilingual, aligned by timestamp), keyframes from trailer HLS for the 3.6K Originals only, and storefront-coverage vectors per title from the hreflang sets.
  • Demo to build: a "where is this title available" matrix. For a seed set of umc IDs, read the hreflang storefront set from the sitemap and the provider from HTML, then show how availability differs by country, using only robots-allowed paths.

Risks to the Index

  • Silent geo-split edge: DNS from mainland China resolves to an edge that answers every page with an empty 404 while serving robots.txt and sitemaps normally (Probe Log). A crawler would log 23M dead URLs, not a block. Detect it by checking that a known-good page returns 200 before each crawl.
  • The contract is stricter than robots.txt: robots allows bots, AI crawlers included, on all title pages, but the Media Services terms ban automated measurement and AI training, and the website terms ban spiders. Any index needs a legal position on that conflict.
  • Sitemap cost and blindness: with no lastmod and about 165 alternates per Original URL, each full pass costs about 2.2GB. The sitemap cannot be polled incrementally.
  • Session-key API: the UTS shape (developer JWT plus a 2-hour utsk) can change without notice; the JWT in the page expires 2026-12-10. Anything built on it is brittle as well as disallowed.
  • Brand collision: "Apple TV" now names the service, the app and the hardware. Since the 2025-10-13 rebrand, entity resolution has to separate them; the site's own JSON-LD already conflates them.

Inferences

  • The China edge's empty 404 is a deliberate block of an unsupported market, not an outage. Basis: the tv-china CNAME, the UTS reply "China is not a supported storefront", and no /cn/ URL in any sitemap.
  • The third-party catalogue is built for search acquisition (Retrieval Notes). Basis: robots-allowed pages with JSON-LD and a provider for 143.6K non-Apple shows, plus organic search as Similarweb's second source.
