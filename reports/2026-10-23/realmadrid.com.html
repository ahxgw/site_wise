Context

realmadrid.com is the official site of Real Madrid C.F., the member-owned Madrid club that runs men's and women's football, a basketball section and a large academy. The site publishes club news (about a dozen Spanish articles a day), fixtures and match reports, squad and academy rosters, a legends archive, the fan-club (peña) directory, and sells stadium tours and VIP hospitality. Tickets, the shop, membership and the RM Play video service live on separate subdomains. The front end is Angular 21 with server-side rendering. Content is authored in Adobe Experience Manager as a Cloud Service and served as JSON through persisted GraphQL queries on publish.realmadrid.com, which answer anonymously. For an index builder the site is the authoritative source for one club's sports entities: every match and player record carries Opta IDs, so it joins cleanly to league and stats data. It also holds a 2021-onward news archive with downloadable original video. Unlike most large sites logged so far, robots.txt does not block AI crawlers.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where realmadrid.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

The API is the crawl target

Every page is an Angular shell plus a transfer-state copy of AEM JSON. Fetching the persisted query directly yields the same record at a fraction of the bytes (4.4KB vs 91.7KB for the sampled article) and without the 1.8MB home-page state. publish.realmadrid.com/robots.txt explicitly allows *.json*, while the www robots.txt disallows /*.json$; the API host is the one whose policy applies.

Identifier & dedup

The slug is the cross-locale key for matches, players and legends: the sitemap lists the same slug under seven translated paths, tied together by hreflang. News is different. Translations get their own slugs and records, and none of the 40 sampled records link a translation, so cross-language news dedup needs content matching. Each news record has a stable AEM _path that encodes the creation date (/news/generic/2022/02/22/…); use it as the primary key. Drop the 376 section-less slugs, which are soft-404s.

Cross-modal joins

Matches are the hub. One match record links the Opta match UUID and legacy ID, the LaLiga/AVET fixture code, the venue and competition (Opta IDs), scorers, broadcaster, and the slug of its match-report article. Players carry optaCode. That makes the site joinable to any Opta-licensed stats feed and to league sites without fuzzy name matching.

Authoritative content field

Use bodyList[].html and date from the API. The JSON-LD description and meta description are the title plus a fixed SEO sentence. The sitemap lastmod on news is edit time (a 2022 article shows 2024-03-20); date is publication.

An unusually open crawl policy

No named-bot groups, no edge UA filtering, and a max-image-preview:large robots meta. inferred The club treats reach as more valuable than control over AI reuse of its content. Basis: the 324-byte robots.txt with no AI-bot groups, the byte-identical response to GPTBot and ClaudeBot, and the Allow: *.mp4* line on the media host.

Spanish is the master corpus

es-ES has 2.2× the news of en-US and about 4.4× the average of the other five locales (calculated by Site Wise). Fan clubs, most legends and nearly all academy rosters exist only in Spanish. An English-only crawl misses most of the corpus.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the news article (body, byline, tags, gallery, video) and the match record (teams, score, scorers, report link). Player and legend entities are attached by slug and Opta code.
  • Primary keys & joins: AEM _path for news; slug + Opta UUID for matches; optaCode for players; match relatedNews.slug → article; hreflang groups → locale variants for entities.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked:
    1. Sitemaps (77K URLs, 114 files, 97MB uncompressed) for discovery, then persisted queries per slug: ~77K API calls at ~4–15KB each, roughly 1GB (calculated by Site Wise). Open, cacheable, and robots-permitted on the API host.
    2. diaryV2 with rolling 90-day windows to sweep fixtures and results, including academy matches.
    3. HTML pages only for JSON-LD validation and as a fallback if the API closes.
  • Recrawl cadence: latest-news sitemaps hourly; diaryV2 on match days; news and entity sitemaps daily, diffing URL sets; legends monthly.
  • Derivatives to generate: ASR on news video (no transcripts or captions exist in the records); keyframes from the MP4s, with a size cap given 300MB+ masters; OCR is not needed.
  • Demo to build: a match timeline. For one season, join diaryV2 results to their match reports and video, keyed on Opta IDs, and answer "every Mbappé goal this season with the clip".

Risks to the Index

  • Exposed credentials may trigger lockdown: the bundle ships an APIM key and a secret-shaped value for api-narm. If the club rotates them and hardens the front end, the anonymous GraphQL host could be put behind the same gateway. Keep the HTML transfer-state path, which carries identical JSON, as the fallback.
  • Persisted-query names are versioned: …V2 suffixes and coexisting diary/diaryV2 show queries get replaced. Re-read the bundle for query names on each deploy rather than hard-coding them.
  • Media rights: match footage is LaLiga/UEFA broadcast territory. Open MP4 URLs are not a license; restrict video use to ASR text and links.
  • Sitemap quality: soft-404 slugs and fixture lastmods set at schedule load mean the sitemap cannot be trusted as a change feed. Diff API records instead.
