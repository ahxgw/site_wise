Context

zillow.com is the largest US home-search portal and is run by Zillow Group (NASDAQ: Z), which also owns Trulia, StreetEasy and HotPads. Revenue comes mostly from agents who buy buyer leads (Premier Agent), plus rentals advertising and Zillow Home Loans. For an index builder Zillow is unusual because it publishes a page for nearly every US housing unit, not just active listings. Each home page (HDP) is keyed by an integer zpid and carries a parcel ID, geo coordinates, the Zestimate, price and tax history, MLS attribution and up to dozens of photos. The site's own sitemaps list ~118M off-market homes beside ~2.8M active listings. Discovery is wide open, but content is not. Every HTML page goes through a PerimeterX check at the CloudFront edge, and the Terms of Use ban automated queries outright. The one licensed bulk path is aggregate market data, which Zillow publishes as CSVs with an attribution requirement. Listing-level data reaches third parties only through Zillow's approval-gated Bridge API. In Q3 2026 Zillow tightened how it excludes bots from its reported visits, recasting prior figures down ~5%.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where zillow.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The zpid is the key; the address slug before it is cosmetic and changes on re-geocoding. A zpid persists across status changes (sale → off-market → rent), so a home appears in different sitemap families over time; dedupe on zpid, not URL. Buildings (/b/…-{6-char id}/) and communities (/apartments/…/{6-char id}/) use short base-62 IDs instead. Store zpid as int64: the highest sampled value sits 0.02% below the int32 ceiling (Probe Log).

Sitemaps as a status ledger

The family a zpid sits in is its listing status, and the families are rebuilt daily. Diffing the family memberships each day yields a free national feed of new listings, pendings and sales at address level, without fetching any HTML. The off-market family is what makes the index a near-census of the US housing stock rather than of listings.

Cross-modal joins

parcelId plus county joins a home to assessor records. mlsid plus the MLS name in priceHistory.source joins it to the originating MLS listing. geo joins it to parcels and OSM buildings. Photo hashes are content-addressed, so the same image reused across relistings dedupes by URL.

Authoritative content field

Use the __NEXT_DATA__ property object, not JSON-LD. The JSON-LD drops history, Zestimate and attribution, and its BreadcrumbList IDs point at zillow.co. Listing facts are MLS-sourced and secondhand. When Zillow and the MLS disagree, the MLS wins, and attributionInfo.lastChecked dates Zillow's copy.

Bot-specific render

The archived HDP's data cache is keyed ForSaleBotsQuery, so crawlers that get through receive a distinct server-side query variant. Wayback captures show that variant and are the only open sample of the full payload.

Freshness & recrawl

Active-family lastmod is minute-precise and real: latest files cover only the last ~2.5 days. Off-market parts carry no lastmod, so recrawl them only on status-family transitions. Zestimates change without any lastmod signal.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the home (zpid), with address, geo, parcel, status and status date from sitemaps. Listing text, facts, photos and Zestimate attach only under a licence.
  • Primary keys & joins: zpid; parcelId + county FIPS to assessor data; mlsid + MLS to listing feeds; geo to parcels; region IDs to research CSVs.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Sitemaps: ~5.6K child files (~3.2GB gzip, 2.6GB of it the 5,000 off-market parts; calculated by Site Wise from sampled sizes) give the national address and status ledger, and the active families alone are ~0.4GB/day. Robots.txt allows this path; storing it is a ToS question to clear first. 2) Research CSVs, monthly, licensed with attribution. 3) Bridge API for listing content and Zestimates, if Zillow approves the use case. 4) MLS/IDX feeds directly, where the index can qualify, as the upstream of most listing content.
  • Recrawl cadence: latest files every 6–12h; full active families daily; off-market and /b/ monthly; robots.txt weekly, given the frequent named-bot changes.
  • Derivatives to generate: a status-transition feed (listing → pending → sold) from daily sitemap diffs; address geocodes joined to parcels. No photo embeddings without rights.
  • Demo to build: a daily US "new listings" counter by state and ZIP, built purely from diffs of the for-sale-by-agent and recently-sold latest files, validated against the research CSV inventory series.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The sitemap side door may close. Basis: sitemaps are served from S3 and skip the PX check that guards every HTML path (Probe Log). Zillow's Q3 2026 visit methodology now hunts automated traffic "designed to resemble consumer activity" (10-Q). A one-line CloudFront behaviour change would gate them too.
  • inferred robots.txt does not predict access. Basis: ClaudeBot's group allows homedetails, yet a declared research UA gets 403 on the first HDP request while robots.txt and sitemaps return 200 (Probe Log). Access is decided by PX scoring, not by robots rules.
  • inferred zpid width may change. Basis: the highest sampled zpid is within 484K of 231−1, and ~20K listing URLs change daily (Probe Log). Pipelines typed as int32 will break if Zillow keeps allocating upward.
