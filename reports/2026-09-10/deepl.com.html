Context

DeepL translates text, documents, speech and images. It is the corporate continuation of Linguee, the bilingual concordance engine Gereon Frahling and Leonard Fink launched in 2009: Linguee spent a decade crawling the web for professionally translated document pairs, and in 2017 a team under Jarosław Kutyłowski trained a neural translator on that corpus and renamed the company. Revenue is subscriptions plus a character-metered API. For an index builder this is the sharpest surface versus corpus split Site Wise has measured. The surface — deepl.com — is a text box whose output is generated per request, never given a URL, never published; its 27K sitemap URLs are one marketing template fanned across 19 locales and 961 language pairs, differing by two words per page. The corpus is on the other host: linguee.com publishes 74.5M pages of human-translated sentence pairs, each linking to the document it came from — and blocks every crawler except six named search engines, under an explicit litigation threat and an admission that the data is deliberately salted.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Identifier & dedup

The tdlp tree needs collapsing before ingest, not after: 20,026 URLs carry one document. With no hreflang and no cross-locale rel=canonical, nothing in the markup says the variants are related — the only signal is the URL grammar /{locale}/translator/l/{src}/{tgt}. Linguee keys cleanly by contrast: /{pair}/translation/{headword}.html, headword percent-encoded with + for spaces, treating multi-word phrases ("einverstanden sein mit") as first-class entries.

Retrieval unit

On deepl.com the useful unit is the document, and there are about 1,600 of them — the page is the wrong unit by a factor of 17. On Linguee the useful unit is not the page but the sentence pair: ~30 per entry, each with its own source attribution. Indexing Linguee at page granularity throws away the alignment that is the entire point of the corpus.

Cross-modal joins

The join that matters runs outward. Each Linguee sentence pair carries a source_url to the document it came from — eur-lex, europarl, espacenet, corporate annual reports. That key joins a sentence pair back to a full document you may already hold under a permissive licence: a different and more durable asset than the Linguee page.

Authoritative content field

On the pair pages, <title> and og:title are the only fields carrying page-specific information; the body is constant. Treating body text as the signal here produces 20,026 identical documents. On Linguee, trust the aligned sentence left/sentence right2 cells over the summary gloss.

Quality & spam

The manipulation risk here is inverted: the publisher, not a spammer, states that the corpus is deliberately salted with reworded and wholly fabricated entries as copy-detection canaries (Probe Log). Any pipeline that ingests Linguee inherits both the poison and the fingerprint that proves where it came from.

Encoding

Linguee serves charset="iso-8859-15", not UTF-8. A pipeline that assumes UTF-8 will mojibake German umlauts and the euro sign across 74.5M pages, silently and consistently. Decode against the declared charset per response.

Query profile & on-site search

Two populations that never overlap. DeepL takes a paste-in text and returns a transform — no on-site search, and no query log a retrieval system could serve. Linguee is a search engine, answering word-and-phrase lookups with evidence of how professionals translated them in context; that long tail is why its URL count is five orders of magnitude larger. Its /sitemap and /top allowlist shows it is built to be found through Google, not browsed.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the document on deepl.com (~1,600, after collapsing the locale cross-product); the sentence pair on linguee.com, if and only if a licence is obtained.
  • Primary keys & joins: {locale, path} with locale stripped for dedup on the shell; {pair, headword, pair_index} on Linguee, joined outward to the source corpus by source_url.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) developers.deepl.com/llms.txt → 143 .md documents — unrestricted, dated, machine-formatted, and the only surface here that repays crawling. 2) The English nx subtree, 593 documents, skipping the other 18 locales. 3) The tdlp tree reduced to one template plus a 961-row pair table extracted from the URLs — do not fetch 20,026 pages to learn two words each. 4) Linguee: no legitimate bulk path exists; the only route is a negotiated licence, and the terms rule out the training use that would motivate one.
  • Recrawl cadence: changelog weekly (it moves — three entries in the first eight days of September 2026); marketing pages quarterly; the pair-page template on change-detection only. Nothing on the estate carries a usable lastmod, so cadence must be policy, not signal.
  • Derivatives to generate: none from the shell — there is no media and no long-form text worth embedding. The one derivative worth building is a capability table: 125 languages × 6 features, parsed from the language data and diffed on each crawl, which turns an undated marketing claim into a dated time series.
  • Demo to build: the surface-versus-corpus contrast on one page. Left: the 27,064-URL sitemap collapsing to ~1,600 documents, with the en/de vs en/fr diff highlighting its two changed words. Right: the 74.5M-URL Linguee census by pair, greyed out, annotated with the wildcard Disallow. One screen showing that this estate's crawlable half is empty and its full half is closed.

Risks to the Index

  • Poisoned corpus with attribution built in. Linguee's robots.txt states the data contains fabricated entries designed to identify copied material. Ingesting it risks both factual corruption and a provable provenance trail — the rare case where the defensive measure is disclosed in advance and still binding.
  • The shell's openness is worth nothing, and could still close. DeepL names no AI crawler, which reads as permissive — but the permissive surface is 1,600 marketing documents, and x-verified-bot: false shows the edge already classifies clients (Probe Log). The enforcement mechanism is in place ahead of any policy that would use it.
  • Two hosts, two postures, one owner. Any crawl policy inferred from deepl.com is wrong for linguee.com, and they share an origin IP. Estate-level rules keyed on the registrable domain will get this backwards in both directions.
  • Documentation drift is the real freshness risk. The API is the product, the docs are the only dated surface, and the changelog shows v2→v3 migrations in progress (languages, translation memories). A stale docs index misdescribes a live API rather than merely aging.
  • Empty declared surfaces. 42 Danish sitemaps advertise zero URLs (Probe Log). A crawler that trusts sitemap declarations as a coverage estimate will over-count this corpus; only fetching the shards reveals it.

Inferences

  • The SEO surface reflects a superseded product generation. Basis: the tdlp tree covers 961 directed pairs over 32 languages, while the API language table lists 125 (Verified Metrics) — the landing pages were generated against the pre-expansion language set and have not been regenerated inferred
  • Pair-specific content is rendered client-side or not at all, so a non-executing crawler sees one document 20,026 times. Basis: both sampled pair pages returned 285,877-byte bodies differing only in the title, with x-nextjs-prerender: 1 indicating a single prerendered shell (Probe Log) inferred
  • Linguee is maintained as a static asset rather than developed. Basis: Apache origin with no CDN, ISO-8859-15 encoding, zero structured data, a dead api.linguee.com, and 42 Danish sitemaps declared but empty (Probe Log) inferred
  • Linguee's sitemap lastmod values are generated at request time and carry no editorial signal. Basis: all 8,940 children returned lastmod 2026-09-10, the fetch date, on a corpus whose German-English half alone holds 11.2M pages (Probe Log) inferred
  • DeepL treats its documentation, but not its product, as a corpus for machine consumption. Basis: developers.deepl.com/llms.txt returns 200 with a 143-entry index while deepl.com/llms.txt 404s and every API read requires a key (Probe Log) inferred

Open Questions

  • How many DeepL users and paying subscribers are there? The JSON-LD claim ("millions of individuals, over 200,000 business teams") is undated and unaudited. Closed by a dated first-party disclosure or a filing for DeepL SE in the German Unternehmensregister.
  • How many distinct sentence pairs does Linguee hold, as opposed to pages? Closed by sampling N entry pages per language pair, counting sentence left rows, and multiplying by the per-pair page census in Verified Metrics — the one measurement that would turn 74.5M pages into a corpus size.
  • What is Linguee's real edit and growth rate? The sitemap lastmod is useless (see Inferences). Closed by hashing a fixed sample of entry pages weekly over a quarter.
  • What fraction of Linguee's attributed sources are themselves openly licensed? EU institutional domains led the one page sampled (Verified Metrics). Closed by extracting source_url across a stratified sample and classifying domains by licence — it would size the portion of this corpus obtainable legitimately from the original publishers instead.
  • Which model version served a given API response? No version header is exposed and the changelog does not map dates to model builds. Closed by a documented model-version field, or by a fixed probe set translated on a schedule to detect output drift.
