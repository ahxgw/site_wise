Context

amazon.co.uk is Amazon's British storefront. It went live on 15 October 1998, the same morning as amazon.de, replacing Bookpages Ltd, which Amazon had bought that April. Measured by sales attributed to a country, the UK is now Amazon's third-largest market after the US and Germany, and it grew faster than Germany in 2025 (Verified Metrics). Ireland used to shop here until amazon.ie opened in March 2025. That store reuses the UK's ASINs and robots.txt byte for byte but keeps a separate review pool (Probe Log). For an index builder, the UK is the most regulated Amazon storefront profiled so far, with three overlapping constraints. CMA commitments bind the Buy Box until June 2028. CMA undertakings bind review and rating integrity. Two certified class actions target the Buy Box. On the technical side it is stricter than amazon.de: search is WAF-challenged, there are no alternate-language trees, and a client outside the UK sees no featured offer at all.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where amazon.co.uk differs from the default assumptions and from the amazon.de profile.

Delivery context gates the offer, not just the currency

On .de a US exit still got a Buy Box price, converted to USD. On .co.uk the same setup gets no featured offer at all, even with a GBP cookie, because the selected delivery country cannot be served. A crawler outside the UK therefore records "unavailable" for in-stock items. Fetch with a UK delivery context, and store countryCode with every offer record so availability can be trusted.

Search is not a seed surface

/s?k= is robots-allowed but WAF-challenged, so it yields no ASINs to plain HTTP (Probe Log). Seed discovery from /gp/bestsellers, which is open and returned 36 ASINs, and from the Creators API's 40 search indexes. Do not count on search result pages, which .de still served.

One ASIN, two stores, two review pools

amazon.ie shares the ASIN, the robots.txt and the CDN, but showed a quarter of the UK rating count on the same yarn and no review bodies. Key the product on (marketplace, ASIN) and keep the review counts per store. Merging .ie into .co.uk records would overstate .ie ratings fourfold on that ASIN.

Review provenance spans the globe

The foreign reviews on .de pages were EU/EMEA. On .co.uk they also come from the US, Mexico, Australia and Singapore, and some are machine-translated ("Translated by Amazon"). Key each review on its source marketplace, and keep a translated flag so the lexical index does not treat Amazon's translations as UK-authored text.

Velocity on a weekly window

The UK badge counts purchases in the past week, where .de counts the past month. Pan-European velocity features need the window normalised, or UK items look four times slower than German ones at equal sales. Recrawl the badge weekly to match its window.

Lean pages, same fields

UK detail pages are about 0.4–0.5MB against .de's 2.6–3.0MB, yet they carry the same title, rating, review, image and HLS fields. Fetch cost per ASIN is roughly 6× lower here (calculated by Site Wise from the Probe Log sizes), but the captcha budget, not bandwidth, is what binds.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: product = (UK, ASIN). Reviews are child documents keyed (source marketplace, review ID) with a translated flag. Offers are sub-records keyed (ASIN, seller, delivery country).
  • Join keys: the ASIN from the path, never the slugged canonical; (IE, ASIN) as a sibling record, not a duplicate; media ID for images; manifest URL for video.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Creators API for catalogue, browse nodes and GBP offers. It is the only surface that covers search-style discovery, since /s is challenged. 2) A slow distributed crawl of /dp/ from UK exits, seeded from bestsellers and API results, for review bodies, HLS and velocity badges. 3) amazon.ie as a separate low-priority store. Full review history: no bulk path.
  • Recrawl cadence: offers daily via the API. Velocity badges weekly, matching their window. Titles and media weekly. Reviews monthly, plus an extra sweep after CMA enforcement news, since deletions arrive in batches.
  • Derivatives: a DOM attribute extractor (no JSON-LD), review language ID and translation flags, HLS keyframe and ASR extraction, and a UK↔IE rating-pool concordance.
  • Demo to build: a "featured offer by delivery country" probe. Fetch one ASIN with a UK, an IE and a US delivery context and show which fields disappear (featured offer, availability) and which persist (rating, reviews, media). It reuses the .de four-context diff and adds the delivery axis.

Risks to the Index

  • Buy Box behaviour is under three constraints with different clocks. The CMA commitments expire on 2028-06-20, the class-action trial date is open (see Open Questions), and any DMCC strategic-market-status designation would add a fourth regime. A Buy Box-winner time series must record which regime applied at each sample date (CMA decision).
  • inferred Review counts can fall without notice. Basis: the 2025-06-06 undertakings let Amazon delete all past reviews of a banned reviewer and remove hijacked reviews from listings (CMA). A drop in rating count between crawls is therefore an enforcement signal, not a parsing error, and should be logged rather than smoothed over.
  • inferred The UK crawl budget is tighter than Germany's. Basis: the captcha arrived by request 11 here against ~14 on .de, under similar pacing, and search was challenged outright (Probe Log). A single-IP crawl of the UK store is impractical even at modest scale.
  • The in-page API pointer is still dead. As on .de, Amazon's error pages still route automated clients to PA-API, which was retired in favour of the Creators API (notice).
