Context

pixiv is Japan's dominant illustration-sharing community, launched September 10 2007 and run by the privately held pixiv Inc. (Sendagaya, Tokyo). It earns from the pixiv Premium subscription, advertising, and adjacent properties — FANBOX, BOOTH, Sketch, Comic. For an index builder it is the mirror image of a walled platform: the corpus is enormous, media-dense, richly tagged, and nearly all of it answers to an undocumented but entirely unauthenticated JSON API. The obstacle is not a login wall or a quota but Article 14 of the Terms of Use. pixiv is also the clearest case yet of a corpus that changed species underneath its own URL space: inferred most new posts are now machine-generated. Basis: a random-ID census on 2026-07-28 returned aiType=2 for 57.4% of works created Jan–Jul 2026 against 0% for a 2021 control cohort (Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where pixiv departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Three disjoint integer ID spaces — illustrations (~147.6M), novels (~27M), users — none namespaced. "ID 27000000" is meaningless without its type, and the official 160M-works figure is the sum across spaces, which is why it exceeds the highest illustration ID. Locale is a URL prefix, not an object: /artworks/{id} and /en/artworks/{id} are one work, and extraData.meta.canonical always names the bare Japanese form.

Retrieval unit

The work, not the image. Half of recent posts are multi-page (up to 78 images) and pages share one title, one tag set, one caption — a page-level index shatters that context and returns 78 near-duplicates for one query. Index the work; treat pages as sub-records keyed {id}_p{n}.

Cross-modal joins

The image URL is itself a join key: i.pximg.net/img-original/img/YYYY/MM/DD/HH/MM/SS/{id}_p{n}.jpg encodes work ID, page index, and upload time to the second. A pixiv image found anywhere on the web resolves back to its work and creator from the filename alone, with no lookup — unusually generous provenance infrastructure.

Authoritative content field

Tags beat titles decisively. Titles are frequently emoji, punctuation, or blank; the tag list is the semantic payload, and per-tag userId attribution plus the locked flag lets you weight author-applied tags above community ones. The first two tags are typically franchise and character — an entity pair, free.

Freshness & recrawl

Deletion, not mutation, is the dominant change event, and the rate is severe: 15% of works vanish within seven months and ~39% within five years (Verified Metrics). A liveness sweep is a first-class job here, not an afterthought — a five-year-old crawl is nearly 40% tombstones.

Quality & spam

The manipulation surface is inverted. Little classic SEO spam — pixiv barely competes in web search — but enormous volume pressure from AI generators, which pixiv itself throttled by limiting rapid multi-posting after its May 2023 announcement. The practical filter is aiType plus the ranking endpoint, not a spam classifier.

Internal vs external search

A near-closed search economy: no sitemap, no JSON-LD, and an artwork page that 302s a plain crawler. Discovery happens on-site through tag search, and pixiv's own search API beats anything an external engine offers — exact tag totals, AI filtering, 60 results a page, no auth. External site: queries are not a meaningful escape hatch.

User query profile

Japanese-language entity lookups — franchise plus character plus situation, composed from the tag vocabulary rather than natural language. Half of active users are non-Japanese (Verified Metrics), which makes tag translation the highest-leverage derivative: demand is cross-lingual, the index is not.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the work (illustration, manga, ugoira, or novel), with pages, comments, and extracted entities as sub-records.
  • Primary keys & joins: (type, id) — never a bare integer. Creator on userId; series on seriesNavData; media on the timestamped i.pximg.net path; entities on the tag vocabulary, normalized against dic.pixiv.net.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A licensing conversation with pixiv Inc. — the only path that survives Article 14, and the one to open first, because everything below is technically trivial and contractually exposed. 2) Ranking + search endpoints — unauthenticated, cheap, self-limiting in volume; yields the curated ~0%-AI surface and exact tag counters, the right corpus for most retrieval products anyway. 3) Creator-scoped enumeration via /ajax/user/{id}/profile/all driven by opt-in creator consent — coverage without a blanket sweep. 4) Full ID enumeration — unobstructed at ~1.3 req/s with no throttle, and the path the ToS most directly prohibits. Ranked last on purpose.
  • Recrawl cadence: metadata weekly for works under 30 days old (counts still moving); liveness-only checks quarterly after that; never re-fetch image bytes, which are immutable. Budget the sweep against 15%/7-month attrition.
  • Derivatives to generate: tag translation JA→EN first — it addresses the ~50% non-Japanese active base against a Japanese-only vocabulary. Then CLIP-style image embeddings for visual similarity, character/franchise entity resolution from the tag pair, and an AI-provenance field carried through from aiType rather than re-predicted.
  • Demo to build: a two-corpus comparator. Ingest the same 500 works twice — once from the daily ranking, once by random ID sampling — and show them side by side: 0% vs 57% AI-flagged, 0% vs 48% R-18, a curated head against a median-852-view long tail. The demo's argument is that on pixiv, choosing an ingestion path is choosing a corpus, and no downstream ranking undoes that choice.

Risks to the Index

  • Article 14 is the whole story. The Master Terms of Use (revised June 22 2026) prohibit "use of crawlers or other programs to aggregate works," and item 4 separately covers conduct prejudicing posters' interests "by using the results of data analysis (including the conduct of learning to develop artificial intelligence)." pixiv's May 9 2023 announcement confirms it enforces this against scraping specifically, and those countermeasures were built under creator pressure — so the enforcement risk is social as much as legal, and creators can delete, which the 39% five-year attrition shows they do. An index on the open API has no technical problem and a standing legal one.
  • Undocumented means unstable. /ajax/* is pixiv's own front-end transport, not a published product: no version, no deprecation policy, no contract. A front-end refactor can rename 57 fields without notice, and there is no changelog to watch.
  • inferred The AI-crawler block and the open API are on a collision course. Basis: robots.txt names 12 AI crawler UAs under Disallow: / while /ajax/illust/ served ~400 unauthenticated calls with no 429 or challenge — a stated policy the infrastructure does not enforce. Closing that gap (keys, signed requests, Cloudflare challenges on /ajax/) is the single change that would most damage a pixiv index, and Cloudflare already fronts the whole site.
  • Nearly half the corpus is adult content and it is not gated on the API. xRestrict=1 works return in full unauthenticated even though mode=daily_r18 is 403 — so a naive crawl ingests ~48% R-18 by default. Carry xRestrict/sl through ingestion as hard filters; in jurisdictions with age-verification duties this is compliance, not preference.
  • Composition drifts fast enough to invalidate a stale index. AI-flagged share went 0%→57.4% in four years and R-18 share roughly doubled (Verified Metrics). A ranker tuned on a 2022 pixiv crawl is not tuned on today's pixiv.

Inferences

  • Most new pixiv posts are machine-generated. Basis: aiType=2 on 57.4% of 204 resolvable works from a random Jan–Jul 2026 ID sample, against 0% of 73 works in a 2021 control where the field is unset — the flag is populated, not noisy.
  • Ranking and raw ID enumeration yield materially different corpora. Basis: 0/50 AI-flagged and 0/50 R-18 in the daily ranking versus 57.4% and 48.0% in the random sample, measured the same day. pixiv runs a separate AI ranking, so the SFW ranking's 0% is partly by construction — which is the point: it is a filtered surface, not a sample.
  • The AI-crawler block and the open API contradict each other. Basis: 12 AI UAs under Disallow: / in robots.txt while /ajax/illust/ served ~400 unauthenticated calls without one 429 or challenge.
  • Structured metadata is deliberately API-side, not HTML-side. Basis: a Googlebot-UA render of an artwork page contains zero JSON-LD blocks, while extraData.meta in the JSON response carries the canonical URL, hreflang alternates, and full OGP payload.

Open Questions

  • Total image count across the corpus — the recent cohort averages 5.33 images per work but the 2021 cohort averages 2.37, so extrapolating either over 125M live works gives a 300M–670M band too wide to publish. Closeable by stratifying the census by ID decile and summing pageCount per stratum.
  • pixiv Inc. revenue, profitability, and Premium subscriber count — the company is private and files nothing public; only unreliable aggregator estimates exist. Needs a company disclosure or a Japanese corporate filing.
  • Monthly active users, versus the 119M registered figure — pixiv publishes registrations only. Needs an official MAU disclosure, or a third-party panel with its estimator named.
  • Whether the unresolvable 15%/39% are deletions, privatizations, unlisted conversions, or suspensions — the endpoint returns an undifferentiated error for all four. Closeable by comparing against isUnlisted on known-unlisted works, or by watching a fixed ID cohort weekly and recording transitions.
  • Whether sl (observed values 2, 4, 6) is a finer sensitivity scale than xRestrict, and how the two interact — the census found sl=6 on more works than xRestrict=1. Needs pixiv documentation or a labelled sample.
  • Whether pixiv offers any licensed bulk or partner feed — no developer portal, API documentation, or partner programme was found. Closeable only by a direct enquiry to pixiv Inc.
