Context

Genius began as Rap Exegesis in August 2009, became Rap Genius that December, and dropped the "Rap" in July 2014 when it expanded past hip-hop. Its premise was never the lyrics — it was the annotation: a crowd-written note bolted to a specific lyric fragment. The lyrics themselves are borrowed. Genius holds no copyright in them; it displays them under licenses obtained from Sony/ATV in 2013 and the NMPA's publisher members in 2014, a distinction the Second Circuit relied on when it killed Genius's $50M suit against Google. MediaLab bought the company for $80M in September 2021 and runs it on advertising. For an index builder that inversion is the whole story: the modality that draws the traffic is the one Genius cannot sublicense, while the layer it can speak for — annotations, credits, sample lineage, per-song popularity — is exposed through a rich unauthenticated web API that robots.txt formally disallows. inferred Genius defends its corpus contractually rather than technically. Basis: identical page bytes were served to curl, a Googlebot UA, and a Chrome UA (Probe Log), while the Terms add an explicit AI/ML prohibition.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Genius differs from the default platform assumptions.

No two-track surface

The methodology assumes a curated SEO sliver for approved crawlers plus a metered API for everyone else. Genius has neither wall: curl and Googlebot get byte-identical lyrics pages, and the whole web API answers without a token. The gate is the Terms and robots.txt, not the server — and that gate is exactly what failed against Google, where the Second Circuit held a contract claim over copied lyrics preempted by the Copyright Act.

Discovery has no map

No sitemap, no Sitemap: directive, no llms.txt, and /search?* disallowed. Enumeration options are the dense integer ID space (75% live, so a full sweep costs ~14M requests for ~10.6M objects) or artist-first traversal via /artists/:id/songs. There is no cheap incremental feed.

Retrieval unit is the referent

The useful unit is not the page but the referent — a lyric fragment with annotations attached, carrying its own ID, URL and embed. It is the only object where Genius's licensed text and its own commentary ship together with attribution, and so the one lyric-bearing thing a third party can legitimately display.

The knowledge layer is a thin head

The median song has zero annotations, zero comments and no description (Verified Metrics). Ranking on "Genius has an explanation" would leave two-thirds of the corpus untouched; the fields that hold across the whole corpus are the boring ones — tag, language, lyrics state.

Popularity is published

stats.pageviews exposes a real per-song traffic count — but only on the head. inferred The field is populated for popular songs only. Basis: 15 of 150 sampled songs returned a non-null pageviews, median 0 across the sample, while the hand-picked hit returned 3.6M (Probe Log).

Freshness is self-reported

Every song object carries lyrics_updated_at and updated_by_human_at. An index can schedule recrawl off these fields instead of polling — rare among the sites profiled here, and the reason a 10M-object corpus stays affordable.

Translations are separate objects

Localized lyrics live as their own song records linked by translation_songs / translation_of (4% of sampled songs). Collapse them onto the source song or they compete with it in retrieval. The 34% non-English share is mostly genuine catalog, not translation duplicates.

Trust tiers are exposed

Annotation quality is legible without modeling it: state (accepted / unreviewed), verified, cosigned_by, votes_total, plus song-level lyrics_verified and lyrics_marked_staff_approved_by. Artist-verified annotations are the top tier and are flagged as such.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the referent (lyric fragment + its annotations) for the annotated head; the song entity record for the whole corpus. Do not index full lyric text — it is the one thing Genius cannot grant.
  • Primary keys & joins: numeric song_id is the stable key (URL slugs are derived from artist+title and move on retitle). Join outward on apple_music_id (63% coverage) and youtube_url (59%); ignore spotify_uuid at 1%. Join inward on referent.song_id, artist_id, album_id, and the 12 song_relationships edges.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Official OAuth API for entities, credits and referents — the only licensable path, and the one to put a commercial agreement behind. 2) /artists/:id/songs traversal from a seed artist list, since no sitemap exists. 3) Per-song embed (embed.js) for any surface that must show lyric text at all. 4) ID enumeration over the web API — technically open and unmetered, but robots-disallowed and Terms-barred; treat as a measurement tool, not an ingestion path.
  • Recrawl cadence: driven by updated_by_human_at, not a calendar. Songs released in the last 90 days weekly; annotated head monthly; the ~70% unannotated tail on change-detection only.
  • Derivatives to generate: a sample/interpolation graph from song_relationships (15% of songs, and the highest-value structure on the site); a credits table keyed on artist ID from writer/producer/custom_performances; annotation embeddings for "explain this line" retrieval. Skip ASR and OCR — Genius hosts no audio and its images are cover art.
  • Demo to build: a licensed-corpus indexer that ingests song entities and referents while refusing to store lyric bodies, then answers "what does this line mean" by returning the annotation plus the official embed — showing exactly how much retrieval quality survives when the primary text is off-limits.

Risks to the Index

  • The corpus is sub-licensed, so the ceiling is not Genius's to raise. Publishers grant Genius display rights; a commercial API deal cannot convey more than Genius itself holds, and publisher-level takedowns can remove songs without warning.
  • The open web API is an accident waiting to be closed. robots.txt already disallows /api/* and the Terms already forbid the extraction; only enforcement is missing. Any pipeline depending on unauthenticated genius.com/api/* should be considered temporary.
  • inferred AI use is contractually excluded, not merely discouraged. Basis: the Terms bar copying "including for any purposes related to AI machine learning/use on AI platforms" and separately bar "data mining, robots, scraping"; robots.txt sets 21 AI agents to Disallow: / (Probe Log).
  • inferred Traffic concentration in Google is a single point of failure for corpus liveness. Basis: 78.4% of visits arrive from organic search (Verified Metrics) while contribution — annotations, transcriptions, comments — is volunteer labor; an AI-answer shift that removes the click removes the contributor.
  • Metadata is thinner than the API's field list implies. A 70-field song object suggests rich structure; measured per-field coverage (Verified Metrics) runs from 100% down to 1%. Budget from measured coverage, not from the schema.

Open Questions

  • What does a commercial Genius API license actually convey, and at what price? Nothing is published; closeable only by quoting api-sales@genius.com.
  • Does the official OAuth API enforce a rate limit? The docs state none and the web API showed none at concurrency 8 — closeable by ramping a tokened client until it throttles.
  • Which publishers are covered by the current license, and what triggers a song's removal? The 2014 NMPA deal is the last public disclosure; closeable only by a Genius or NMPA statement.
  • What caused the density collapse to 33% around song ID 3M when neighbouring bands run 62–92%? Closeable by dense enumeration of the 2.9M–3.1M range and inspecting whether the gap is deletion, merge or a migration artifact.
  • Are the ~4.5K videos licensable separately, and does Genius hold full rights to the performances in them? No public terms exist for the video library.
  • Official counts of songs, annotations and contributors — Genius publishes none, so every figure here is a Site Wise ID-space estimate.
