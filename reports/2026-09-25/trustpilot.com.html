Context

Trustpilot was founded in 2007 and is headquartered in Copenhagen (Wikipedia). Trustpilot Group plc trades in London. Anyone can review any business, and businesses cannot hide reviews. Revenue comes from businesses, not consumers: companies subscribe to tools that invite, showcase and analyse reviews. Since September 2025 a separate product, Data Solutions, sells API access to the whole review corpus. The filings now describe the corpus as input for AI answer engines: Trustpilot calls itself the "#1 cited review platform" and sells businesses "Answer Engine Optimisation". For an index builder, that strategy explains the unusual crawl posture. robots.txt disallows everyone by default but allowlists 24 named agents, including GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended. The consumer Terms still forbid scraping and text mining, AI training included. Anonymous pagination also ends after the 200 newest reviews, so the full archive is effectively available only through the paid API.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Trustpilot differs from the default platform assumptions.

The AI allowlist is inverted

The default assumption is that AI crawlers are blocked. Here training and answer bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot) get the same rules as Googlebot, while Common Crawl, Apple training, Meta, ByteDance and Cohere are blocked. The Terms still forbid mining. Read the allowlist as a list of bilateral relationships, not as permission for any crawler that sends one of those user-agent strings.

Identifier & dedup

A business has two keys: its URL slug (the reviewed domain, identifyingName) and a 24-hex businessUnit.id. Store both. Merged businesses and locations 307 to a surviving slug, and isMerged flags them. Review and consumer IDs are ObjectIds. For reviews published since mid-2022, adding 7,200s to the ID's timestamp reproduces publishedDate, so a review can be dated without fetching it.

A 200-review window, English by default

Logged-out paging ends at page 10, and the languages= filter is robots-disallowed. For the 93% of sampled businesses with 10 or fewer pages, the public page is the whole corpus. For large brands it is a recent slice, so a crawl of big brands measures recency rather than reputation.

Provenance is machine-readable

Each review says how it arrived: organic, invitation link, Shopify or WooCommerce integration, and so on. It also carries a verification flag and whether the business replied. Index these as ranking features. Invited reviews are submitted after a purchase and skew positive, so keep them separate from organic reviews when computing a sentiment score.

Two copies of every review

The JSON-LD and __NEXT_DATA__ hold the same 20 reviews. Only __NEXT_DATA__ has experiencedDate, the source label and replies, but it adds about 300KB. Use the Range-fetched JSON-LD for text and rating, and fetch the full page only when provenance is needed.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the review, keyed by its 24-hex ID and attached to a business. The business is a second unit, carrying rating, category rank, claimed/paid status and reply behaviour.
  • Primary keys & joins: business slug = the reviewed website's domain, which joins directly to a web index's host table. Then businessUnit.id → review ID → consumer ID (opaque) → countryCode → category ID.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Contract for Data Solutions. It is the only complete, licensed path, and the Terms rule out any other route to the full corpus. 2) If allowed only public display, use the daily sitemap diff: 78.8K changed URLs a week, fetched with a 128KB Range request for JSON-LD, about 19% of page bytes. 3) Do not use the allowlisted user-agent strings to pass the WAF unless you are the named operator.
  • Recrawl cadence: follow the sitemap lastmod daily. Refetch a business within a week of any change, because fake-review removals take effect after publication. Businesses untouched for more than 3 years (193K URLs) can be checked yearly.
  • Derivatives to generate: per-business aspect sentiment computed separately for organic and invited reviews, and a domain-level trust feature joined to the web index's host table.
  • Demo to build: a removal tracker. Take the 20 review IDs per page from a fixed sample of businesses, re-read them weekly, and chart the IDs that disappear. This gives a public measurement of the post-publication fake-review removal rate, which the filings report only as a share of submissions.

Risks to the Index

  • Legal exposure contradicts the technical openness. The WAF lets anyone claiming to be ClaudeBot through, but the Terms prohibit mining "for any purpose" without permission. A crawler that relies on the open door is in breach of the Terms.
  • Deleted reviews linger in an index. About 10% of submissions are removed as fake. An index that doesn't refetch will keep serving reviews Trustpilot has already removed, along with ratings computed from them.
  • inferred The allowlist follows Trustpilot's commercial deals and may change without notice. Basis: the filings present LLM citations as a growth driver (FY2025 p.2: "click-throughs from AI search up 1490%"). robots.txt admits the crawlers behind every answer engine named in the 2026-09-24 press release (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini), plus Anthropic's, and blocks Common Crawl, Apple training, Meta and ByteDance. At the same time, Data Solutions sells the same corpus. An agent's access depends on its relationship with Trustpilot, not on a stable policy.
  • Regulatory action changes what ratings mean. In March 2026 Italy's competition authority (AGCM) fined Trustpilot €4M over an unfair commercial practice. Trustpilot is appealing (H1 2026 results). Consumer-protection rules on reviews could change how scores are computed or displayed. Keep the raw reviews and recompute scores yourself instead of storing trustScore as ground truth.

Inferences

  • Search-engine agents are checked by IP, while AI agents pass on the user-agent string alone. Basis: from the same IP, the Googlebot and bingbot strings got the WAF challenge, while the ClaudeBot, GPTBot and PerplexityBot strings, and the bare token ClaudeBot, got 200.
  • The two-hour hold started between March and June 2022. Basis: the gap between ID time and publication time is 0s on every sampled review dated 2022-03 or earlier and 7,200s on every one from 2022-06 on.
