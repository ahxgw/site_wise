Context

foxnews.com is the digital arm of FOX News Media, part of Fox Corporation's Cable Network Programming segment. It is free and ad-supported, with no paywall flag on any sampled page. In 2026 it absorbed two former standalone hosts: OutKick became a foxnews.com vertical in June, and press.foxnews.com now redirects into /press. For an index builder it is the opposite of the cnn.com profile. CNN blocks about 90 named crawlers and abandoned RSS. Fox blocks none, publishes full-text RSS with a WebSub hub, emits the complete articleBody in JSON-LD, and lists its sitemaps in an llms.txt. The open question is not access but scope. The public sitemap tree runs 7–12% short of what the site's own CMS API reports. Video splits into open clip MP4s and token-gated full episodes that expire.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where foxnews.com differs from the default assumptions and the cnn.com profile.

No AI-crawler block at all

The default assumption is that AI crawlers are named and refused. Here robots.txt has a single * group of seven path rules, and llms.txt points agents at the sitemaps. Treat the openness as a crawl permission, not a licence. The page copyright line and the absence of any reuse terms still apply. A one-line robots change could close the whole site, so snapshot robots.txt with every crawl.

Sitemap lastmod is not a date for old articles

Pages from about 250 to 334 carry lastmods packed into seconds on 2014-11-17 and a few days after (Probe Log). That is a bulk migration stamp. Take the publish date from the page's datePublished or Spark publication_date, never from the sitemap, or about 400K articles will collapse into one week of 2014.

One feed replaces the poll loop

The RSS carries full bodies and declares a WebSub hub. Subscribe to the hub for push, and keep a 15-minute poll of fastchanging as backstop. The 25-item feed window is under four hours at about 155 articles per day, so a feed-only crawler that misses a cycle loses stories. The news sitemap's 48-hour window is the safer catch-up.

VideoObject fields are swapped

Fox puts the watch page in contentUrl and the MP4 in embedUrl, the reverse of schema.org intent. A generic extractor will try to embed an MP4 and download an HTML page. Map them by hostname (/video/ page versus vod. / tokenvod.), not by property name.

Three video classes, three policies

Legacy media2 and current vod clips are open MP4s. tokenvod full episodes are signed, carry expiry dates days to weeks out, and 403'd even with a fresh token. Their captions on the same host did fetch. Model episodes as caption-plus-metadata documents with a hard TTL. Of the 84 dated entries on page 1, 5 had already passed expiry at fetch time and were still listed, so enforce expiry yourself.

One host, several brands

The news sitemap is 37% OutKick (outkick-sports, outkick-betting, outkick-culture). The video sitemap mixes in vod.foxbusiness.com, and one Spark record originated on cms.foxsports.com. Brand is a field (path prefix, origination_station, wp__origin.site), not a host. Betting content brings oddschecker widgets into the page, so filter it out if the index has a gambling policy.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, keyed by canonical URL path. Its embedded videos are child documents keyed by Brightcove ID (/video/{13-digit id}). Each episode is a caption document with an expiry.
  • Join keys: Spark UUIDv5 (spark_id) for articles, tags, persons and sections; WordPress post_id as a secondary key; Brightcove video ID (external_id) linking sitemap, page and Spark; video_spark_ids on articles for article-to-video joins.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Full sitemap backfill: 1.67M article and 608K video URLs, with page fetch for JSON-LD bodies. 2) WebSub on the RSS hub plus fastchanging polling for freshness. 3) Spark records only as an enrichment and gap-check layer, rate-limited, given that www robots disallows its mirror path (Risks).
  • Recrawl cadence: new articles at +1h and +24h (dateModified usually equals datePublished at first); the archive yearly; video metadata on publish; episode records removed at expiration_date.
  • Derivatives: clip ASR and keyframes from the open MP4s; caption normalisation (case restore, fixing live-transcription errors); a brand classifier from path and origin; a sponsored flag from fn__story_type (5 of 100 sampled Spark records were "Sponsored").
  • Demo to build: a "TV-to-web" linker that matches episode caption segments to the articles and clips published from them the same day, joined on Brightcove ID and show tags.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The Spark API is not an invitation. Basis: www robots.txt disallows /api/article-search, the same-origin path for this search backend. api.foxnews.com/robots.txt returns 422 rather than a policy, and the page config exposing the endpoints also carries an OAuth software-statement JWT, which marks it as a client integration, not a public service. Use it sparingly for counts and gap checks, and build on sitemaps and RSS.
  • inferred The sitemap undercounts the corpus. Basis: 1.67M sitemap articles against 1.80M from Spark, and 608K videos against 688K (Verified Metrics). The missing 7–12% may be unpublished, expired or deliberately unlisted items; that is an Open Question. A sitemap-only crawler should not claim completeness.
  • Openness is a policy choice that can flip. Fox is in the minority among large US publishers in naming no crawler at all (compare cnn.com). Index state should record the robots.txt snapshot each document was fetched under.
  • inferred The host keeps absorbing brands. Basis: outkick.com and press.foxnews.com both now 301 into foxnews.com (Probe Log), and the June 2026 release describes OutKick as a vertical "under the FOX News Digital domain". Expect more path-level brands and old-host redirect chains to resolve during dedup.
