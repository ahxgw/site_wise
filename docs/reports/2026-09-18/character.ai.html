Context

Character.AI is a UGC platform whose unit of content is a Character: a name, a persona description, and a hidden definition that conditions a chat model. Users create them, others chat with them, and the chats stay private. Founded November 2021 by two ex-Google researchers, the company sold Google a $2.7B non-exclusive technology licence in August 2024 and lost both founders to Google DeepMind in the same deal; it remains independently owned (see Verified Metrics). Three things make it unusual for an index builder. The retrievable object is a persona card, not a document — the generative payload behind it is never served. The corpus is legally fragmented: the terms assign each Character to its creator, not the platform. And the crawl surface contradicts itself — robots.txt allows every AI crawler by omission while the terms forbid automated collection outright. inferred That permissiveness is SEO neglect, not an open-data stance. Basis: the file is 7 lines with a single User-agent: * group and its sitemap has not regenerated since 2026-06-30 (Probe Log), against an explicit anti-scraping clause in the terms (Verified Metrics).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where character.ai differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Four namespaces per character: the 8-char short_hash in the URL, a 43-char base64url external_id used by the API and OG images, an identifier of the form id:<uuid>, and a separate lorebook_id. Only the first two are externally useful and neither is derivable from the other — the character.about hop exists purely to bridge them. Slugs are decorative and drift: one sampled character is /tjVpzAWl/bl-best-friend in the sitemap but /tjVpzAWl/river-best-friend-sleepover in its own JSON-LD. Key on the hash, treat the slug as a label.

Retrieval unit

The persona card — name, title, description, avatar, creator, engagement counts. It is not a document and has no body: the definition that makes the character behave is flagged by has_definition but never served, and greeting came back empty on 14/14. The index retrieves an invitation to a conversation, and the conversation is not in the corpus.

Cross-modal joins

Everything hangs off external_id: avatar path, OG card, comment thread, voice binding. The creator join is user__username, resolving directly to /profile/<username> — a stable, reversible edge that makes the creator graph fully traversable even though no profile appears in the sitemap.

Authoritative content field

Inverted from the usual case: the HTML head is the least reliable source and the hydration payload the most — JSON-LD and per-object og:title render on 4/15 sampled pages while __NEXT_DATA__ is complete on 15/15. inferred Head metadata is injected client-side and loses a race against response flush. Basis: every meta tag carries Next.js's data-next-head attribute, and the pages missing per-object tags serve identical site-wide defaults rather than nothing.

Freshness & recrawl

The sitemap is not a freshness signal — all 280K entries share one lastmod 80 days stale (Verified Metrics). Per-record updated is the only usable timestamp. Content and engagement move on different clocks: persona text is written once and rarely edited, while the interaction counter climbs continuously.

Quality & spam

The dominant quality problem is rights, not spam. inferred Unlicensed third-party IP is the majority of the indexable corpus, not a tail. Basis: 8 of 15 characters drawn at fixed intervals from the sitemap are identifiable franchise or real-person personas — Gojo Satoru, Katsuki Bakugo, Shuichi Saihara, The Hashiras, The Avangers, a Wuthering Waves character, and two Stray Kids member personas.

Internal vs external search

robots.txt disallows /search and, via Disallow: /*?*, every query-string URL — discovery is entirely internal and no results surface is indexable. External engines can rank characters only on the thin persona card, which is why on-site browse carries the traffic.

User query profile

Queries are character-name and scenario lookups ("gojo satoru c.ai", "enemies to lovers bot"), not informational. inferred The site treats itself as a destination rather than a search target. Basis: the homepage 403s even to the Googlebot UA while object pages return 200 (Probe Log), and the sitemap has gone 80 days without regeneration.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the persona card, keyed by external_id, with the creator record as parent and engagement counters as a fast-refresh sub-record.
  • Primary keys & joins: external_id (canonical) ↔ short_hash (URL); creator edge user__username → /profile/<username>; media via avatar_file_name → characterai.io.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Creator profiles — highest yield per request: one fetch returns a creator plus their whole authored set with external_id already resolved, skipping the character.about hop, and reaches characters the sitemap omits. Seed from usernames harvested during (2). 2) Sitemap → tRPC chain — complete and ordered, but two requests per character and capped at 280K. 3) Character HTML — same record in one request, at ~320KB versus ~2KB of JSON; use only where the API is unavailable. 4) Head metadata — do not build on it, 4/15 coverage.
  • Recrawl cadence: persona text monthly (write-once in practice); engagement counters weekly for the head of the distribution and never for the tail; creator profiles weekly; sitemap re-census monthly, purely to detect whether the 2026-06-30 freeze breaks.
  • Derivatives to generate: franchise/entity linking from persona names to a canonical IP registry — the highest-value derivative, since it turns a name soup into a rights-aware, browsable facet; avatar embeddings for near-duplicate detection across re-uploads of the same character; creator-to-franchise affinity for ranking.
  • Demo to build: a franchise-collision browser — resolve the sampled personas to their source franchises and show how many distinct creators publish a character of the same name, quantifying the duplication the short_hash namespace hides.

Risks to the Index

  • The access path is undocumented, not licensed. The tRPC endpoints answer without auth, but the ToS bans "data mining, robots, scraping or similar automated data gathering" with no search-engine carve-out and forbids evading an IP block. Building on this corpus is a terms risk, not a technical one, and the permissive robots.txt does not cure it.
  • No single party can license the corpus cleanly. Creators own their Characters; Character.AI holds only a sublicensable licence (Verified Metrics). A bulk deal with the company is legally possible but would pass through rights most creators never held, given the franchise concentration measured above.
  • The endpoints are internal and unversioned. One Next.js refactor retires character.about or reshapes the hydration blob, with no deprecation notice because nothing was published. The 404s on character.search and character.comments show the surface is already narrower than the client's own needs.
  • Ownership is unsettled. The company was reported in August 2025 to be weighing a sale or new round amid rising costs. PYMNTS verified An acquirer would likely close the open surface before monetizing it.
  • Safety regulation shrinks the corpus. The under-18 withdrawal removed a user class rather than moderating it (Verified Metrics). Further pressure plausibly deletes character classes outright, making the deletion rate policy-driven and unpredictable rather than organic.
