Context

Cambridge University Press & Assessment is a non-teaching department of the University of Cambridge, funded by publishing and exam revenue rather than by shareholders. Two very different properties share the cambridge.org domain. Cambridge Core (www.cambridge.org/core) hosts the scholarly corpus — 2.56M registered DOIs — and is sold to institutions by subscription and article-processing charge. Cambridge Dictionary (dictionary.cambridge.org) is a free ad-supported learner dictionary that the organisation calls the world's number-one dictionary website by page views. For an index builder these are opposite problems. The dictionary publishes a 1,163-file sitemap, allows every crawler on entry pages, and reserves nothing. Cambridge Core publishes no working content sitemap, sets a machine-readable tdm-reservation, and routes commercial mining to a business-development mailbox. inferred Cambridge treats AI access as a licensing negotiation rather than a robots.txt line. Basis: the ODRL policy at /core/tdm/tdm-policy.json grants tdm:mine unconditionally only when purpose equals stm:eu-dsm-article3 and attaches an obtainConsent duty otherwise, while robots.txt names no AI crawler at all (both in the Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where cambridge.org differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier, dedup & joins

Core has three usable keys: the DOI, the 32-hex title_id in KBART, and the S-prefixed ID in content/view/S1365100525100758 — the DOI suffix uppercased, so DOI ↔ ISSN/ISBN ↔ title_id ↔ collection closes for free. The dictionary has no ID at all: the slug is the only key, the same headword recurs under 26 locales and 30+ bilingual pairs, and headword ↔ audio ↔ examples ↔ thesaurus join only by parallel slug — a string match that breaks on locale. Locale collapse is the largest dedup job on this domain.

Retrieval unit

Core: the work (article, chapter or Element) keyed by DOI, with the abstract as body. Dictionary: the sense, not the headword — a page for "run" carries dozens of independently answerable senses, so page-level retrieval returns the wrong granularity for a definition query.

Authoritative content field

For Core the deposited Crossref abstract outranks anything scraped: publisher-supplied, CC0-licensed, JATS-tagged. But it exists for only 48.7% of journal articles and 0.2% of book chapters, so chapters are title-and-metadata only unless the book is OA.

Freshness & recrawl

Inverted from a typical large site: the corpus that generates the traffic barely changes. 18K new dictionary items a year against ~18M URLs is a ~0.1% annual delta, and 94% of Core's DOIs are backfile. Recrawl budget belongs almost entirely to the 152.6K current DOIs.

Quality & duplication

The adversarial surface here is piracy, not spam: 53M pirated links were removed in one year. A general web index will meet many more copies of a Cambridge PDF than authorised ones, so canonicalising to the DOI — rather than to whichever host ranks — is a correctness requirement.

Internal vs external search

inferred The dictionary is built to receive external search traffic straight onto entry pages rather than to run its own discovery. Basis: robots.txt disallows /search/, /browse/, /autocomplete/ and /spellcheck/ on all 26 locales while leaving entry pages open, and the sitemap enumerates entries but no navigational surface. The two query populations are disjoint: single-word and "meaning of X" lookups on the dictionary, citation-shaped title/author/DOI lookups on Core.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: Core = work keyed by DOI, abstract as body; Dictionary = sense, keyed by slug + locale + dictionary-pair.
  • Primary keys & joins: DOI as the spine; ISSN/ISBN → KBART title_id → collection; DOI suffix uppercased → the content/view article ID; dictionary slug with an explicit locale-collapse table.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Crossref REST — 2.56M records, CC0, no auth, no meaningful quota; covers metadata, abstracts, licences, references and peer reviews. 2) KBART title lists — 695 files giving holdings, collection membership and paid/free flags that exist nowhere else. 3) OpenAlex/Unpaywall — resolves the ~78% of 2025 articles that have a legitimate OA copy, usually not on cambridge.org. 4) Cambridge Dictionary API — the only lawful bulk route to the dictionary. 5) Direct crawl — permitted by robots for dictionary entries but blocked in practice by Cloudflare; treat as unavailable unless you hold verified-crawler status.
  • Recrawl cadence: Crossref incremental by from-index-date daily; KBART quarterly; dictionary annually; backfile DOIs never.
  • Derivatives: embeddings over abstracts and titles only; a locale-collapse map for dictionary near-duplicates; a DOI-canonicalisation table to fold pirated mirrors onto the authorised record. No ASR — the pronunciation audio is not licensed to hold.
  • Demo to build: a TDMRep-aware ingest gate. Read /.well-known/tdmrep.json and the linked ODRL policy for each target host, classify the request as EU-DSM-Article-3-permitted or consent-required, and refuse to fetch the second class — turning a machine-readable reservation into an enforced pipeline rule instead of a footnote.

Risks to the Index

  • Absence of a robots.txt block is not permission. Cambridge names no AI crawler, yet reserves TDM rights machine-readably for the entire site. A pipeline whose only rights check is robots.txt will classify cambridge.org as open and be wrong on 2.56M works.
  • The licensable corpus is a moving subset. Cambridge licenses AI rights only for authors who opt in to a contract addendum, so "what Cambridge may license" is smaller than "what Cambridge publishes" and changes as authors respond (The Bookseller).
  • Discovery via the site is already broken. The domain's only advertised sitemap resolves to 19 marketing URLs and a 404, and every live fetch in this report was refused. Any plan that assumes crawlable discovery of Cambridge Core has no foundation.
  • The full-text link is mislabelled as a mining path. Crossref carries a link for 2.34M works, but its intended-application is similarity-checking and the target 429s on the first request. Counting those 2.34M as retrievable full text overstates coverage by roughly an order of magnitude.
  • inferred The wall will keep hardening. Basis: both hosts already serve Cloudflare managed challenges on robots.txt itself, and the origin returns Varnish 429 on a single unthrottled request to the one robots-allowed service path.

Inferences

  • Cambridge treats AI and commercial mining as a licensing negotiation, not a crawler block. Basis: tdm-reservation: 1 site-wide plus an ODRL policy whose only unconditional grant is purpose = stm:eu-dsm-article3, with obtainConsent and a business-development contact for everything else — while robots.txt names no AI user agent.
  • The dictionary, not the press, carries the domain's traffic. Basis: the dictionary sitemap enumerates ~18M URLs across 26 locales and its entry pages are open to *, whereas Cambridge Core's 2.56M DOIs have zero working sitemap coverage; the Annual Report separately claims the number-one dictionary website by page views.
  • Cambridge Core has no functioning public discovery surface. Basis: robots.txt carries exactly one Sitemap directive, and its index resolves to a 19-URL marketing file plus Cannot GET /highereducation/sitemap/sitemap-1.xml.
  • The dictionary is designed to land external search traffic rather than to be browsed. Basis: /search/, /browse/, /autocomplete/ and /spellcheck/ are disallowed on all 26 locale prefixes while entry pages stay allowed, and the sitemap enumerates entries only.
  • Crossref's full-text links are not a mining route. Basis: the sampled record's intended-application is similarity-checking, and its target content/view path returned Varnish 429 on a single request.
