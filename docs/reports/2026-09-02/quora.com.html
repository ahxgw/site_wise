Context

Quora was founded Jun 25 2009 by ex-Facebook employees Adam D'Angelo (still CEO) and Charlie Cheever, and opened publicly Jun 21 2010. It remains private and independent, last marked at $2B in May 2019 (Wikipedia). Revenue comes from display advertising, the Quora+ subscription (Aug 5 2021), and Poe, the multi-model AI chat product launched Feb 3 2023 on shared infrastructure.

For an index builder Quora is the sharpest case in the top 200 of a site enormously valuable to retrieval and almost entirely closed to it. The corpus is long-form natural-language answers to explicit natural-language questions — the exact shape of a RAG or training target — and roughly half of Quora's traffic arrives from organic search, so it demonstrably ranks. Yet there is no public API, no sitemap, no bulk dump, and a robots.txt whose opening lines are a contract notice rather than a rule. Access is not metered; it is negotiated. The one unauthenticated machine-readable endpoint in the estate belongs to Poe, and it describes models, not answers.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Quora departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

No exposed numeric object ID; the slug is the key, and it is unusually unstable. Questions get merged (/*/merged exists as a blocked path), silently redirecting one slug into another, and the 24 language editions restate the same question under different hosts with no published hreflang join. Treat the answer permalink /<Question-Slug>/answer/<Author-Slug> as the durable unit and expect the question slug alone to alias.

Retrieval unit

The answer, not the page. A question page is an ordered list of independent, competing documents by different authors; indexing the page concatenates a dozen unrelated arguments into one blob and destroys authorship. It matters more here than on article-shaped sites because the question is the natural query and the answer is the natural result — the corpus is pre-shaped as query/document pairs.

Cross-modal joins

Every join key here is a blocked path: author→answers (/*/all_posts), question→topics (/*/topics), answer→discussion (/*/comments), question→variants (/*/merged) are Disallowed in all 29 groups. Even an allowlisted crawler must reconstruct the graph from bylines and inline links rather than fetch it.

Freshness & recrawl

Quora inverts the usual decay curve: answers are edited in place indefinitely and old questions keep accruing new answers for years, so content changes long after the URL stops being new. Revision history exists at <url>/log — Quora says so in its own robots.txt comment — but /*/log is disallowed to every agent, so there is no cheap change-detection signal. Recrawl must be blind and periodic.

Authoritative field, quality & spam

The title is a genuine user question in natural language, not an SEO construct — the highest-value field on the site and the reason Quora ranks. The distortion sits below it: the answer body is where marketing spam lives, since the incentive is to place a link inside a question that already ranks. Trust the title, score the body. The site-specific hazard is retroactive anonymization: Quora's robots.txt argues at length that authors must be able to anonymize sensitive answers after publication, so an indexed byline can become wrong rather than merely stale. That is a live de-anonymization risk — and a standing deletion obligation — for anyone caching this corpus.

Query profile & search escape

The URLs are the query log: a slug like /What-is-the-number-of-questions-on-Quora is a verbatim natural-language question, making Quora's URL space a rare public sample of long-tail informational intent — subjective, experiential, comparative — the class that returns badly from document-centric indexes. Quora's stance on external search is explicit: /search?q= is Disallowed in all 29 groups while question pages are not, so engines must enter at the answer. With roughly half of visits arriving from organic search (Verified Metrics), Google is effectively Quora's search box.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the individual answer, carrying its question title as the paired query, its author byline, and its rank position on the question.
  • Primary keys & joins: /<Question-Slug>/answer/<Author-Slug> as primary key; question slug as a soft group key that must tolerate merge-aliasing; author slug as the identity join; language subdomain as a facet, with cross-language linking treated as unsolved entity resolution rather than a lookup.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Write to robotstxt@quora.com — Quora publishes this address in robots.txt and the entire text corpus sits behind it. 2) api.poe.com/v1/models — free, unauthenticated, live, but models only. 3) Rendered browser crawl: solving the challenge is not a legitimate path here, since * is Disallow: / — excluded on policy, not capability. 4) Third-party mirrors and scraper packages: prohibited by the robots.txt notice and the terms.
  • Recrawl cadence: robots.txt weekly — it is the actual contract and it changes; question pages monthly under an agreement, since edits are invisible without /log; the Poe catalog daily (records stamped two days before this probe).
  • Derivatives to generate: question→answer pairs as native retrieval training data — the highest-value derivative here and the reason the site is a licensing target; author-reputation features from byline frequency; per-answer promotion scoring; cross-language question clustering to collapse the 24 editions.
  • Demo to build: a robots.txt diff monitor. The 824-line file names 29 agents individually and is the single mechanism controlling access to the whole corpus; a daily fetch alerting on group additions, removals and rule-level diffs would detect a new licensing deal — or a new blanket block — the day it lands, using the only endpoint on the site that reliably answers.

Risks to the Index

  • Access is a contract revocable by editing one file. Ten agents are permitted by name; adding or removing a group is a one-line change on a 4-hour cache TTL. An index built on allowlisted crawl has no notice period.
  • Declared policy and enforced policy disagree. robots.txt permits seven paths to unnamed agents; all seven return a Cloudflare challenge (Probe Log). Anything built by reading robots.txt alone misjudges what is reachable.
  • inferred Google holds a materially better position than every other AI operator, and it is visible in the file. Basis: Google-Extended receives the same 62-rule path-level block as Googlebot rather than the bare Disallow: / given to GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot and thirteen others — and is permitted three answer-permalink patterns Googlebot itself is denied (Probe Log). A policy this asymmetric is a commercial arrangement expressed as configuration.
  • inferred Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yandex and Baidu are excluded by omission. Basis: none appears among the 29 groups, so each falls through to * = Disallow: / (Probe Log). Quora's search distribution is a Google monoculture by policy, and any index sourced from a non-Google engine's coverage will be structurally short of Quora.
  • inferred Quora and Poe share origin infrastructure, so a policy change at one host can move both. Basis: both emit the proprietary x-q-stat header, and both media CDNs use the qph.cf2. prefix (Probe Log). Poe's open API should not be assumed to be governed independently of Quora's closed one.
  • inferred The open Poe catalog endpoint is likely to close. Basis: it is the only unauthenticated JSON surface across the three hosts, it returns 200 even with an invalid bearer token, and adjacent inference endpoints are metered (Probe Log). Snapshot it on a schedule rather than depending on it.

Inferences

Four inferences carry their basis in Risks to the Index and are not restated: Google-Extended's privileged position, exclusion of Bing/DuckDuckGo/Yandex/Baidu by omission, shared Quora–Poe infrastructure, and the Poe catalog as a transitional state. Two sit only here:

  • Per-profile and per-topic RSS feeds exist. Basis: Disallow: /profile/*/Rss and Disallow: /topic/*/Rss appear in all ten path-level groups — a site does not write disallow rules for endpoints it never built (2026-09-02).
  • Spaces live under /q/<space> and carry a creator-earnings surface. Basis: eight /q/*/… admin paths (admin_log, stats, settings, queue, suggestions, submissions, quality, earnings) are enumerated in every path-level group (2026-09-02).

Open Questions

  • Total live questions and answers — Quora has published no figure since its early years, and the widely cited "200M+ questions" traces to a 2024 user answer, not a company statement. Closeable only by an allowlisted crawl census or a Quora disclosure.
  • Structured-metadata coverage (JSON-LD QAPage/Question/Answer) on question pages — unmeasurable from an unnamed agent, since every candidate path is both challenge-blocked and Disallowed. Closeable by one fetch from an allowlisted user-agent.
  • Whether an actual AI-training licensing agreement exists between Quora and Google — the robots.txt asymmetry is strong circumstantial evidence but neither party has announced a deal. Closeable by a company statement or a filing.
  • Poe monthly active users and Quora's revenue split across ads, Quora+ and Poe — every figure in circulation (18M Poe MAU, $190M 2026 revenue) traces to SEO statistics aggregators with no named methodology, which the estimator rule excludes. Closeable only by a Quora disclosure.
  • What the ten allowlisted agents actually receive, and whether the challenge is uniform or IP-reputation dependent — every request here came from one address under one identity. The IP half is closeable by repeating the seven-path sweep from varied addresses; the user-agent half only from an allowlisted position, since testing it otherwise means misrepresenting a crawler identity.
  • The terms offered through robotstxt@quora.com — price, volume caps, redistribution rights, and whether AI-training rights are separable from search-indexing rights. Closeable by initiating the correspondence.
