Context

Stripchat is an adult live-webcam platform. Viewers watch free public streams and pay performers in tokens for tips, private and group shows, recorded videos, and fan-club tiers. It launched on 2016-05-03 and is run by Technius Ltd of Limassol, Cyprus. The same platform also runs xHamsterLive as a white label: one model pool and API under a second brand. An affiliate program (Stripcash) drives most measured traffic. Its EU VLOP designation (December 2023 to 2025) was upheld by the General Court on 2026-10-14. For an index builder, Stripchat is unusual in two ways. The main object is a live stream that exists only while a model is online: about 9.9K at a time, out of a back catalog of hundreds of thousands of profiles. And the persistent corpus (profiles, photo albums, recorded-show trailers) was fully listed in Google-style image and video sitemaps until mid-2026, when those sitemaps were removed.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where stripchat.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

The SEO sliver was withdrawn, not gated

The default two-track model expects a sitemap served to approved crawlers. Here robots.txt dropped all 8 Sitemap: lines, and every sitemap path now returns a JSON 404 to all UAs, crawler UAs included. Meanwhile model and video pages stay index, follow, and crawler UAs are explicitly whitelisted. inferred The site gave up sitemap discovery but not search indexing. Basis: the robots.txt diff between Wayback 2026-05-01 and the live 2026-10-17 copy, the live 404s, and the unchanged meta robots and isWhitelistedUserAgent flag (Probe Log).

Two crawler checks: UA and network owner

SSR state carries both isWhitelistedUserAgent (true for Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot and ClaudeBot strings) and isGooglebotGeoIpOrg (false for all from a non-Google IP). The UA flag only lifts the Accept-header gate. It did not change page content in this probe. Any Googlebot-only enrichment would hang off the network-owner (geo-IP organisation) flag, which a third party cannot spoof, so do not compare against Googlebot output.

Identifier & dedup

The numeric model id is the stable key, and the username is the URL. The census shows IDs from 8,005 to 286M, with a median of 233M, so most live models are recent accounts. The HLS stream name equals the id, which joins live stream, snapshot thumbs (/thumbs/{ts}/{id}) and profile. The same id appears on xHamsterLive, so treat the two brands as one corpus and collapse by id. Each model is also mirrored across 35 locale subdomains.

Retrieval unit: model, then show, then recording

A query here is a person or attribute search ("Colombian couple, Lovense, HD, cheap privates"), which is a structured filter over live state. Text relevance plays almost no part. Recordings link back to the live session through showId (423 of 622 in the sample), and their titles are timestamped show names ("…[26 Sep 08:16] Private Show"). Index model → session → recording, with the recording's trailer as the only visual you can display.

Freshness is the product

56% of the census changed status within about 50 minutes of the probe, and the ranked list reshuffled enough within an 8-minute pass to drop 20% of the roster. A useful live index needs passes every few minutes, which is about 157 requests each, and should be sharded by tag rather than paged deeply. The persistent layer (profiles, videos, photos) can be weekly. A model's online state cannot be reconstructed from crawled HTML after the fact.

Quality & spam

No UGC spam tier exists: models are KYC'd (the /models/{id}/persons/kyc routes are in the bundle). The ranking risk is attention concentration (top 10% of models hold 68% of viewers) and affiliate-driven landing pages. Self-declared tags such as ageTeen are marketing attributes, not facts. Downweight them and never surface birthDate.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the model (id, username, attributes, rates, toy and stream flags, profile counts), with live state attached as a time series. Recordings are a child unit with trailer, duration, access mode and showId.
  • Primary keys & joins: model id = HLS stream name = snapshot path; showId links recordings to sessions; usernames are shared with xHamsterLive. Locale subdomains collapse to the bare host.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked:
    1. Models API roster pass (about 157 requests, ~8 minutes at 1.5 s), repeated on a schedule for live state.
    2. The Wayback 2026-05-01 sitemaps as a one-time seed: 157 child files hold about 614K usernames, 4.5M photo URLs and 528K video entries with trailer URLs.
    3. /{u}/profile SSR, only for models seen live or in the seed, at 2 s per request. A full refresh at 614K is about 14 days, so prioritise recently active models.
    4. Video pages for VideoObject and the per-model video list. One page returns a model's whole catalog.
  • Recrawl cadence: live roster every 5–15 minutes. Profiles of models seen live in the past week, weekly. Dormant profiles quarterly, or never.
  • Derivatives to generate: trailer keyframes and image embeddings for recorded content; an hourly online-presence histogram per model (schedule prediction); a normalised attribute vector from the 24 tag categories. Never derive from live segments.
  • Demo to build: a "who's online that matches" search: rolling roster snapshots joined to the seed profiles, answering attribute queries and "usually online at this hour" from the presence histogram.

Risks to the Index

  • Discovery was removed in 2026: without sitemaps, new models and recordings are discoverable only through the live roster and listing pages. The seed ages from 2026-05-01 onward.
  • Request-shape gating: the Accept 406, the curl 403 and the per-endpoint Cloudflare rules (the /cam endpoint 403 while /models stays open) can tighten without notice. Fail closed and keep request rates at about 1 every 1.5 s.
  • Regulatory exposure: the 2026-10-14 judgment upholds the Commission's method for counting users, and EU age-verification and DSA duties apply to adult platforms. Any index must carry adult classification, never store personal fields, and honour takedowns (performers' DMCA requests and StopNCII hashes are linked in the footer).
  • Paid-content boundary: about 90% of one model's recordings are paid or tier-gated. Trailers and covers are the displayable surface; anything beyond them is a licensing breach.
