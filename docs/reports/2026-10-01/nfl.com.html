Daily Site Profile

nfl.com

NFL.com sitemaps 339K URLs: 161K full-text news stories since 2007 and 164K clips. It server-renders play-by-play for 5.6K games, but its terms forbid compiling databases.

Context

NFL.com is the league's own publication. It carries news written by an in-house staff (Around the NFL, NFL Network reporters), short-form video, game centers, player and team pages, and draft coverage. On 2026-02-01 ESPN closed its acquisition of NFL Network, linear RedZone and NFL Fantasy, and the NFL took a 10% stake in ESPN valued at $3B. The league kept NFL.com, along with NFL Films, NFL+, the podcast network and the 32 club sites (TheWrap). For an index builder the site is unusual in three ways. First, a sports publisher exposes its whole archive: complete news and video sitemaps reach back to 2007. Second, every news page ships the full article text in JSON-LD. Third, game pages embed structured play-by-play and box scores in the server render. robots.txt names no AI crawler. The constraint is contractual: the terms prohibit "systematic retrieval … to create or compile … a collection, compilation, database, or directory".

Sitemap corpus
339.5K unique URLs across 76 child sitemaps. That breaks down as 163.8K video, 161.1K news (including about 800 /mundo/noticias/), 5.7K draft-tracker, 5.6K game, 801 prospect and 1.6K evergreen pages. Probe Log verified
News archive depth
The archive runs from 2007-07-16 to now. Annual volume peaked at 18.6K in 2012 and fell to 4.9K in 2025, about 13 per day (calculated by Site Wise). The fast-changing sitemap held 100 stories over 6 days, about 16 per day. Probe Log verified
Video archive
163.6K clips carry a duration, totalling 7.3K hours. The median clip is 63s (calculated by Site Wise). 99.6% are dated 2018 or later: only 669 clips predate 2018-08. There were 18.7K new clips in 2025, and the fast-changing sitemap shows about 40 per day in game week. Probe Log verified
Video asset ID rate
The numeric mcpID rose from 2.19M (2025-01-19) to 2.69M (2026-10-01), about 800 IDs per day, while the sitemap gained about 51 clips per day. Only around 6% of media IDs therefore become public /videos/ pages (calculated by Site Wise). Probe Log verified
News full-text coverage
All 30 random news pages returned 200 with NewsArticle JSON-LD carrying the full articleBody. The median body is 1.8K characters and the maximum 13.7K. 18 of 30 name a Person author with an author URL. 5 of 30 end "Copyright … by The Associated Press" (2007–2014). Probe Log verified
Migration watermark
12 of 30 news pages have dateModified 2023-02-28 despite publish dates from 2015 to 2022, so dateModified is not an edit signal for back-catalogue stories. Probe Log verified
Game corpus
The games sitemap holds 5,591 games, from the 2010 season to 2026, about 333 per season (270 in 2020, when no preseason was played). 3 of 3 sampled games, from 2010, 2025 and 2026, ship 158–176 plays in the server-rendered drive chart. That puts the ceiling near 0.9M play descriptions (calculated by Site Wise). Probe Log verified
Page weight
Decoded HTML medians are 694KB for news and 321KB for video, and game pages run 0.9–1.2MB. One HTML pass over the sitemap is about 170GB (calculated by Site Wise). Probe Log verified
Images
Video thumbnails resolve to 1920×1080 JPEG originals on Cloudinary through static.www.nfl.com. URL transforms work: w_200 returns 9KB against 111KB at t_q-best. Probe Log verified

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where nfl.com differs from the default platform assumptions.

Open robots, closed terms

Most platforms in this series block AI crawlers in robots.txt. NFL.com blocks none, and serves full text to any UA. The restriction lives entirely in §1.3 of the terms. The crawl-policy signal and the contractual signal point opposite ways, so the index should follow the stricter one.

The sitemap is an inventory, with a lag

Unlike most media sites, the sitemaps enumerate the full back catalogue: 2007 news and 2018-onward video, with no evident pruning, since 50 of 50 sampled URLs resolved. The weak point is the middle. The archives were last cut on 2026-08-31 and the fast-changing files hold only 100 items, so September 2026 stories exist in neither. Poll the fast-changing sitemaps at least daily, and treat archive shards as unstable: they are newest first, so shard N's contents move.

Video is metadata-only by design

Video sitemaps give title, description, duration and tags but no media URL, the page JSON-LD claims NewsArticle, and the stream URL needs a token. A full-modal index gets text-about-video (title, description, show, team tags), thumbnails and a deep link. It does not get frames, ASR or captions. The campaignId (show) and team-UUID tags are the best facets.

Game pages are structured data in disguise

Each game center ships its drive chart and box score as JSON inside the RSC payload. Every play carries clock, down and distance, field position and a natural-language description with player names. The retrieval unit can be the play ("Rodgers to Nelson 9 yards, Super Bowl XLV"), joined to highlight clips through team UUIDs and the game date. No other surface on the site is this granular.

Two frontends, two schemas

News and games run on a Next.js App Router stack; video, player and team pages run on an older RequireJS "pocket" stack that leaks league-frontend.pocket.nfl.com into JSON-LD URLs. Use two parsers, and rewrite the leaked host to www.nfl.com before deduplicating.

Geo is in the response, not the URL

Responses vary on x-nfl-geo and echo a Nielsen DMA code, so a crawl from one market sees that market's local-broadcast and ways-to-watch modules. Article bodies were identical across the sample. Pin one US egress and treat the "how to watch" modules as non-indexable.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the news story (headline, full body, authors, section, keywords, timestamps); the video clip (title, description, duration, show, team tags, thumbnail, mcpID); the game (teams, season, week, final score) with its plays as sub-units; and the player and prospect entities.
  • Primary keys & joins: URL slug (no numeric ID in news URLs); video entityId UUID plus mcpID; team UUIDs (10403900-… for PIT) shared across video tags, game centers and rosters; game ID (legacy date-encoded or UUIDv1) plus the game slug's season, type and week; GSIS-style player and prospect UUIDs; and author URLs carrying legacy 0ap… IDs.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The fast-changing news and video sitemaps every 30 minutes on game days and every 2 hours otherwise. 2) A one-time backfill over the 66 archive shards: 325K pages, about 165GB. 3) Game centers from the games sitemap for play-by-play, re-fetched after each game's final. 4) Team roster pages weekly, then player pages, since no player sitemap exists. 5) Prospect sitemaps each spring.
  • Recrawl cadence: news at 1 hour and 1 day after publish, then never, since the 2023-02-28 migration re-save makes dateModified useless as a change signal. Video metadata monthly. Game pages live during games, then final. Rosters weekly in season.
  • Derivatives to generate: named-entity links from play descriptions ("A.Rodgers" to a player UUID through the roster); story-to-game joins by team UUID and date window; an AP-wire flag from the copyright trailer, to exclude that copy from any snippet store; and thumbnail embeddings at w_400.
  • Demo to build: play search. Type "fourth-down conversion Mahomes 2023 playoffs" and get matching plays from game-center drive charts, each linked to the game page and the nearest highlight clip by team and date. It would be built for local research only, under the terms constraint below.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms ban compilation outright. §1.3 prohibits systematic retrieval to build "a collection, compilation, database, or directory" without written consent (NFL Terms). That covers exactly what a play-by-play or news index is. Treat the public index as headline, snippet and link discovery, and get consent or a data licence before storing bodies or plays.
  • Third-party copyright inside first-party pages. AP wire stories (5 of 30 sampled, 2007–2014) and Getty-style photography sit under the NFL's URLs but belong to their owners. Detect the AP trailer and exclude those bodies.
  • Ownership churn moves surfaces. The 2026-02-01 ESPN deal moved NFL Fantasy and NFL Network off the league. NFL Network-branded video and Fantasy pages could migrate to ESPN hosts and leave 301s or 404s. Store a tombstone and redirect target per URL.
  • Sitemap lag hides recent news. If the fast-changing poll misses a window, stories from that window stay undiscoverable until the next archive regeneration. Back the sitemap poll with team and section listing pages.

Inferences

  • The archive sitemaps are regenerated about monthly, not continuously. Basis: both archive shard 1 files end exactly on 2026-08-31, while the fast-changing files start on 2026-09-25 (news) and 2026-09-29 (video).
  • Most mcpIDs are internal media assets (game feeds, club clips, ad and promo cuts) that never get a /videos/ page. Basis: about 800 IDs per day against about 51 sitemap clips per day over the same 620 days, and an is-league-video flag in the player markup.
  • The video library was rebuilt around August 2018 and most earlier clips were dropped. Basis: 669 clips dated before 2018-08 against 15.0K from 2018-08 to year-end, and the lowest sampled mcpID (254K) dated 2018-08-30.

Open Questions

  • How many player pages exist, including retired players. Could be closed by crawling all 32 /teams/{slug}/roster pages and historical roster or stat leader pages, and deduplicating player slugs.
  • Whether play-by-play coverage is complete for every game from 2010 to 2026 or thins out in early seasons and preseason. Could be closed by sampling 5 games per season from the games sitemap and counting play- keys.
  • Whether NFL Network-branded video and NFL Fantasy URLs are migrating to ESPN hosts. Could be closed by re-checking 200 /videos/ URLs tagged NFL Network shows monthly for redirects.
  • Whether captions or transcripts exist for clips. Could be closed by inspecting the authorized player manifest in a browser session for WebVTT tracks.
  • Rate limits on page fetches. Could be closed by a controlled ramp from 1 to 5 requests per second over 10 minutes, watching for 429 or Fastly blocks.
  • An official, dated NFL.com traffic or digital-audience figure. Could be closed by the league's annual digital-audience press release or a named third-party panel.
