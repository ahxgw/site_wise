Context

support.apple.com is Apple's official help site. It is a cost centre that serves the installed base of iPhone, Mac, iPad, Watch and services users, and it routes them to repair, AppleCare and the Apple Store. It has no ads and no login wall. For an index it holds three distinct corpora. The first is about 2,100 knowledge-base articles under six-digit IDs, each in up to about 120 locales. The second is 168 user guides ("books") split into about 15,600 topic pages, translated into up to 134 locales. The third is an asset CDN with product PDFs and how-to videos that ship with WebVTT captions. All three are first-party, authoritative, and much more stable than user-generated content. The site mostly says what an Apple product does. This is the opposite posture to the other Apple hosts covered here (apple.com, apps.apple.com, tv.apple.com): nothing is metered or walled, and the work is in deduplicating locales and versions, not in getting access.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where support.apple.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

No curated sliver: the whole corpus is listed

Here the SEO track and the full corpus are the same thing. robots.txt has no AI-crawler groups, disallows only search, app-sourced views and manual PDFs, and lists every guide topic in every locale. The one restricted crawler is Baiduspider, which is confined to Simplified Chinese. The cost is volume, not access: 86% of URLs are translations of about 15.6K guide topics.

Two CDNs, one origin

Resolvers that Apple's GSLB places in China get a Kingsoft Cloud edge, and everyone else gets Akamai. Both served byte-identical robots.txt and the same Last-Modified. A crawler running in or near China should still pin the ROW edge, because the two caches age independently.

Identifiers & joins

Article key: the six-digit ID. It is locale-independent, so /{loc}/{id} × ~120 locales is one document cluster. Legacy HT/DL/SP IDs 301 to it, so store them as aliases. Guide key: (book, topic-ID suffix). The localized slug before the suffix is cosmetic, and the book TOC's tocid is a second key. Video key: the hash plus epoch in the HLS path. hreflang clusters are dense (up to 131 alternates), so one fetch yields every locale URL.

Dates lie in three directions

The visible "Published Date" is the last republish, not first publication: 102624 shows 2026-10-16 while its video asset is from 2025-01-13. Sitemap lastmod tracks the same republish. Guide parts have no lastmod at all. For first-seen dates use the ID order (100100 → 149851 is roughly chronological) or archive captures. Use a microdata parser that tolerates broken attributes; the <time> tag is malformed.

Answer-engine flags in JSON-LD

inferred Apple tags articles for generative answer systems. Basis: JSON-LD keywords on sampled articles carry gb-do-not-answer (on the overheating-safety article 118431), gb-needs-review (Apple Card billing, 123703) and gb-loc-disclaimer. An index that synthesizes answers should honour do-not-answer by quoting and linking rather than summarizing.

Captions make video cheap

The sampled video exposes a WebVTT track in its HLS master, so the video modality can be indexed as timed text without decoding media. Thumbnails and titles come straight from the sitemap's video extension.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article (ID) and the guide topic (book + topic ID). Each holds per-locale text, inline images, video with timed captions, the version/OS scope from the guide's platform segment (/ios, /mac, /ipados), and the published date.
  • Primary keys & joins: six-digit article ID with legacy-ID aliases; (book, topic-ID); tocid; HLS hash; CVE IDs from security articles joined to release rows in 100100.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The full sitemap tree (686 files, about 1.6M URLs), fetched at ~3 req/s from the Akamai edge. 2) For each cluster, fetch the primary locale and expand the others from hreflang, fetching translations only for the languages the index serves. 3) Asset CDN for HLS subtitle tracks and PDFs. 4) /docs/{product} hubs for PDFs that no sitemap lists.
  • Recrawl cadence: security index daily. ac sitemaps weekly, diffing lastmod. Guide sitemaps weekly, using the URL set diff plus conditional GETs on Last-Modified, with an extra sweep after each major OS release. Docs hubs monthly.
  • Derivatives to generate: a locale-collapsed canonical doc with a language list; a version-scoped facet (OS and device) from guide platform segments and "available for" text; a caption-to-transcript join for videos; a CVE → product → release-date table.
  • Demo to build: an "answer with citation" panel over en-us articles and guides that respects the gb-do-not-answer flag, quoting the article instead of summarizing it, and that jumps to the caption timestamp when the answer lives in a video.

Risks to the Index

  • Translation bloat. 1.64M URLs collapse to about 17.7K documents (2,122 articles plus 15,574 guide topics; calculated by Site Wise). Crawling every locale naively spends about 90× the budget for near-duplicate content.
  • Silent version drift. Guide topics carry no sitemap lastmod, and URLs stay the same across OS versions, so a cached topic can describe the previous iOS while still looking current. Store Last-Modified and the OS segment with every chunk.
  • Unlisted surfaces. Docs hubs, PDFs and per-release security advisories reached by link are absent from or under-represented in the sitemaps. A sitemap-only crawl misses them.
