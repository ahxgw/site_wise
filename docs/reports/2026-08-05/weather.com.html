Context

weather.com is the web front end of The Weather Company, a Francisco Partners portfolio company since February 2024 rather than an IBM unit. The brand is split: Allen Media Group owns the television channel, Francisco Partners owns the domain, the apps, and Weather Underground. The site monetizes attention through advertising against location pages, while the forecast engine is sold separately as a metered B2B API. That split defines the index problem. The valuable object is a forecast for a place at a time, and it is deliberately not in the HTML: location pages ship only WebPage and Organization JSON-LD, while the numbers arrive through an API that returns 401 without a key. inferred The consumer site is positioned as an ad surface and the data as a separate product, not as a crawlable dataset. Basis: the location-page JSON-LD sample in the Probe Log carries no forecast fields at all, while api.weather.com rejects unauthenticated reads with CDN-0004 Missing apiKey.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where weather.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Answer-time allowed, training blocked

The usual pattern is to block AI crawlers wholesale. weather.com splits them by purpose: ChatGPT-User (on-demand fetch for a live question) is allowed while GPTBot (training) is blocked, and AmazonAdBot is allowed while Amazonbot is blocked. inferred The site wants citation in live assistant answers without contributing to model weights. Basis: the paired allow/deny of both UA pairs in one file, plus a curated 8.4 KB llms.txt.

The sitemap key does not join

The most expensive trap here. Sitemap URLs carry a 64-hex canonicalCityId; the canonical pages they resolve to carry a 32-hex one. Different ID spaces, so a naive join on canonicalCityId silently yields zero matches. Key on the resolved canonical URL, not the sitemap parameter.

Half the sitemap is aliases

50% of sampled monthly URLs canonicalize to a different named town — small places fold into a nearby covered city. Treating sitemap entries as distinct places overstates the corpus by roughly a quarter and produces duplicate documents with near-identical bodies. Resolve canonicals before deduping, and keep alias names as retrievable surface forms for the canonical city.

Two URL schemes, split by page type

A migration is visibly in flight: today/hourbyhour/tenday canonicalize to clean /us/{state}/city/{city}/{type} slugs, while weekend/monthly keep the legacy query-string form. The sitemap ships only monthly — the page types users actually want are the ones not enumerated anywhere.

Dates are refreshed, not authored

dateCreated 2024-05-04 against datePublished 2026-07-22 on the same article. Here datePublished is a freshness signal for search, not a fact about the text. Use dateCreated for age; ranking on datePublished rewards the republishing behaviour directly.

The payload is not in the document

Location pages carry WebPage and Organization JSON-LD and nothing else — no schema.org weather types, no forecast fields. Every number a user came for arrives from the metered API. A text crawl of 30K city pages yields boilerplate chrome around values already stale when fetched. Unlike back-catalog sites there is no archive tier worth crawling slowly, only a live tier no crawler can keep up with.

Query profile is navigational

Queries are overwhelmingly "{place} weather" plus a horizon. Retrieval is entity resolution over places, not topical matching — which makes the alias collapse the central correctness problem, and makes the missing hreflang across nine locales a live risk of serving the wrong language for the right city.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: two, kept apart. (a) The place — one document per resolved canonical city holding names, aliases, state, ZIP, and available page types; forecast values are a live lookup, never an indexed field. (b) The article — one document per editorial URL, indexed conventionally.
  • Primary keys & joins: key places on the resolved canonical URL, never on canonicalCityId (two ID spaces, 2.2% null). Carry the 32-hex ID as a secondary attribute and the 64-hex one only as crawl provenance. Join articles to places by explicit place mentions — article paths are topical (/storms/tornado/news/...), not geographic.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Nine locale sitemaps for discovery — free, ceilings at ~30.8K en-US URLs, enumerates only monthly location pages. 2) Canonical resolution on every discovered URL — one fetch each, mandatory, and the only thing that makes the corpus dedupe correctly. 3) llms.txt as a section map to reach editorial areas the sitemap omits. 4) Licensed api.weather.com at $500/month per 1M calls if live values are required; the only path to the data, and it does not scale to 2.2B coordinates. 5) No path at all for video and photo collections until the S3-backed sitemaps are fixed.
  • Recrawl cadence: places monthly — identity is stable even though values are not, and lastmod moves in batch. Articles daily, since they carry no lastmod and get silently republished. robots.txt weekly; it was edited 9 days before this probe. Forecast values: never cached, fetched live or omitted.
  • Derivatives to generate: an alias table mapping the ~50% of rewritten place names to their canonical city — the highest-value artifact on the site, and it exists nowhere else. Place-name embeddings for fuzzy resolution. ASR and keyframes are not justified: there is no bulk path to the video corpus.
  • Demo to build: resolve the full 30,538-URL en-US location sitemap to canonicals and publish the true distinct-city count plus the alias table. The 40-URL sample says 50% get rewritten; running it completely turns an estimate into the one number nobody publishes, and the alias table falls out as a byproduct.

Risks to the Index

  • The URL migration completes and invalidates every key. Three of five page types already moved to /us/{state}/city/{city}/{type}. When monthly follows, every sitemap-derived key breaks at once and the 64-hex ID space disappears. Keying on resolved canonicals is what survives this.
  • Private-equity ownership favors metering. Francisco Partners bought the data business in Feb 2024; the free surface is ad-supported HTML and the forecast values already sit behind a hard 401. Further enclosure raises the price of the only path to the actual data.
  • The AI-crawler stance is unstable and freshly edited. robots.txt changed 9 days before this probe and grants ChatGPT-User an exception no other assistant fetcher gets. That allowance is a business decision, not a policy, and can be revoked in one commit.
  • The broken media sitemaps may be intentional. An S3 AccessDenied reproduced across three locales and four sitemap classes is either a long-standing misconfiguration or a deliberate withdrawal; planning a video index on the assumption they will return is planning on a guess.
  • Alias collapse changes without notice. Which towns fold into which covered city is a product decision inside the forecast system. If coverage expands, former aliases become canonical and cached place documents silently point at the wrong entity.

Inferences

  • The consumer site is an ad surface and the data a separate product. Basis: location-page JSON-LD contains zero forecast fields while api.weather.com returns CDN-0004 Missing apiKey — the numbers are routed away from the crawlable document.
  • The site wants live assistant citations without contributing training data. Basis: ChatGPT-User allowed while GPTBot blocked, and AmazonAdBot allowed while Amazonbot blocked, in the same file — the split is by fetch purpose.
  • Distinct en-US cities number roughly 29K rather than 30,538. Basis: the 40-URL canonical sample resolved to 29 distinct cities, a ~27% reduction; extrapolating is an estimate, not a measurement, which is why the demo above exists to replace it.
  • Comments are served by OpenWeb rather than natively. Basis: the homepage set amplitude-variants={"show-open-web-comments":{"value":"on"}} as a first-party cookie.

Open Questions

  • How many distinct cities does weather.com cover worldwide? Census all nine locale sitemaps and resolve every URL to its canonical, then deduplicate — the en-US half is the demo above; the other eight locales range from 13 to 88 child sitemaps and were not enumerated.
  • How large is the article archive? The sitemap exposes 50 URLs against a site publishing since 1995. Crawl the collections-* section hubs and the llms.txt map with pagination to establish a floor.
  • How many videos exist, and is there any legitimate bulk path? Ask TWC whether the vidfeed-* sitemaps are meant to be live; an S3 AccessDenied cannot distinguish misconfiguration from withdrawal.
  • What do the API contract terms permit — caching duration, redistribution, attribution? Neither the pricing page nor the developer docs state them; this needs the licence agreement behind the trial form.
  • Are the nine locales distinct city sets or translations of one set? No hreflang was present on sampled pages. Compare resolved canonical city sets across en-US, en-GB and en-IN for overlap.
  • What fraction of traffic is navigational versus search-referred? Needs a third-party panel; no first-party figure is published, and 330M MAU spans all properties rather than the domain.
