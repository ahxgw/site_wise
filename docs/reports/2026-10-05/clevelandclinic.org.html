Context

Cleveland Clinic is a nonprofit academic health system founded in 1921. Its site my.clevelandclinic.org does two jobs. It is a consumer medical reference (the Health Library: diseases, drugs, treatments, tests, symptoms, anatomy), and it is a patient-acquisition funnel (care pages, locations, doctor search, appointments). A sister host, Health Essentials, runs the wellness blog. The site earns indirectly, through care referrals and display ads ("Advertising on our site helps support our mission", on every Health Library page). For an index builder, the site differs from yesterday's Mayo Clinic in three ways. The edge lets every crawler in, AI bots included. The front end is being rebuilt as Next.js on a public Sanity dataset, so the podcast catalogue, with transcripts and SNOMED CT codes, can be read as JSON. And the contract is the strictest seen so far: the terms name retrieval-augmented generation as a prohibited use.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where clevelandclinic.org differs from the default platform assumptions.

Open door, closed contract

Unlike the default, robots.txt names no AI crawler and the edge serves ClaudeBot, GPTBot and CCBot the same 200 as Chrome; the provider host goes further and allowlists them by name. The restriction lives entirely in the terms, which ban RAG grounding and AI summarization specifically. A crawler that obeys robots.txt alone will ingest content it may not use for answers.

Use the visible review date, not dateModified

JSON-LD dateModified and sitemap lastmod track CMS edits (ad slots, layout migrations) and run a median 813 days ahead of the "Medically Reviewed. Last updated on" date. datePublished is also unreliable: one page shows a 2021 review date but a 2023 datePublished, which looks like a migration import date. Parse the visible review date for clinical freshness and use lastmod only as a recrawl trigger.

Numeric ID is optional

About a quarter of disease URLs and most procedure URLs have no numeric ID, and the ID is never exposed in JSON-LD or RSC props. Key on the canonical URL path; when the ID exists it also names the illustration file, which gives a free page-to-image join.

The CMS is the cleanest feed, for part of the site

The public Sanity dataset returns structured JSON for podcasts, care pages, patient stories and video pages, with SNOMED CT codes already attached: a coded concept join that no other health site profiled here has exposed. The Health Library and Health Essentials articles are not in it, so they still need HTML parsing.

Transcripts sit in the CMS, not at the video host

Mayo's video text was in Kaltura captions. Here Kaltura holds 0 captions, while Sanity holds speaker-segmented transcripts. The full-modal join is Sanity episode → kalturaId (duration, thumbnail) → transcript blocks → SNOMED terms → guest provider IDs.

Two stacks, one URL space

Next.js on the edge serves /health/*, podcasts and Health Essentials; Sitecore serves trials, locations and the /-/scassets/ media path. Sitecore pages carry no JSON-LD and are about 40KB. Use x-matched-path to tell the stacks apart and route each to its own parser.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the Health Library page split into its named sections (from RSC sectionTitles), carrying the visible review date and reference list. Other units: the podcast episode (transcript windows of speaker turns), the Health Essentials post, and the provider entity. Drug pages get title-and-link stubs only.
  • Primary keys & joins: canonical URL path; numeric page ID where present (equals the illustration filename); Sanity _id and canonicalUrl for CMS-backed pages; kalturaId for media; SNOMED CT concept IDs for topic joins to external vocabularies; the Kyruus 7-digit provider ID, which /staff/ redirects and podcast hostGuests links both resolve to.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Written permission or a licence from Cleveland Clinic. The terms require it for any automated access and forbid RAG use without it. 2) Sanity GROQ for podcast and care-page JSON: one paged query replaces about 2K page fetches. 3) Sitemaps for discovery and change detection, a few MB in total. 4) HTML at Crawl-delay: 10 for Health Library text, only under the permission in 1. Exclude the 4.1K Elsevier drug pages.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemaps daily (they are generated on request); refetch a page only when lastmod changes; Sanity _updatedAt diffs daily; providers weekly.
  • Derivatives to generate: section chunks with review-date metadata; transcript windows linked to the episode URL; a SNOMED-coded topic graph that joins podcast episodes to Health Library pages by concept; image-to-page links from the shared ID.
  • Demo to build: a SNOMED-keyed "listen and read" lookup. A query such as "prediabetes diet" resolves to a SNOMED concept, returns the matching transcript window from the Sanity episode and links the Health Library page on the same concept. Local, over a sample only.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms forbid the use case itself. Since 2025-05-14, grounding any AI model on the site "including through a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) model", or summarizing it in model output, is prohibited (Terms of use). The permissive robots.txt and edge do not override this. Without a licence, an answer engine can at most link to the site.
  • The open CMS may close without notice. Anonymous Sanity reads depend on a dataset visibility setting the owner can flip. Snapshot rather than depend on it live, and fall back to HTML.
  • Third-party content inside first-party URLs. 37% of the Health Library is Elsevier drug content and needs its own exclusion rule.
  • The migration will keep reshaping HTML. Sections moving from Sitecore to Next.js change size (40KB to 300KB+), schema and media paths; extractors must branch on x-matched-path.

Inferences

  • The Cloudflare 403 is a tooling-UA rule, not an AI-bot block. Basis: same URL, same egress, same minute: curl/8.7.1 got 403 while ClaudeBot, GPTBot, CCBot and an empty UA got the same 200 page as Chrome.
  • dateModified reflects CMS touches, not clinical review. Basis: it is later than the visible review date on 12 of 12 pages, matches sitemap lastmod on 15 of 15, and two unrelated drug pages share the same dateModified (2026-02-27).
  • Video podcasts are also published on YouTube, which may carry captions Kaltura lacks. Basis: the Kaltura category "On YouTube - Do not add" on the sampled entry.
