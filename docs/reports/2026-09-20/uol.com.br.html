Context

Universo Online launched in 1996 and is the content arm of Grupo Folha, the family group that also owns the newspaper Folha de S.Paulo. The legal entity, Universo Online S.A., consolidates PagBank, so its group revenue is a payments number; the media business sits in the parent-company column and earned R$885.5M net in 2024, now roughly three-quarters advertising and one-quarter a shrinking subscription base (see Verified Metrics). For an index builder the interesting thing is not the portal's size but how it sells access. In May 2026 Grupo Folha and Grupo UOL signed Brazil's first commercial content licence with OpenAI, settling a 2025 Folha lawsuit. That deal is legible in the crawl policy: Folha writes it as an explicit Allow: / for five OpenAI agents while blocking everyone else, and UOL achieves the same result by omission — 49 named crawler groups in robots.txt, not one of them an OpenAI agent (Probe Log). Crawl policy here is a contract surface, not a hygiene file.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where UOL differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The URL is a weak key — content lives on www.uol.com.br and on section subdomains with different availability. The strong key is the embedded mediaId: unique per article and self-dating, since its last eight characters are the publication date. Use it, not the slug, for dedup.

The extension is a licence tier

UOL splits articles across two extensions and prices them differently. .ghtm was free in all 346 sampled cases; .htm mixes free, paywalled and unstructured pages. Google-Extended's carve-out grants AI training rights over exactly the .ghtm set — a filename suffix doing the work of a rights field.

Retrieval unit

The article, keyed by mediaId, with mediaType carried as a first-class field: column means paywalled opinion, news means free reporting. An index that flattens the two will serve snippets of content the reader cannot open.

Discovery ceiling

The sitemaps are a ~70-hour rolling window covering eight lifestyle and sport verticals. Hard news, politics and economy have no sitemap at all. A sitemap-only crawler sees roughly three days of the softest part of the portal and none of the archive.

Two edges, two policies

www sits behind CloudFront and tolerated the session's full volume; the Akamai-fronted content subdomains cut over to a sticky 403 for all agents, robots.txt included. A crawler that cannot read robots.txt must treat that subdomain as disallowed, so aggressive crawling here locks you out of the very file that would have authorised you.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: article, keyed by mediaId, with mediaType, isAccessibleForFree and the URL extension stored as access-tier fields.
  • Primary keys & joins: mediaId → canonical URL → author /autor/ URL → image base (crop suffix stripped) → section from the breadcrumb.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) the eight news sitemaps, polled every 6h — free, complete for those verticals, but only ~3 days deep. 2) rss.home.uol.com.br/index.xml — 112 items with images and credits, catches front-page items the sitemaps miss. 3) Range-limited fetches (first 60KB) of every discovered URL for JSON-LD and mediaId at ~4% of full cost. 4) Full-body fetch only for .ghtm and for .htm pages marked free. 5) Hard news and economy — no sitemap; needs link-following from the homepage, against Akamai-fronted subdomains.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemaps 6-hourly; homepage hourly (10s edge TTL means it genuinely moves); article bodies once, since dateModified tracked datePublished; the toca monthly archive quarterly.
  • Derivatives to generate: crop-variant collapse on the image base (8:1 on the sampled lead photo), pt-BR embeddings, and an entity pass over football club and player names, which dominate the corpus by volume.
  • Demo to build: a Range-request classifier — walk the news sitemaps, pull only the first 60KB of each URL, and emit a JSONL index of mediaId, tier and access flag, reporting the bytes saved against a full crawl.

Risks to the Index

  • The policy wall can become a real one overnight. Today's 48 Disallow: / rules are unenforced (Probe Log), so an index built on generic crawling is running on a courtesy that costs UOL nothing to withdraw.
  • Volume triggers a sticky block that hides the rules. The Akamai 403 covered /robots.txt itself, leaving no compliant way to re-derive permission. Rate-limit per subdomain, not per site.
  • inferred Crawl policy tracks commercial deals, so it will keep moving. Basis: UOL names 49 crawler groups and no OpenAI agent, while sibling Folha grants five OpenAI agents an explicit Allow: / and blocks ClaudeBot — both observed 2026-09-20, both consistent with the May 2026 OpenAI licence and with no other AI licensee existing.
  • inferred The paywalled tier will grow. Basis: subscription revenue fell 19.5% in FY2024 while advertising rose 18.4% (Verified Metrics), and the paywall already maps onto the columnist franchise — 78 of 79 paid URLs under /colunas/. Pressure to convert runs toward metering more of the free .ghtm tier.
  • Sitemap coverage is a policy choice, not an accident. Eight lifestyle and sport verticals are listed; hard news is not. If that list shrinks further, sitemap-driven ingestion degrades without any error to alert on.

Inferences

Basis: zero matches for gptbot, oai-, chatgpt or openai in a robots.txt that names 49 other crawler groups, against Folha's explicit Allow: / for five OpenAI agents in the same group's estate, both observed 2026-09-20.
