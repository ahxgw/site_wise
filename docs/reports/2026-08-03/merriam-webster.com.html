Context

Merriam-Webster is the United States' default dictionary, owned since 1964 by Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc. and free on the web since 1996. The free site runs on display advertising plus an upsell to Merriam-Webster Unabridged; the corpus itself is also licensed through a developer API and print. For an index builder it is an unusual case: almost every technical signal says take this. Robots.txt carries seven narrow Disallow rules and names no AI crawler at all, a 113-file sitemap index enumerates 601K URLs, entry pages emit schema.org DefinedTerm, and pronunciation audio sits on an unauthenticated S3-backed CDN with a one-year cache header. The restraint is entirely legal rather than technical: Britannica and Merriam-Webster sued Perplexity in September 2025 and OpenAI in March 2026 over exactly the crawling and reproduction that robots.txt permits. inferred The premium corpus, not the free one, is the asset being protected. Basis: unabridged.merriam-webster.com/robots.txt returns a blanket Disallow: / while the free host publishes its full sitemap index (both observed in the Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Merriam-Webster differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The headword slug is the key: /dictionary/{word}, URL-encoded for apostrophes and diacritics (/dictionary/%27ava). One namespace per reference, so /dictionary/shock, /medical/shock and /legal/shock are three distinct objects about the same string — a dedup trap for anything keyed on the word alone. Inflected forms redirect to the lemma, so URL count overstates distinct headwords.

Retrieval unit

The entry page is too coarse and the word too fine. The useful unit is the sense: headword + part of speech + sense number + gloss + example. Nothing on the site exposes sense-level identity — JSON-LD stops at one DefinedTerm per page — so sense IDs must come from the API payload or be minted locally.

Cross-modal joins

Audio is the one real cross-modal join, and it is not derivable from the headword: serendipity maps to serend01.mp3 / serend02.mp3, with the subdirectory taken from the filename's first character. The mapping only exists inside the entry payload or its AudioObject markup, so the audio index cannot be built without first parsing every entry.

Authoritative content field

Unusually, the meta description is authoritative rather than promotional — it carries the actual first gloss ("the ability to find valuable or agreeable things not sought for"). SEO distortion lives instead in the FAQPage block, which is written for rich results and appends utm_campaign=rich_results to its own internal links.

Freshness & recrawl

changefreq: daily on 244K dictionary URLs is aspirational. The real cadence is a lexicographic release: 90% of dated dictionary entries share one July 2026 stamp, and 72% carry no lastmod at all. Poll the sitemap index for stamp movement rather than recrawling entries on their declared frequency.

Quality & spam

No UGC means no spam surface — an outlier among top-100 sites. The quality question inverts: a third of the crawlable corpus is combinatorially generated Word Finder pages that carry the brand's authority without its editorial process. An index that treats sitemap membership as an authority signal will over-rank them.

Internal vs external search

Lookup demand arrives pre-resolved from search engines straight to /dictionary/{word}, which is why the entry URL is a clean slug. Merriam-Webster reads its own lookup spikes as a cultural signal and ships them as a product: Word of the Year 2025 was slop, chosen largely from search-lookup data.

User query profile

Single-token, spelling-uncertain, definition-seeking — the shortest queries on the open web. This is also the query class generative assistants answer without a click, which is the injury both complaints assert.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the sense (headword + POS + sense number), with the entry page as parent document and the pronunciation clip as an attached media child.
  • Primary keys & joins: reference + headword-slug as the entry key; entry_key + pos + sense_index for senses; audio joined by the contentURL harvested per entry, never reconstructed from the headword.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Dictionary API — cleanest sense data, but 1,000/day/reference and non-commercial means 244K entries take ~8 months on a free key and production needs a licence first. 2) Sitemap-guided crawl of the 601K URLs — complete and unmetered, but Cloudflare-challenged, and the two active suits make unlicensed bulk ingestion a legal question rather than a technical one. 3) WOTD RSS for the daily increment — the only path unambiguously intended for third parties. 4) Audio CDN, fetched strictly from URLs discovered in 1–2.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemap index daily to detect release batches; entries only when their lastmod moves; WOTD daily; editorial monthly; Word Finder and taxonomy annually, if at all.
  • Derivatives to generate: sense-level embeddings for definition search; phoneme alignment between the respelling and the audio clip; a lemma↔inflection map recovered from redirect chains; a cross-reference graph over the five references keyed on shared headwords.
  • Demo to build: a sitemap-to-entry-card pipeline that samples N dictionary URLs, extracts the JSON-LD DefinedTerm and AudioObject, fetches the clip, and reports what fraction of entries actually expose pronunciation audio — closing the coverage question below.

Risks to the Index

  • Permissive robots.txt is not a licence. The owner is litigating against two AI companies over content its own robots.txt allows any crawler to fetch. An index built on "we obeyed robots.txt" has no defence here.
  • Free-tier terms bar the obvious use. The API licence is explicitly non-commercial and forbids "automated or recorded queries"; a commercial index must negotiate before ingesting, not after.
  • inferred The open posture may not survive the litigation. Basis: the site currently names zero AI crawlers in robots.txt and publishes no llms.txt (Probe Log), an unusual stance for a plaintiff in two AI copyright suits; the cheapest hardening step is adding the blocks its peers already have.
  • inferred Cloudflare, not robots.txt, is the operative gate on HTML. Basis: /dictionary/serendipity is allowed by robots.txt yet returned 403 cf-mitigated: challenge to both a browser UA and a Googlebot UA (Probe Log); challenge policy can tighten without any published change.
  • Ownership is in motion. Britannica Group confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1 on Jan 29 2024; a listing would put the licensing posture under public-market pressure.
