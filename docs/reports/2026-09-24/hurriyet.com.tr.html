Context

Sedat Simavi founded Hürriyet on 1 May 1948 (Wikipedia). In 1997 it became one of the first Turkish newspapers online. In March 2018 the Doğan Group sold all its media assets to the Demirören Group for $916M, largely financed by the state-owned Ziraat Bank (RSF Media Ownership Monitor). The publisher, Hürriyet Gazetecilik ve Matbaacılık A.Ş., is still listed on Borsa İstanbul, so it files a public annual report. That report is the source for the audience, revenue and video-output numbers below, which are unusually good primary data for a news site. The business is shrinking and still print-heavy: circulation and printing make up 61% of revenue and advertising 36%. For an index builder, the site's policy and its plumbing disagree. robots.txt blocks ten named AI and plagiarism crawlers but advertises no sitemap. /sitemap.xml redirects to an empty storage bucket. Every article still ships its complete body in JSON-LD to any user agent, and the homepage RSS feed is formatted for Microsoft's MSN ingestion, with a syndication-rights flag on every item.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Hürriyet differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The 8-digit number at the end of each URL is the key, and articles and Hürriyet TV videos share the counter. The key alone does not resolve an article, because the slug is required (Probe Log), so store the full canonical URL next to it. Image IDs are 24 hex characters whose first 8 characters encode the upload time, so images can be deduplicated and dated without fetching them.

No sitemap, by accident

/sitemap.xml still redirects to a storage path in a bucket named -jobs-prod, which suggests a generation job that stopped writing its output. Discovery therefore rests on RSS and on homepage and section link-following. Neither goes deep: feeds are curated mixes, and ?p=N does not paginate.

Authoritative content field

Use articleBody in JSON-LD: it is present on every sampled article and comes before any ad markup. Do not trust wordCount, which is higher than the body's word count on 56 of 65 articles. The RSS modified field is a null date, so use JSON-LD dateModified instead.

Sponsored content is labelled

Advertorials live under /advertorial/, carry the keyword Sponsorlu İçerik, and appear in the homepage feed as AdsContentListCard. Items under /resmi-ilanlar/ (7 of 76 feed items) are statutory public notices. Rank both below editorial content, or filter them out.

The feed is an MSN contract

The homepage RSS feed uses Microsoft's ingestion schema, with hasSyndicationRights = 1 on every item. This is the only machine-readable rights signal on the site, and it is addressed to one partner. Record it, but it gives a third party nothing.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, keyed by its numeric ID plus canonical URL, with section, author, keywords and an advertorial/notice flag. Recipes are a second unit type with structured ingredients.
  • Primary keys & joins: numeric ID → canonical URL → author Person name → image 24-hex ID (size segment stripped) → video htv_{ID} → section from BreadcrumbList.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Poll the homepage feed and the 8 category feeds every 30 minutes; this is free and returns about 660 URLs, with old items mixed in. 2) Fetch the first 64KB of each new URL by Range request; that covers JSON-LD with the full body on 65 of 65 articles, at about 72% of full-page bytes. 3) Follow links from the homepage and section pages to catch items the feeds miss. 4) Coverage of the archive older than about a week is open; there is no sitemap and no pagination.
  • Recrawl cadence: feeds every 30 minutes. Refetch each article once more after about 1 hour, since 42 of 66 were edited, almost all within minutes. Keep a 3-day tail for the slow edits. Images and video never need refetching.
  • Derivatives to generate: ASR on Hürriyet TV clips (short, 480p, clean audio from the newsroom), Turkish embeddings of articleBody, and a recipe entity table from the Recipe blocks.
  • Demo to build: an ID-gap monitor. Poll the feeds, record every numeric ID seen, and chart the share of the ~1,660/day ID sequence that feed discovery actually reaches. That share measures how much of the site the missing sitemap hides.

Risks to the Index

  • The robots blocks could start being enforced at the edge. The ten Disallow: / groups are not enforced today (Probe Log). An index that relies on generic crawling depends on a CDN rule Demirören could switch on at any time.
  • A fixed sitemap would change the crawl overnight. If the bucket job is repaired, the sitemap becomes the best discovery path. Check /sitemap.xml weekly instead of assuming it is dead.
  • inferred The digital product is likely to be cut back rather than expanded. Basis: revenue fell 13% in inflation-adjusted terms, and advertising dropped from 38% to 36% of the total (Verified Metrics). The video feed has not updated since 2026-04-29, and the sitemap job is broken. Both are signs of maintenance debt, which suggests feeds and endpoints may disappear quietly.
  • Ownership affects source weighting. The owner is politically aligned and bought the group with state-bank financing (Context). A news index should treat Hürriyet as one voice among several and not as a neutral reference for Turkish politics.

Inferences

  • Articles and videos share one global ID counter, so the ID rate overstates the number of articles. Basis: video htv_43317348 sits 35 IDs below article 43317383 in the same feed, and the ID rate (about 1,660/day) is far above the size of the homepage feed.
  • Image IDs are MongoDB-style ObjectIds with an embedded timestamp. Basis: 24 hex characters whose first 4 bytes decode to 18:27:20Z, 20 seconds after the item's pubDate.
  • The sitemap is broken, not deliberately removed. Basis: the redirect still exists and points into a bucket named hurriyet-jobs-prod that returns NoSuchKey. A deliberate removal would more likely drop the redirect too.

Open Questions

  • Total archive size and how far back it goes. Could be closed by a site:hurriyet.com.tr count, by sampling the Wayback CDX API for numeric-ID URLs, or by probing low IDs with slugs from archived pages.
  • Articles per day as distinct from all IDs. Could be closed by polling all feeds and the homepage for a week and counting distinct non-video IDs against the ID sequence.
  • What /api/ serves Flipboard. It is disallowed to everyone else and was not probed; it could be closed only by asking Hürriyet or Flipboard.
  • Photo licensing. media:credit is empty in the feed; this could be closed by checking in-page captions for agency credits (DHA, AA, Reuters) across a sample of articles.
  • Whether Google News gets a private news sitemap through Search Console. Not observable from outside; it could be closed only by the publisher.
  • Access terms for bigpara.hurriyet.com.tr market data. Could be closed by a separate robots.txt, header and feed probe of that host.
