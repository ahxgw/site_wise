Context

Transfermarkt is the Hamburg-based football database best known for its community-set player market values, plus transfers, rumours, squads, fixtures, injuries and a large forum. transfermarkt.com.tr is the Turkish edition: the same entity database rendered in Turkish, with its own Turkish news desk and machine-translated copies of other editions' forum threads. Revenue is advertising. For an index builder, the site is an entity graph, not a document collection. Players, clubs, competitions, matches and transfers carry stable numeric IDs shared by all 27 editions, so one crawl of one edition gets the graph and the other editions only add labels and local news. The catch is access. There is no sitemap, the clean JSON backend is closed by robots.txt, the terms ban bots and AI training, and the origin rate-limits at a few dozen pages a minute (Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Transfermarkt differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Entity IDs are shared by every edition: /x/profil/spieler/401923 301s to the right slug on any of the 27 hosts. Key on (type, ID) and drop the host and slug. News IDs are global too, but each article lives on one language group; a wrong-edition ID redirects to the homepage. Missing players 302 to a "most valuable players" page, so check the redirect target before counting a 200.

Retrieval unit

The entity profile (player, club, competition), with time series attached: market-value history, transfer history, absences and season performance. For questions like "how much was X worth when he moved to Y", the useful unit is the transfer event. It joins two clubs, two coaches, a fee, a market value and a contract end on one date (fields from the tmapi transfer sample).

Cross-modal joins

Player ID ↔ portrait filename prefix ↔ news keywords (player names, not IDs) ↔ forum board per player (<tm-player-discussions-widget playerId>). Competition codes (TR1, IT1) are human-readable and stable. There is no external ID (Wikidata, FIFA) in page markup. inferred Cross-site joins will rest on Wikidata's Transfermarkt player-ID property, not on anything Transfermarkt publishes. Basis: none of the 7 sampled page types and none of the tmapi player fields contain an external identifier.

Authoritative content field

Market values are Transfermarkt's own editorial and community estimate, not a price, and every edition shows them in EUR. On Turkish forum pages the translated title is a derivative; the German post body and the .de canonical are the originals. JSON-LD is unreliable as a source: blank authors, malformed image URLs and 1970 dateModified values (Probe Log).

Freshness & recrawl

Without sitemaps or lastmod, change detection has to come from Transfermarkt's own list pages (latest transfers, market-value updates, the neuestetransfers and marktwertetop lists) and from RSS for news. Origin caching is short (120–600s), so the cost is the rate limit, not cache staleness. New player IDs appear at the top of the sequence; poll above the last known ceiling.

Internal vs external search

On-site search (/schnellsuche) is name lookup. External search is the main way in: "{player} transfermarkt" is a navigational query and the slug carries the name. Each edition ranks for its own language, so the Turkish host exists mainly to win Turkish-language queries on the shared database.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: entity profile (player / club / competition) with attached time series; transfer events and matches as first-class records; news articles and forum posts as text satellites linked by entity.
  • Primary keys & joins: player:{id}, club:{id}, competition:{code}, match:{id}, transfer:{id}, news:{id}, thread:{id}. The edition host is only a label locale.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked:
    1. A licence agreement with Transfermarkt for the entity database. The terms forbid bots and reserve TDM, so this is the only clean route at scale.
    2. For permitted, low-volume use: a paced HTML crawl of one edition by ID enumeration. 1.61M player IDs at ~20 requests/min is about 56 days for one pass of player profiles alone (calculated by Site Wise). Other editions are fetched only for localized names.
    3. RSS on all 10 feed editions for news discovery, plus article fetches.
    4. Wikidata's Transfermarkt ID property as an external seed and join table, so no enumeration is needed for notable players.
    tmapi is not a path: robots.txt disallows it, even though it answers anonymously.
  • Recrawl cadence: news daily (the RSS window is ~1.5 days). Transfer and market-value list pages daily during windows, weekly otherwise. Match pages after full time. The full player pass yearly, given the rate limit.
  • Derivatives to generate: a per-player valuation time series and transfer graph (club → club edges with fee and value); Turkish ↔ German ↔ English entity-name aliases from the shared IDs. No ASR or OCR targets: there is no video, and images are small agency photos.
  • Demo to build: the cross-edition alias table. Fetch one player ID on all 27 hosts at a polite pace, collect the localized title and slug, and measure how many editions add a distinct name form. That tests whether multi-edition crawling earns anything beyond multilingual labels.

Risks to the Index

  • Contract over robots: robots.txt allows all crawlers, but §11.1 bans automated access and AI use and reserves TDM. An index that relies on robots.txt alone is out of compliance with the terms. Under §44b UrhG a machine-readable reservation is what counts, and the prose reservation's legal weight is open.
  • Silent rate-limit corruption: the 403 has an empty body and also hits pages that exist. A naive crawler records live entities as missing; in this session the first density pass came back almost all 403s. Treat every 403 as "retry later", never as absent.
  • Backend churn: the page widgets moved data out of the HTML into Svelte components backed by tmapi. inferred More profile data may leave server-rendered HTML over time, shrinking the robots-allowed surface. Basis: 22 custom elements on a player page, and the market-value chart and transfer history now come from tmapi JSON rather than page markup.
  • Image rights: portraits and news photos come from the IMAGO agency (Probe Log). Thumbnails in a third-party UI need a separate licence.

Open Questions

  • Counts of clubs, coaches, agents and transfers. Closeable with the same paced ID-density method on /startseite/verein/{id} and other ID families (~100 samples each).
  • New-player creation rate. Closeable by re-running the ID ceiling search on a later date and diffing against 1,611,357.
