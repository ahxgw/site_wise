Context

tabelog.com (食べログ) is Japan's dominant restaurant-review and reservation site, run by Kakaku.com, Inc. (TSE 2371), which also operates the price-comparison site 価格.com. Restaurants pay for reservation tools and listing ads, and that segment is now Kakaku's largest and most profitable. Ownership is changing: EQT-backed Kamgras 1 is running a recommended tender offer to take Kakaku private. The tender closes on Nov 12 2026, and the buyer's stated aim is a platform "not replaceable by AI", less dependent on SEO traffic. For an index builder the site is unusually legible. Every restaurant URL encodes prefecture, area code and a numeric ID, and the restaurant pages carry rich JSON-LD with geo coordinates, rating, price band and phone. A root sitemap.xml, unlisted in robots.txt, enumerates every restaurant and review. Access, however, is tiered by language and crawler. English and other inbound-language pages answer an honest research UA, but the Japanese originals, which hold the full address and review text, return a site-branded 403. Japanese copyright law (Art. 30-4) permits information analysis, but the terms of use prohibit storing site information without consent.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where tabelog.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Restaurant URLs are /{pref}/{A-area}/{A-subarea}/{rst_id}/; the integer rst_id is the key and its leading digits encode prefecture (13… Tokyo, 27… Osaka, 70… Netherlands). Language editions share the ID under /en/, /kr/ and so on, with hreflang. Reviews are …/{rst_id}/dtlrvwlst/B{review_id}/, so every review carries its parent restaurant. Collapse on (rst_id) and (B-id), never on language path.

Language-tiered access

The block is not site-wide. Inbound-language pages (built for tourists) answer at 200, while the Japanese originals return a branded 403 that impersonates an overload, even to CCBot (Probe Log). The inbound edition withholds the street address and full reviews. A non-Google index therefore gets a tourist-grade entity record: geo, cuisine, price, rating and phone, without the review corpus.

Hidden sitemap

robots.txt names no sitemap, yet /sitemap.xml is a 2,527-part index with per-URL lastmod. That makes it the cheapest complete census in this series: ~13 full fetches resolve both corpus totals, because review parts are published only when full (newest parts hold exactly 50K).

Authoritative content field

Use JSON-LD over page text. The EN telephone is internationalized (+81) while JP is local, and EN servesCuisine is translated. Reviews on EN pages are machine translations labelled as such, so index the Japanese original as the authoritative text and the translation as a derivative.

Freshness & recrawl

Per-URL lastmod is real: restaurant dates span 2010–2026 rather than all reading "today". Diff the restaurant sitemaps weekly and the newest two review parts per family daily. The B-ID counter (~538M) runs far ahead of the 37M listed reviews, so the ID space is shared or sparse; do not estimate volume from IDs.

Quality & spam

Tabelog's 3.00–5.00 score sits on a different scale from Google or Tripadvisor stars (the sample has 3.59 labelled "Very good"). Treat the score as a Tabelog-specific signal. Do not merge it with Google or Tripadvisor stars.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the restaurant (rst_id), with geo point, cuisine list, price band, rating and phone, plus reviews (B-ID) and photos as children, if licensed.
  • Primary keys & joins: rst_id across language editions; geo and phone to join with OSM, Google Places and Tripadvisor; area codes (A1303) and station IDs (R1528) from BreadcrumbList as a gazetteer.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A Kakaku data licence for reviews, which is the only route to the 37M-review corpus; contract terms may shift under new ownership. 2) The root sitemap, weekly, for the full ID graph and lastmod (~2.5K files). 3) /en/ detail pages, paced, for entity facts; storing them is a ToS question to clear first. 4) Wayback id_ captures for one-off Japanese research samples.
  • Recrawl cadence: newest review parts daily; restaurant parts weekly; robots.txt and the 403 behaviour monthly.
  • Derivatives to generate: a restaurant gazetteer (ID → geo, area, station); multilingual entity embeddings from the five inbound editions; photo embeddings from 640px thumbnails, under licence only.
  • Demo to build: a review-density map built from sitemap URLs alone. Count review permalinks per rst_id and plot them by prefecture code to show where Tabelog's 37M reviews concentrate, with no page fetches.
