Context

Mercado Livre is the Brazilian storefront of MercadoLibre, Inc. (Nasdaq: MELI), founded in Buenos Aires in August 1999 and now Latin America's largest commerce platform. Brazil is its biggest market: $5.5B of the company's $10.2B in Q2 2026 net revenues. Four businesses meet on the site — marketplace, payments and credit (Mercado Pago), logistics (Mercado Envios), and an ad system that just crossed 10% of LatAm digital advertising — with a free ad-supported streaming service, Mercado Play, on a sibling host. For an index builder the site splits unusually sharply, and that split is why this report exists: the ontology — 31 top-level categories, thousands of nodes, hundreds of typed attributes per leaf — is served to anyone without a token, while every instance keyed to it sits behind OAuth or an account-verification redirect. You can mirror the schema of Brazilian commerce for free, and none of the inventory.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Mercado Livre differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Every object carries its country in the ID: MLB for Brazil, MLA Argentina, MLM Mexico, on one shared API host. The same physical product therefore has a different ID per country and no cross-border key is published. Within Brazil there are two layers to dedup — the seller listing (MLB-123456789 on produto.) and the catalog product (/p/MLB1055309028) that many listings compete on. Treating them as one object silently merges dozens of offers.

Retrieval unit

The catalog product is the right search unit and the listing the right price unit, but only the ontology above them is fetchable. An unauthenticated index can hold the category and attribute layer complete, and the item layer not at all.

Cross-modal joins

The join keys are published even though the objects are not: category_id, domain_id (MLB-CELLPHONES), and attribute IDs (BRAND, MODEL, INTERNAL_MEMORY) come back from open endpoints with their value types and hierarchy roles. domain_discovery/search maps free text to a domain and category, so you can classify an external product into Mercado Livre's taxonomy without ever reading a listing.

Authoritative content field

Typed attributes beat titles here more than on most marketplaces: the schema marks each attribute required or catalog_required and validates on listing, so brand and model are enforced fields rather than free text. Stock is the field never to trust — it is deliberately bucketed to a range floor even for token holders (Verified Metrics).

Freshness & recrawl

Three distinct clocks: taxonomy changes on the order of months, category counters are cached rather than live (Probe Log), and prices move continuously but are unreachable without a token. There is no sitemap and no feed, so there is also no change signal — an index has to poll or subscribe to seller notifications.

Quality & spam

Because catalog products aggregate many sellers, the manipulation surface is offer-level rather than page-level: hijacking a well-reviewed catalog product, or listing in a mismatched category to escape competition. The published catalog_required attribute flags are the platform's own defence and are reusable as validation rules by an external index.

Internal vs external search

Inverted from the usual pattern: search listings and product pages are the parts hidden from crawlers, while marketing surfaces — category hubs, deals, brand stores, the blog — render freely. With no sitemap and /mais-vendidos/MLB blocked, search engines get the storefront and not the catalogue.

User query profile

Portuguese, price-led, and weighted to replacement parts: auto accessories alone are 32% of listings (Verified Metrics), implying fitment queries — vehicle make/model/year matched against a part — rather than the brand-and-model queries a general marketplace sees.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: catalog product (/p/MLB…) for search; seller listing (MLB-…) as an attached offer record; category node as a first-class object in its own right, because it is the only one obtainable in bulk.
  • Primary keys & joins: MLB-prefixed IDs are country-scoped, so key everything on (site_id, object_id). Join items to the taxonomy on category_id, to the schema on domain_id, and to external catalogues on the BRAND/MODEL/GTIN attribute triple.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Unauthenticated categories + attributes API — free, complete, no quota, but ontology only. 2) OAuth item and search endpoints — the only route to inventory; per-Client-ID limits, unpublished, negotiable. 3) Public crawl of category hubs, deals and brand stores — ~30 product links per page with prices in HTML, no structured data, no sitemap to enumerate from. 4) Search listings and product pages — closed behind the account-verification wall for Googlebot too. 5) Mercado Play — browser rendering only, no metadata without JS.
  • Recrawl cadence: taxonomy monthly; category counters daily at most, since they are cached; item and price data only as fast as your granted quota allows, prioritised by category velocity rather than uniformly.
  • Derivatives to generate: an attribute-schema diff to detect taxonomy drift; a text→category classifier trained on domain_discovery responses; OCR on listing photos to recover spec text that sellers omit from typed fields.
  • Demo to build: the category census itself — walk the open endpoint, emit a category tree with live listing counts, and render the treemap that reproduces the 326.5M figure and the 32% auto-parts share. It is the rare case where a full-site size estimate needs no credentials at all.

Risks to the Index

  • The open taxonomy endpoints are the anomaly, not the policy. /sites/MLB/categories — the documented root of the same tree — already returns 403 while /categories/{id} does not. The free census depends on that inconsistency surviving.
  • Documented endpoints are being retired mid-flight. The Items & Searches docs list /sites/$SITE_ID/search?seller_id= as replaced by a token-scoped per-user endpoint, and ?nickname= as having "no replacement" — deprecation without migration.
  • Data is coarsened at the source. Bucketed available_quantity (Verified Metrics) means even an authorised index cannot compute true inventory; any stock-based ranking signal is bounded by that.
  • inferred AI retrieval is being handled by blocking rather than licensing. Basis: robots.txt puts seven AI agents — including Amazonbot and both the crawler and user-agent variants of ClaudeBot, GPTBot and PerplexityBot — at Disallow: / while explicitly allowing four social preview bots (observed 2026-08-25), and no AI licensing programme appears on the developer portal.
  • inferred Search-engine coverage of the catalogue is thin and shrinking. Basis: no Sitemap: directive in robots.txt, no schema.org Product or Offer markup on any sampled page, and product pages redirecting a Googlebot UA to the account-verification wall — all observed 2026-08-25.

Open Questions

  • How often the total_items_in_this_category counters refresh — closeable by polling one volatile category hourly for a week and finding the step interval.
  • Whether an ordinary OAuth application can read reviews and Q&A for listings it does not own — needs a registered app and a call to /reviews/item/{id} with a token.
  • The actual numeric rate limit per Client ID — the docs state the enforcement dimension but publish no figure; only an app in production reveals it.
  • Seller count and listing count for Brazil specifically — MELI reports buyers and items sold company-wide but no seller or SKU count by country.
  • Mercado Play catalogue size and whether any metadata endpoint exists — the page is client-rendered, so this needs a rendered-DOM crawl or an XHR trace.
  • Whether listing photos on http2.mlstatic.com may be displayed by third parties — no robots.txt and no published media policy exist for that host.
