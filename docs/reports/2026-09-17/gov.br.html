Daily Site Profile

gov.br

gov.br walls its HTML behind a WAF, then publishes Brazil's entire 5,711-service federal catalog as one unauthenticated 41MB JSON file.

Evidence: verified inferred (must state basis) open — conventions in the methodology.

Context

Decreto 9.756 of Apr 11 2019 ordered every federal executive agency onto a single portal and banned new .gov.br registrations from that July, folding roughly 1,600 sites into one Plone installation run by the Secretaria de Governo Digital and hosted by SERPRO. There is no business model: no ads, no paid tier, no licensing program. For an index builder the site inverts the usual shape: the crawlable HTML is the worst path, while an unadvertised, unauthenticated endpoint on a SERPRO host returns the whole service catalog as structured JSON, rebuilt daily, carrying millions of citizen ratings that appear nowhere on the website. The binding constraint is not access but license — every page declares CC BY-ND, permitting verbatim copying and forbidding adaptation, the reverse of the usual arrangement.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where gov.br departs from the default platform assumptions. It departs on nearly all of them.

The bulk path is open and the crawl is the obstacle

The default assumption is that bulk access is the metered privilege and crawling the fallback. Here it is reversed: the crawl is obstructed while the dump is anonymous, complete and daily, advertised nowhere but inside a Conecta catalog entry. Nor is the obstruction defending anything — robots.txt names no AI crawler, there is no key to buy, and the content is CC-licensed. inferred The wall is unmaintained infrastructure and the dump a build artifact, not a published product. Basis: the 403 falls on the exact sitemap URL robots.txt advertises and the F5 challenge fires on sitemaps the platform itself generates; the dump is served by nginx/1.22.1 while the portal answers as Zope/Plone, with no version envelope and a whole-file Last-Modified.

License is the real constraint, and it points the wrong way

CC BY-ND 3.0 permits verbatim copying and redistribution, including commercially, but forbids distributing adapted material. An index that stores and serves generated summaries, translations or rewritten answers produces exactly what NoDerivatives excludes; a snippet-and-link index is squarely permitted. Most platforms ban the copying and tolerate the summarizing — gov.br is the reverse. The open-data half of the property does not inherit this: dados.gov.br runs on Decreto 8.777/2016 instead, so two arms of the same government carry different reuse regimes.

Identifiers & joins

The web surface has no ID — the slug is the key, and Plone view suffixes alias it ({path}, {path}/view, {path}/@@download/file). The dump supplies what the pages lack: a numeric servicoId, a SIORG agency code, and an orgao.id that dereferences into the federal organizational-structure dataset. Key on those, not URLs. The dump's url field joins back to the crawled page, which is what makes the two corpora composable. The missing join is document-to-service: a PDF sits in an agency's folder tree, not under the service it belongs to, so that link must come from the referring page's anchor text.

Retrieval unit & query shape

Two units. The service is the high-value unit — 5,711 records that answer a citizen's actual question, each already decomposed into ordered steps with their own documents, costs and channels, so a step is independently retrievable. The document is the high-volume unit: 23,893 PDFs holding the regulations and forms the records merely cite. Indexing only the HTML gets neither. Queries here are task-shaped, not topic-shaped — how to obtain, renew or appeal something, named by the document (CPF, CNH, INSS) rather than the agency — and the right answer is one service record plus its entry link, which 76.6% of them carry as a field.

Freshness & recrawl

Two clocks. The dump moves daily and supports conditional GET, so the structured corpus costs one request per day. The HTML lastmod coverage is complete and appears genuine — the distribution spreads across 2014–2026 rather than collapsing onto a build date — so it can drive recrawl, and it exposes a dormant quarter untouched since 2020. Recrawl by lastmod age; treat that quarter as archival.

Quality & spam

No spam surface: no user-generated content, no ranking incentive, no commercial submitters. The quality signal is the ratings instead — 49.1% positive is a usable per-service prior, and one the site never shows a visitor. The real defects are staleness and cross-agency duplication of the same guidance; prefer the publishing agency's copy and trust lastmod.

Internal vs external search

On-site search renders client-side from a SERPRO recommendation service (api.recomgov…serpro.gov.br, Probe Log), so the search endpoint is not an enumeration path. inferred External search carries most discovery. Basis: the site-wide WebSite JSON-LD ships a SearchAction template for /search?SearchableText=, which exists to hand query handling to external engines.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the service record from the dump, with its steps as retrievable sub-units; the PDF corpus as a second unit joined by referring-page anchors.
  • Primary keys & joins: numeric servicoId as the primary key; the record's url field as the join to crawled HTML; SIORG code and the estruturaorganizacional.dados.gov.br URI as the agency key; canonical paths with Plone view suffixes stripped for the page corpus.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The bulk dump — one anonymous 41MB GET for the whole structured catalog including ratings; nothing else is close. 2) servicos.gov.br/api/v1/ per-record reads between dumps. 3) Per-subsite sitemap.xml.gz for the page and document corpus — free and lastmod-complete, but only 5 of 52 subsites. 4) Plain GET link-following for the 33+ without one. 5) dados.gov.br for datasets, after registering a key.
  • Recrawl cadence: conditional GET on the dump daily — one request keeps 5,711 records current. HTML by lastmod age: touched within a year weekly, the pre-2020 quarter quarterly. PDFs once, then only on lastmod change.
  • Derivatives to generate: OCR and layout extraction over the 23,893 PDFs, where the regulatory text actually lives; the GovernmentService JSON-LD the publisher never emits, built from dump fields; a document-to-service link table from anchor text; quality priors from the ratings. Keep these internal and serve verbatim snippets — generated summaries sit outside the CC BY-ND grant.
  • Demo to build: a dump-vs-page differ — fetch a service both ways and show, field by field, what the JSON carries that the rendered page does not, with the 4.86M ratings as the headline gap.

Risks to the Index

  • The best path is undocumented, so it promises nothing. The dump is not in robots.txt, not linked from the portal, and has no version envelope. It can move or close with no deprecation notice. Monitor it as infrastructure, not an API, and keep the sitemap path warm as a fallback.
  • The license constrains the product, not just the crawl. CC BY-ND 3.0 is declared site-wide, so the exposure sits in what an index serves rather than in what it fetches — see Retrieval Notes.
  • A whole corpus can go dark behind HTTP 200. Every gov.br news item currently returns a success status with an authentication gate in the body (Probe Log), in the run-up to Brazil's Oct 2026 general election. A crawler trusting status codes will index thousands of gate pages as content and silently lose the news corpus. Validate bodies, not statuses.
  • The open-data half is closing while the website stays open. Both dados.gov.br endpoints now require a key, documented as a global requirement over all 20 paths — the structured surface trending away from anonymous access as the HTML surface stays permissive.
  • inferred A re-platform would void every path assumption here. Basis: the server banner reports Zope 2.13.28 on Python 2.7.16, a runtime that reached end of life in 2020; the @@ views, search_rss endpoints and sitemap.xml.gz naming this report depends on are all Plone-specific and would not survive it.
