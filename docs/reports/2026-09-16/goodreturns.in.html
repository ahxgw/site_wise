Context

goodreturns.in is the business and personal-finance portal of Oneindia, run by Greynium Information Technologies Pvt. Ltd. of Bengaluru, and it publishes in six languages — English plus Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and Kannada (about page). Revenue is programmatic display advertising: its ads.txt declares 829 distinct ad systems (see Verified Metrics). The site holds two very different corpora. One is editorial — 87K English articles plus 216K in the language editions, back to 2011. The other is a database layer: gold, silver and platinum rates per city, fuel prices, IFSC codes, pin codes, toll plazas, mutual-fund schemes, and about thirty calculators. For an index builder the interesting fact is that these two layers are governed in opposite directions. inferred Greynium treats editorial as the crawlable asset and the rate database as an on-site-only asset. Basis: none of the twelve database path prefixes tested appears in any of the nine English sitemaps, while 98.5% of sitemap entries are article paths, and the Googlebot-News block disallows the same rate and tool surface by name (all three measurements in the Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where goodreturns.in departs from the default platform assumptions.

The AI-crawler default is inverted

The default assumption is that robots.txt blocks AI crawlers. Here all seven named AI agents get an explicit Allow: /, and the UA probe confirms ClaudeBot and GPTBot receive the identical page a browser does. The wall points at unidentified clients instead — a bare curl UA gets 403. Politeness, not permission, is the binding constraint.

The SEO sliver runs backwards

Usually the curated sliver is reserved for approved crawlers. Here Googlebot-News is the restricted agent: 46 Disallows fence it out of rates, calculators, IFSC, pin codes, stocks, IPOs and partner content — all of which stay open to generic crawl. inferred These are news-policy compliance, not access control. Basis: every excluded path is a database or tool page rather than a dated article, and none carries a Disallow for * or any AI agent.

Sitemaps cover the archive, not the product

98.5% of sitemap entries are articles, and not one of the twelve database prefixes tested appears in them. An index built from sitemaps alone gets the commentary about gold prices and none of the gold prices; the rate layer needs its own hub-and-spoke crawler seeded from the category nav.

Crawl economics

At 1.3%–3.1% text yield, this corpus costs roughly 50× its useful bytes to fetch — about 200GB of HTTP to pull 317K pages once. Fetch and extract in one pass; never store raw HTML.

Identifier & dedup

Articles end in a 6–7 digit numeric ID (...-1534689.html) that survives slug rewrites; MF pages end in a lowercased ISIN (...-inf846k01k01.html → INF846K01K01) that joins straight to AMFI and any other fund dataset. Both beat the URL as keys. Strip ?utm and ?ref, already disallowed in robots.txt.

Cross-modal and cross-language joins

Language editions are linked by hreflang on rate pages (six locales, path-identical) and by nothing at all on articles. inferred The database layer is one multilingual entity set while the news layer is five independent corpora. Basis: six hreflang links on /gold-rates/ versus zero on the sampled article and about page, and the language hosts carry 216K sitemap URLs of their own rather than mirroring the English IDs.

Authoritative content field

JSON-LD gives a clean headline, description, author URL and both timestamps but omits articleBody — the body still needs DOM extraction from a 500KB+ ad shell. On MF pages the reverse holds: FAQPage answer text is the cleanest copy of NAV and AUM. Those titles embed the live NAV (NAV Today [117.66]), a freshness trap for a text index.

Freshness by object class

Three regimes, not one: news minutes-to-hours, rates daily, NAV a batch five days stale on the probe date. A site-wide interval is wrong for all three.

User query profile

Lookup, not discovery: "gold rate today Chennai", "IFSC code for X branch", "<fund name> NAV". These want a current value, not a document — exactly the layer the sitemaps omit and the terms restrict.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: two. The article (numeric ID, one per language host) for editorial, and the quoted value (metal × purity × city × date, or ISIN × date) for the database layer. Indexing a rate page as a document loses the only thing anyone queries it for.
  • Primary keys & joins: host + numeric article ID; ISIN for funds; (metal, purity, city, date) for rates; author.url slug for bylines. ISIN is the one key that joins outward to AMFI and to other finance sources.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Sitemap crawl with lastmod diffing — 317K URLs, full archive, no auth, editorial only. 2) The 16 RSS feeds for low-latency discovery; headlines only, so pair with a body fetch. 3) A hub-and-spoke crawler over the rate and reference DB seeded from the category nav — the only route to what the sitemaps omit. 4) Nothing else: no public API, no bulk dump, and the rate JSON endpoints are robots-disallowed.
  • Recrawl cadence: news sitemap every 30 minutes (~23 items/day); rate hubs and city pages daily after the morning restamp; MF schemes weekly, since the NAV batch itself lags; article archive quarterly, driven by lastmod.
  • Derivatives to generate: body extraction is mandatory at a 1.6% text ratio. Do not generate thumbnails — the CDN serves any WxH/ on demand with a 1-year cache, so store the URL template. Normalise rate tables into a time series; that is the reusable asset. ASR and OCR are not worth it for 101 videos.
  • Demo to build: a rate-table time-series extractor — crawl the 168 gold city pages daily, parse each HTML table into (city, purity, date, price) rows, and chart how far the index drifts after one missed crawl. It exercises exactly the surface no sitemap advertises.

Risks to the Index

  • robots.txt and the terms disagree. Seven AI crawlers are allowed by name; the linked terms permit only personal non-commercial use with no derivative works. An indexer relying on the robots signal is relying on the weaker document.
  • inferred The terms may not even be scoped to this host. Basis: the Greynium terms page binds the reader "by using this website www.greynium.com" and names no other property, yet it is the only terms document goodreturns.in links; /terms-of-use.html on goodreturns.in itself returns 404 (Probe Log).
  • The UA gate is the fragile dependency. Access rests on Cloudflare bot rules, not documented policy; the 403 for generic UAs can be widened to cover declared AI agents with no notice and no announcement surface.
  • inferred Ad-tech bloat is likely to grow, not shrink. Basis: 829 declared ad systems and a 1.3%–3.1% text ratio against an origin still advertising x-powered-by: PHP/7.4.21 — monetisation layered onto an end-of-life stack rather than the page rebuilt. Budget crawl cost upward.
  • Derived financial values carry someone else's errors. NAV, AUM and metal rates are restatements, and the malformed uploadDate in VideoObject shows the templating is unvalidated. Range-check every numeric field before it enters the index.
