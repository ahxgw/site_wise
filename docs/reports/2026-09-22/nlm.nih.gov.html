Context

The National Library of Medicine is an NIH institute and the world's largest biomedical library. It runs on appropriation, carries no ads and no paywall, and its subdomains are the asset: ncbi holds 38 Entrez databases, pubmed the citation index, pmc the full-text archive. This inverts the commercial pattern — with no revenue to protect the data is given away in bulk, and with no growth incentive the plumbing is where friction lives. Access is rationed not by price but by which enumeration paths happen to work. inferred NLM optimises for the bulk downloader, not the crawler. Basis: PubMed's declared sitemap is an empty <sitemapindex> and its record pages return a 203 challenge to every UA tried including Googlebot, while the same records ship as a 51.77 GB FTP dump and answer unauthenticated efetch calls (Probe Log).

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where nlm.nih.gov differs from the default platform assumptions.

Crawl policy

The default fails in both directions. No AI crawler is named on any literature host — permission by omission across 41.18M citations — while the one host that does name GPTBot and anthropic-ai blocks them, and it guards digitised books rather than current science. The binding constraint is not robots.txt at all: PubMed's record pages are shut by a 203 challenge robots.txt never mentions and Googlebot does not escape.

Two tracks, inverted

The usual split — an SEO sliver plus APIs metered by revenue — does not apply: no revenue meter, no partner tier, and a free key only raises 3/s to 10/s. What replaces it is a plumbing meter. The API answers any question about one record and refuses to enumerate past 9,999, so the corpus is reachable only by taking it whole. Access is free; the toll is 51.77 GB.

Identifier & dedup

Unusually clean: PMID, PMCID and DOI are external, stable, and reconciled for you in a table rebuilt daily. Two traps — nucleotide and nuccore are the same records under two names, so 12% of the headline corpus is one alias; and S3 keys carry a version suffix, so the PMCID alone is not the key, PMCID + version is.

Authoritative content field

Inverted: the rendered page is the weakest artefact — PubMed's does not render, PMC's carries no JSON-LD. Authority runs JATS XML → .txt rendering → HTML. Where efetch and PMC disagree, PMC XML wins on body content and the PubMed record on indexing metadata, since MeSH terms exist only there.

Quality & trust

No spam surface — every record is publisher-deposited and there is no UGC — so the usual manipulation analysis is moot. Quality signals are instead shipped as fields: is_retracted in each S3 sidecar, the retracted and preprint publication types counted in Verified Metrics, and medline[sb] separating curated records from citation-only ones. An index ignoring these serves retracted findings as current.

Freshness & licensing granularity

The rare site that tells you how stale it is: einfo returns a lastupdate per database in one free call — and reports that 20.6% of the record count has not moved in a year. Article-level freshness is the reverse: no lastmod anywhere in the PMC sitemap, so the S3 version suffix is the change signal. On rights, "US Government site" is the expensive mistake: NLM-authored content is public domain, the literature is not. Reuse exists only inside the OA Subset and is stated per article in license_code.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, keyed PMCID + version where full text exists and PMID where only a citation does, with the passage as sub-unit. Attach MeSH terms, license_code and is_retracted to every unit.
  • Primary keys & joins: PMID ↔ PMCID ↔ DOI resolved from PMC-ids.csv.gz rather than scraped; MeSH descriptor IDs as the entity layer; NCT numbers to reach ClinicalTrials.gov; SetID for DailyMed.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) PubMed FTP baseline + daily updates — 51.77 GB, no auth, the only complete path to 41.18M citations. 2) S3 pmc-oa-opendata — 8.24M articles as XML, text, PDF and figures, driven by PMCIDs from path 1 since the bucket has no manifest. 3) PMC sitemap crawl at the declared 1s delay for the 4.39M non-OA articles, metadata and display only. 4) CTG API v2 and DailyMed v2 — small enough to take whole. 5) E-utilities with a free key for deltas and counts, never enumeration. 6) PubMed HTML — skip; it does not render.
  • Recrawl cadence: poll einfo daily for all 38 lastupdate values and let that drive everything downstream — the cheapest freshness oracle here. Apply PubMed update files daily, re-list changed S3 prefixes weekly against stored versions, refresh the join table daily, re-pull CTG on its dataTimestamp, and leave the seven stale databases alone until their stamps move.
  • Derivatives: passage embeddings over the .txt renderings; figure-caption pairs from JATS XML joined to the packaged JPEGs — cross-modal training data with per-article licences already attached; a MeSH-to-passage entity index; a retraction overlay propagating is_retracted to every passage at query time.
  • Demo to build: a licence-aware corpus slicer — walk PMC-ids.csv.gz, fetch only the S3 .json sidecars, and report how many articles are redistributable, under which license_code, and how many are retracted or OCR-derived. Kilobytes per article instead of megabytes, answering the question that decides whether the other 8.24M downloads are legal.

Risks to the Index

  • The bulk path just moved, the old one was deleted, and the manifests went with it. NCBI announced on 12 Feb 2026 that in August 2026 the PMC datasets would leave FTP, the OA Web Service API would retire, and bulk packages would end in favour of individual files. All three are confirmed dead in the Probe Log, so any pipeline written before 2026 is broken. Worse, the new bucket has zero top-level objects: 8.24M articles cannot be discovered from inside it, leaving PMC-ids.csv.gz as the only index. A single 254.7 MB file on the service NLM is retiring is now the entry point to the service replacing it.
  • Open-access licences have been revoked before. The PMC readme documents COVID-19 Collection articles whose reuse terms expired with the public health emergency — some pulled from the OA Subset, some removed from the archive entirely, with downstream users told to update their datasets. Re-check license_code; never cache it once.
  • The 203 gate can spread. It already covers PubMed record pages under every UA tried, and NCBI's own API documentation. Nothing in robots.txt announced it and Googlebot does not bypass it, so treat any currently-open HTML surface here — PMC articles included — as revocable without notice.
  • Appropriation risk is infrastructure risk. This corpus has no revenue defending it and its cloud egress is donated. Budget pressure surfaces as stale databases before outages — proteinclusters has not updated since 2017 while still serving 1.14M records as though current.
  • inferred The AI-crawler silence is unconsidered, not permissive. Basis: the 390-line NCBI robots.txt is built from 11 blocks for mostly defunct crawlers (htdig/3.1.6, Teleport Pro, Exabot, yacybot) with no entry for any post-2022 agent, while a separate NLM team wrote a 9-line file naming GPTBot and anthropic-ai for the digitised collections. One file was maintained; the other was not. Do not read the omission as a grant.

Inferences

  • The AI-crawler omission on the literature hosts is neglect rather than policy. Basis: 11 user-agent blocks across 390 lines, all for pre-2022 or defunct agents, versus a separate 9-line file on collections.nlm.nih.gov naming GPTBot and anthropic-ai explicitly.
  • The 203 gate is a bot-management tier in front of PubMed, not part of the application. Basis: the challenge responds server: Microsoft-IIS/10.0 where the same host serves its homepage from nginx, and varies on X-NCBI-CAPTCHA and X-GCP-Client-IP — headers injected upstream of the app.
  • The version suffix in S3 keys is the article-level change signal the sitemap omits. Basis: every bucket prefix is PMC{id}.{version}/ with a matching version field in the sidecar, while no PMC sitemap child sampled contained a <lastmod>.

Open Questions

  • What actually triggers the PubMed 203 — closeable by requesting record pages from a residential IP, a cloud IP and a verified Googlebot IP with a solved-CAPTCHA cookie, and comparing outcomes.
  • Whether the 3/s E-utilities limit is enforced at all, given 12 rapid unauthenticated calls all returned 200 — closeable by ramping from one IP until a 429 appears, keyed and unkeyed.
  • The exact license_code distribution across the 8.24M OA Subset — closeable by fetching only the .json sidecars for every PMCID in PMC-ids.csv.gz, which is the demo proposed in the Indexing Plan.
  • How many PMC articles carry more than one S3 version, and how often versions are superseded — closeable by counting distinct suffixes per PMCID across two snapshots.
  • Whether the seven stale Entrez databases are deprecated or merely slow, since neither einfo nor the site says — closeable by checking each one's NCBI announcement history against its lastupdate.
