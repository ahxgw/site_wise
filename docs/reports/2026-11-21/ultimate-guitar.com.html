Context

Ultimate Guitar (UG) is the largest guitar-tab catalogue: user-submitted chords and tablature for guitar, bass, ukulele and drums, plus Guitar Pro files, licensed "Official" tabs sold through the UG Pro subscription, news, gear and album reviews and a forum. Tabs were the archetypal copyright fight of the 2000s; UG answered with publisher deals from 2005 and a Harry Fox Agency licence in 2010, so the free catalogue is licensed display, not tolerated piracy. It is the flagship of Muse Group, which also owns MuseScore, Audacity and Hal Leonard. For an index builder it is unusually generous: robots.txt names no AI crawler, there is no bot wall on tab pages, and every tab page ships its full text, chord fingerings, view counts and revision IDs in one server-rendered JSON blob. The catch is that the music itself is symbolic notation in a private markup, and the highest-value versions (Official and Guitar Pro) are paywalled or binary.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

The JSON is the page

Default assumption: rich data hides behind JS or an API. Here the server ships the full object graph in one HTML attribute, so a plain GET plus html.unescape and json.loads yields text, structured music facts and engagement counts. No rendering is needed. The visible HTML is a thin React shell; parsing the DOM instead of the blob loses chord fingerings, strumming and stats.

Song is the entity, tab is the version

A song has many tabs: Wonderwall chords alone has 12 versions, plus tabs, bass, Official and video siblings. song_id (on every sampled tab) groups them; artist_id and album_id join up. Rank versions inside a song by votes×rating and views, and collapse them in results. The ID in the URL is the tab ID; slug words are validated (wrong artist 404s), so dedup on the tab ID, not on the path.

Notation needs its own parser

[ch]Em7[/ch] marks chords positioned over lyrics; [tab]…[/tab] marks fixed-width blocks whose column alignment carries the meaning. Tokenizing it as prose destroys both. Split into lyrics text (searchable, but licensed and sensitive), a chord sequence (key, progression, capo-adjusted), and section labels ([Verse], [Chorus]). That enables queries like "songs with Em–G–D–A in G with capo 2" that no general engine answers.

Explore is a free facet engine

/explore is open to every crawler but Bing's, and its facet counts reconcile with the sitemap to 0.02%, giving an exact census by type, key, tuning, difficulty, genre and decade without walking 2M pages. Each query returns at most 5K rows, so it is a counter and a freshness feed (order=date_desc), not a crawl path.

The hreflang half is a mirror

All 2.19M tab URLs list an es. alternate with identical notation and a translated interface. Do not double-index: map es. to the tabs. URL and keep the language only as an interface attribute.

The best versions are dark

Official tabs (licensed, transcribed in-house) and Guitar Pro files (full multi-instrument scores) are the highest-quality notation. Official URLs redirect to a paywall, and GP files sit behind a robots-disallowed download path with dead preview SVGs. A free-tier index sees titles and metadata only for 15% of the catalogue (Official + Guitar Pro + Power + MuseScore, calculated by Site Wise from the type facets).

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the tab version, keyed by tab ID, grouped under a song entity (song_id) with artist, album, type, tuning, key, capo, difficulty, rating, views and revision ID. Text tabs carry three sub-documents: lyric lines, chord sequence and tab blocks.
  • Primary keys & joins: tab ID (sequential, ~3K a day issued, 32% public); song_id, artist_id, album_id; user_id for contributors; YouTube video ID for Video tabs; MusicBrainz/ISRC by artist+title match (no external ID observed on the page).
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Sitemap census (83 files, 86MB) then a paced GET of each tab for the js-store blob; at 1 req/1.5s, 2.2M tabs take ~38 days from one IP (calculated by Site Wise), so prioritise by explore popularity. 2) sitemap_last.xml + /explore?order=date_desc daily for new tabs. 3) Articles and reviews via their sitemaps. Forum: skip until a browser-grade path is justified.
  • Recrawl cadence: daily for sitemap_last and the newest 1K; monthly for the top 50K by views (comment, rating and revision drift); yearly for the long tail, whose lastmod rarely moves.
  • Derivatives: normalised chord progressions (Roman numerals relative to key), transposition-invariant fingerprints for "same song, different key" matching, difficulty from chord vocabulary, and lyric-line embeddings kept as snippets only.
  • Demo to build: a progression search: type "vi–IV–I–V in any key, capo allowed" and get ranked songs with the matching section, built from [ch] sequences and applicature.

Risks to the Index

  • Lyrics are the licence trap. UG's Harry Fox licence covers lyrics display on UG (Verified Metrics). An index that shows whole lyric lines from 1.8M tabs republishes licensed text. Store lyrics for matching, show chords and short snippets, link out.
  • Publishers can pull tabs at any time. The help centre says UG may be "in negotiation with publishers" and blocks songs meanwhile; 1 of 37 sampled tabs carried blocked: true. Treat blocked as a tombstone signal and re-check it on recrawl.
  • inferred The open blob could close. Basis: the forum already sits behind a Cloudflare managed challenge on the same zone (Probe Log), so the switch exists. Muse Group's 2026 refinancing with J.P. Morgan credit (ownership release) raises the pressure to monetise, and full tab text in one JSON attribute is the easiest thing to copy.
  • inferred Most issued IDs are not public. Basis: about 3K IDs a day against ~320 approved tabs, and 27 of 40 random IDs 404. Pending, rejected, private and deleted tabs share the sequence, so an ID walk is wasteful; use the sitemap.
