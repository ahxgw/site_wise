Context

Mayo Clinic is a nonprofit academic health system. Its website does two jobs: it is a consumer medical reference (Diseases & Conditions, Symptoms, Tests & Procedures, Drugs & Supplements, Healthy Lifestyle) and it sells Mayo care (appointments, doctor bios, departments). In January 2014 Mayo merged mayoclinic.com, mayo.edu and mayoclinic.org onto one Sitecore platform, about 60K pages at the time (Mayo News Network, 2014-01-24). The site makes money indirectly: through referrals to clinical care, display ads, and licensing the same content by API through Mayo Clinic Global Business Solutions (GBS). For an index builder, three things stand out. The reference corpus is small, editor-written and fully translated, with stable IDs. The site is moving from Sitecore to a new stack one section at a time. And there are two walls: Akamai bot management at the edge, and terms that ban all automated access.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where mayoclinic.org differs from the default platform assumptions.

One ID, every language

Each content object has a type prefix and an 8-digit ID (syc-20371444). The ID is identical across en, es, ar and zh-hans, and it reappears as pocId in page state and as Kaltura's referenceId. Deduplicate on prefix plus ID, not on the slug: the drug sitemap lists the same ID up to three times, and slugs differ by language. That makes it a parallel corpus of about 9K aligned pages per language.

The concept is the unit, tabs are facets

A condition is split across tabs that have different IDs but the same slug: syc (symptoms and causes), drc (diagnosis and treatment), ddc (doctors and departments) and mac (care at Mayo). Only the first two are reference text. ddc and mac are patient-acquisition pages, so index them as entity links to departments rather than as answers. Join tabs on the shared slug under /diseases-conditions/{slug}/.

The sitemap is not the inventory

The sitemaps leave out every in-depth article (art), the slideshow images, and the English ddc pages, which appear only as hreflang targets. Wayback also shows more syc IDs than the sitemap lists. To find the full set, harvest the hreflang alternates, crawl the hlv- topic landing pages, and fill gaps from CDX.

Two schemas mid-migration

The same URL family can come from Sitecore (IIS, about 600KB) or from AEM on Next.js (GCP, about 1.3MB, full content in __NEXT_DATA__). The parsers differ: Sitecore misspells signORsymptom, the GCP stack leaves datePublished empty on articles, and only the GCP stack exposes workId, sseSubjectId and related-subject IDs. Both stacks keep the URLs, so key on the URL and record which stack served each page.

Video text lives at the video host

Video pages ship no transcript and no VideoObject. But Kaltura's widget API returns duration, views and human-quality captions (3Play-processed, VTT and SRT), keyed back to the page by referenceId. That gives the full-modal join (page, video entry, caption cues, thumbnail) with no ASR needed.

Authority field

Unlike crowd-sourced health sites, every reference page is staff-written and cites its sources, with "Accessed" dates. Use the newest reference-access date as the evidence-freshness signal. lastmod and datePublished are unreliable: they are empty on the GCP stack and bulk-stamped in the drug sitemap.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the condition concept, built from its syc and drc sections (Symptoms, Causes, Risk factors, Diagnosis, Treatment) and carrying the four language versions. The other units are the article or expert answer, the video (Kaltura entry plus caption cues), the recipe, and the physician entity. Drug monographs get title-and-link stubs only.
  • Primary keys & joins: type prefix plus 8-digit ID (shared across languages and equal to pocId and Kaltura's referenceId); the condition slug, which joins the four tabs; MedicalWebPage.about names, for linking to external vocabularies (SNOMED or MeSH by string match); sseSubjectId for topic clusters (GCP stack only); and the department slug, which joins ddc pages to physician bios.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A GBS content licence. It is the only path the terms allow, and it covers text and translations. 2) The Kaltura widget API for video metadata and captions, one referenceIdEqual call for each of the 1.1K vid IDs (about 2.2K calls in total). 3) Sitemaps plus hreflang harvest for URL discovery and lastmod (the files are small, about 25MB uncompressed in all). 4) Wayback or Common Crawl captures for research-only gap analysis. Never bulk-fetch the live site against the terms and the bot manager.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemaps weekly, diffing lastmod; condition pages on a lastmod change; the video catalogue quarterly; bios monthly.
  • Derivatives to generate: section-level chunks (Symptoms, Causes and so on) with language-aligned embeddings; caption-cue windows that link back to the page; and a symptom-to-condition graph from signOrSymptom plus the sym pages, which list possible causes.
  • Demo to build: a cross-lingual symptom lookup. A query such as "dolor de rodilla e hinchazón" retrieves the English syc section through the shared ID and shows the matching Kaltura caption cue. It would run locally on the sample only.

Risks to the Index

  • The terms ban crawling and even deep-linking. Since the 2024-04-12 revision, any automated access and deep-linking are prohibited (Terms of use). robots.txt allows everything, so the permissive signal and the contractual one disagree; the stricter one governs. Mayo's paid GBS syndication is the obvious alternative.
  • Edge blocking is IP-based and silent. Every UA got the same 403, including on robots.txt, so a crawler cannot even read the crawl policy from a blocked egress. An index fed from datacenter IPs would see a dead site rather than an error page with content.
  • The migration will reshape the HTML. As more sections move from Sitecore to AEM/Next.js, extractors tuned to the Sitecore DOM will break page by page. Watch the share of mayo-origin-source values in the headers.
  • Third-party licensed content sits inside first-party URLs. The Merative drug monographs (2.6K pages) belong to another rights holder and need their own exclusion rule.

Inferences

  • The Sitecore-to-AEM migration is incremental and per page, not per section. Basis: in captures from 2026-10-01/02, syc-20353004 came from gcp while syc-20369950, -20374343 and -20361694 came from sitecore. Page state also shows an AEM spapage component, and robots.txt disallows *.model.json.
  • The 403 is an IP-reputation block, not a UA rule. Basis: five different UAs, including an empty one, got byte-identical 386-byte 403s on robots.txt, while Wayback captures from other egresses got 200 on the same paths in the same week.
  • Video captions are produced by 3Play Media, a human-verified captioning vendor. Basis: the Kaltura tag 3play_processed on 3 of 3 entries, and SRT assets carrying an accuracy field.
