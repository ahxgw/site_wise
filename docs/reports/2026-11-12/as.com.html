Context

AS is a Madrid sports daily founded in 1967 and owned by PRISA, which also owns El País and Cadena SER. as.com carries the Spanish site plus country editions for Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina, Peru and Spanish-language US (as.com/us/). en.as.com ("AS USA") is an English edition that covers football and US sports, Meristation gaming and a large block of US consumer news. The site makes its money from advertising, with betting and commerce side sites; all 20 sampled articles were marked signwallType: "free". For an index builder, the publisher is split in two. Spanish HTML sits behind DataDome and has no public sitemap, so it is closed to a non-browser client. The English edition runs on the same Arc XP stack with no challenge and publishes monthly sitemaps back to 2016. In both cases the legal notice reserves the content against text and data mining (TDM) and any AI use, so that notice, not access, is the binding constraint.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where as.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

The open edition is not the main product

The English host is fully crawlable, but the Spanish site is AS's core. The English corpus over-weights US SEO content: latest_news (20.6K) plus entertainment roughly equal soccer (29.8K). Its translations cover only part of Spanish output (6 of 20 sampled). An index built from en.as.com alone would misrepresent AS. Treat it as its own publication and flag the translated items.

Identifiers & dedup

Key on the 26-character Arc articleID from the data layer. URLs changed scheme twice: legacy {epoch}_{id}.html (to 2022), then slug-{n|v|d|r}/, then slug-fYYYYMM-n/ (from Oct 2025). A -n-2/ suffix marks a slug collision, not a duplicate. Use the es-us hreflang to join a translation to its Spanish original without text matching.

Video pages lie about their content

-v/ pages carry NewsArticle markup with a boilerplate app-promo articleBody. Indexing that field would attach the same paragraph to every video. Take title and description from the page head, and duration and MP4 from MRSS. ASR is the only real text source, and running it needs a licence.

Two feed generations

The as.com/rss/*.xml feeds still return 200 but stopped updating in 2022–2024. The en.as.com/rss/ page still lists them, so a feed-based crawler would silently ingest nothing new. The live feeds are feeds.as.com/mrss-s/pages/as/site/{host}/…, and they carry summaries, not full text.

Freshness & recrawl

Monthly files bucket URLs by publish month, and lastmod moves on edit (264 of 24.9K 2024 URLs carry later dates). Re-fetch only the current and previous month's files daily, plus news.xml every 15 minutes. Older months need a lastmod diff about once a month.

robots.txt is a moving blocklist

The named-bot block went live on 2026-05-05 (per its own comment), and it allows OpenAI's crawlers by omission. The rules differ by crawler, so an indexer's access depends on its UA identity, not on a site-wide policy. Re-read robots.txt before every crawl cycle.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article, with headline, body, author, tags, lead image and a translator flag. A video is a separate unit (title, description, duration, watch URL), and a live blog is one unit updated in place.
  • Primary keys & joins: Arc articleID; canonical URL; the es-us hreflang for English↔Spanish pairs; tag {slug}-a and author IDs; numeric PRISA media ID for video.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) A content licence from PRISA. This is required for any TDM or AI use and is the only route to the Spanish corpus. 2) English: walk the monthly sitemaps once (132 files, about 5 minutes), then fetch pages at ≤1 req/s (~2 days for 175K) for metadata and snippets only. 3) Spanish: MRSS feeds for headlines, summaries and media links (a recent window, no backfill). 4) Do not bypass DataDome.
  • Recrawl cadence: news.xml and the front-page MRSS every 15 minutes; current-month sitemaps daily; live blogs every 5 minutes while flagged live; the archive monthly by lastmod.
  • Derivatives to generate: a translation-pair table linking English items to their Spanish originals; per-tag timelines; a corrected video text field (title plus description, or ASR under licence).
  • Demo to build: a corpus-shape dashboard for en.as.com from sitemaps alone. It would chart monthly volume by section from 2016 to 2026, showing the 2023 peak and the growth of latest_news, with no page fetches.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The English opening could close without notice. Basis: en.as.com shares the Arc edge (x-arc-edgecontroller 1.8.5) and the same 4,616-byte robots.txt as as.com, where DataDome is already active. Only the DataDome header is missing on en.. Snapshot the sitemap census now, and expect the en.as.com sitemaps to be the next thing walled.
  • inferred Archive gaps cannot be filled from archives. Basis: since 2026-05-05 robots.txt blocks archive.org_bot, ia_archiver and CCBot. Wayback holds no as.com/sitemaps/ capture after 2016, and its recent /aviso-legal/ captures are 403. Spanish-side history depends on PRISA.
  • Legal exposure is explicit. The art. 67.3 reservation and the AI clause (Verified Metrics) remove the EU TDM exception for commercial miners. Using the open English pages for model training or embeddings needs a licence.
