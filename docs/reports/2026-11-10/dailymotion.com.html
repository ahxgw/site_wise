Context

dailymotion.com is a Paris-based video platform founded in 2005. Vivendi bought it in 2015, and in 2024 it moved under Canal+ ahead of Vivendi's split. It now sits in Canal+'s "Content production, distribution and other" segment alongside STUDIOCANAL. Revenue comes from advertising: the consumer site, the embeddable player sold to publishers (Dailymotion Pro) and an ad stack (Dailymotion Advertising). Canal+ said ad weakness hurt the platform in Q3 2026. For an index builder, Dailymotion is the most open large video platform seen in this series. Its sitemaps carry full Google video extensions (title, description, duration, views, tags, geo restriction and uploader) for about 55M videos. Its legacy REST API answers anonymously with more than 100 fields per video. robots.txt names no AI crawler. The constraint is contractual: the July 2026 terms prohibit automated access without written approval and ban using platform content to train AI.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where Dailymotion departs from the default platform assumptions.

The sitemap is the catalog

Most large platforms publish a curated SEO sliver; Dailymotion publishes nearly everything public. 17 of 20 public recent videos outside latest were in a default family, and no private video was listed (Probe Log). A full run is 3,700 gzip files, roughly 9GB compressed, calculated by Site Wise from 2.5MB average parts. It replaces 55M page fetches.

Identifier & dedup

The video key is x + base-36 ID. Old IDs use every residue mod 4; since about 2023 new IDs use residues 0 and 2, and since mid-2026 only 2 (sitemap census). Use the API checksum (SHA-1 of the source file) to catch re-uploads of the same file across channels. This matters here because the latest set is dominated by "Full Movie" long-form uploads.

Timestamp trap

Sitemap publication_date is Paris local time with a Z suffix, so it runs 1–2h ahead of true UTC. Take created_time from the API, or subtract the CET/CEST offset, before joining with other sources.

HTML is the weakest surface

Video pages give a non-search UA a generic <title> with no OG or JSON-LD. Sitemap XML and the API carry the structured fields, so parse those and never scrape HTML. Whether verified search crawlers get richer markup is left untested (see Inferences).

Open to AI crawlers in robots, closed in contract

From 2024 to 2026 robots.txt never named an AI crawler, and llms.txt points LLMs to the sitemaps. Yet the July 2026 terms forbid scraping and AI training without written authorization (Verified Metrics). This is the sharpest robots-versus-contract split in the series so far.

Quality & spam

In the 48h latest set, 64% of videos had zero views and the median ran 57 minutes. Titles like "…Full Movie English" with long hashtag tails (the xbusbv2 sample) point to bulk long-form uploads. Weight partner/verified, owner.videos_total and view velocity before admitting a video to a ranked index.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the video (x-ID) with title, description, tags/hashtags, duration, channel category, language, owner, view count, geo policy, thumbnail and embed URL. The channel (/user/{name}) is the parent entity.
  • Primary keys & joins: the x-ID internally. checksum for duplicate files, soundtrack_isrc to join with music catalogs, and owner username to join news-publisher channels (AFP, France24, euronews) with their own sites.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Written approval or a data agreement with Dailymotion; ToS §2.6 requires it for any automated ingestion, and AI use needs a separate grant. 2) A full pass over the 3,700 sitemap files, then the latest and latest-heuristic families daily. 3) API enrichment, only for fields absent from sitemaps (checksum, language, partner, hashtags). 4) oEmbed or the embed player for display.
  • Recrawl cadence: latest every 24h; it holds 48h, so one missed day loses nothing. Re-pull the tail parts of the default families weekly for view counts. Run a full default-family diff monthly to find deletions, which simply drop out of the sitemap.
  • Derivatives to generate: a per-channel quality score from views per upload, a re-upload cluster map keyed by checksum, and per-country availability vectors from the restriction lists.
  • Demo to build: a "where can I watch this" geo-availability map. Parse one day of latest and plot the restriction country sets by channel. This works from sitemap XML alone, without touching video pages.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The open sitemap may not survive cost pressure. Basis: Canal+ singled out Dailymotion's Q3 advertising decline (Verified Metrics), and the sitemap feeds every crawler with titles, durations and view counts at no charge. Losing it would turn a ~9GB pass into 55M API or page fetches. Diff the 9 Sitemap: lines monthly.
  • inferred Index pollution from bulk long-form uploads. Basis: 46% of new listed videos run ≥1h and 64% have zero views, and 47% carry the same single-country (VN) denial (Verified Metrics). That uniform pattern points to a few high-volume uploaders, not organic creators.

Inferences

  • Sitemap stamps are Paris local time mislabeled as UTC. Basis: a +2h offset on an April 2006 video and +1h on two November 2026 videos, compared with API created_time epochs (Probe Log).
  • The sitemap generator leaves private and unpublished videos out but includes nearly all public ones. Basis: in the 40-slot density sample, 0 of 18 403s and 17 of 20 public videos were listed. Expect about ±20% sampling error at n=20.

Open Questions

  • Total public videos, as opposed to sitemap-listed ones. Closeable with a ~400-slot random API sample stratified by ID era (all residues before 2023; residues 0/2; residue 2 only), weighted by slot counts.
  • Caption coverage. Closeable by calling /video/{id}/subtitles on ~300 random sitemap videos, split between partner and user families.
  • Whether verified Googlebot gets OG and JSON-LD on video pages. Closeable from Google's Rich Results Test on a video URL, or from a Common Crawl capture of /video/*, compared with the markup served to the research UA.
  • The anonymous API request quota and API terms. Closeable by reading the developer terms from a non-blocked network (legal.dailymotion.com/en/api-terms-of-use/ returned 403) and by a paced ramp test that watches for 429s.
  • Dailymotion's standalone revenue and audience. Closeable from the Canal+ 2026 universal registration document, if it breaks out Dailymotion, or from an audited panel (Médiamétrie, Comscore) for the 400M MAU claim.
