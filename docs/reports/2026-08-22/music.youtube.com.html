Context

YouTube Music is Google's streaming service built on top of YouTube's video corpus rather than beside it. It sells the same subscription as YouTube Premium and rides the same ad inventory — YouTube's "twin engine" — so Alphabet never discloses a Music revenue or subscriber line of its own. For an index builder the useful object is the recording, and the awkward fact is that one recording usually maps to two YouTube video IDs: a label-delivered "Art Track" (audio plus static art on an auto-generated … - Topic channel) and the official music video. The music layer adds identifiers on top — MPRE… releases, UC… artists — and those are what make the catalog navigable as music rather than as video. Access posture inverts the usual pattern for a platform this size: robots.txt is eleven lines and forbids nothing but the internal API, there is no sitemap and no official Music API, yet the server hands normalized per-entity metadata to any unauthenticated curl. inferred Google treats YouTube Music as a client of the video graph, not an independent corpus. Basis: no sitemap of its own and absent from all 23 entries of the youtube.com sitemap index (Probe Log), while oEmbed resolves music.youtube.com URLs to youtube.com/embed video payloads.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where YouTube Music differs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

The central problem: one recording commonly has two videoIds — an Art Track and an official music video (Probe Log). Neither is canonical; both are legitimate. The reliable discriminator is oEmbed's author_name, where a … - Topic suffix marks the Art Track channel. Dedup on (normalized title, artist, duration) and keep both IDs as aliases rather than picking a winner.

Retrieval unit

The recording, not the page and not the video: a recording carries 1–n video IDs, belongs to 0–n releases, and is the object users actually name. Release (MPRE…) and artist (UC…) are separate units worth their own documents; the app shell is not a document at all.

Existence detection

HTTP status is useless here: the catch-all SPA answers 200 for everything. Validity must come from the body instead — a specific og:type, plus size (real entity ≈ 92–95KB, shell ≈ 384KB). A crawler that trusts status codes ingests an unbounded pile of identical shells.

Authoritative content field

Inverted from the usual case: the site's own og:title is cleaner than the official oEmbed API. og gives "Never Gonna Give You Up" / "Rick Astley"; oEmbed gives the uploader-authored "Rick Astley - Never Gonna Give You Up (Official Video) (4K Remaster)". For music metadata, prefer scraped og over the sanctioned endpoint — then use oEmbed only for the Art Track discriminator.

Cross-modal joins

videoId is the stable join key to everything on youtube.com: thumbnails (i.ytimg.com/vi/{id}/), comments, captions, the embed player. Music-layer IDs (MPRE…, the OLAK5uy_… album-playlist alias) join only inside YouTube Music with no documented mapping outward — and the playlist alias is unrendered, so it cannot be resolved by fetching it.

Crawl policy

The opposite of the default assumption. Rather than banning AI crawlers, both robots.txt files never mention them, leaving GPTBot and peers formally unrestricted. The single meaningful prohibition is /youtubei/ — exactly the endpoint that would make the catalog machine-readable. Permission is broad; usefulness is withheld.

Internal vs external search

Nothing on this host is search-engine-visible by design: no sitemap, no JSON-LD, no-store on the shell. External music queries land on youtube.com video pages instead, so the web index knows the videos and not the music entities. On-site search is strong but HTML-only — OpenSearch offers no JSON result type.

User query profile

Short entity lookups — artist, song, album, "artist + song" — plus mood/activity phrases ("focus", "workout") that map to editorial playlists rather than recordings. Lyric fragments are a first-class query type here, and nothing in the accessible surface exposes lyrics, so that intent cannot be served from this corpus.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: recording, with alias list of video IDs; release and artist as sibling document types.
  • Primary keys & joins: recording = normalized (title, artist, duration) with videoId[] aliases; release = MPRE…; artist = UC…; videoId joins outward to the youtube.com video graph.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) /search?q= inline JSON — robots-permitted, unauthenticated, ~30 typed rows per GET; the only path yielding relationships in bulk. 2) og layer per entity URL — typed confirmation plus artist monthly audience, one entity per request. 3) YouTube Data API v3 — authoritative video metadata and comments, 10,000 units/day, no music entities. 4) oEmbed — Art Track discrimination and embed HTML. 5) /youtubei/ InnerTube — complete but robots-disallowed; excluded.
  • Seeding strategy: discovery is the binding constraint, so bootstrap from an external music authority (e.g. MusicBrainz artist/release names) and resolve each through /search?q= to acquire YouTube-side IDs. The index is only as complete as its seed list.
  • Recrawl cadence: recording and release metadata quarterly; artist monthly-audience monthly; editorial playlists weekly; never recrawl the shell.
  • Derivatives to generate: title normalization to strip "(Official Video)", "(Remaster)", "(feat. …)" noise; an Art-Track ↔ music-video alias table; text embeddings over (title, artist, album) for fuzzy lookup. No ASR — audio is unobtainable, and captions belong to the video-side pipeline.
  • Demo to build: a music.youtube.com entity resolver — paste artist/song names, hit /search?q=, decode the hex-escaped initialData blob, emit JSONL recording records with both video IDs, and flag which rows would have been silently accepted as valid by a status-code-trusting crawler.

Risks to the Index

  • The primary path is undocumented, not promised. The inline initialData blob is an internal rendering detail, and its variable name already differs from youtube.com's ytInitialData (Probe Log) — the shape is host-specific and can change with no notice or deprecation window. Parse defensively and alert on schema drift.
  • Permissive robots.txt is a lagging indicator. Eleven lines with no AI-crawler rules reads as a page nobody has had reason to maintain, and it is the cheapest thing on the site to change. Do not treat today's open access as durable.
  • inferred Rendering the catalog server-side is not a goal, so the og layer may thin rather than thicken. Basis: the album-playlist alias /playlist?list=OLAK5uy_… already returns no og: tags at all while /browse/MPRE… does (Probe Log) — per-entity metadata is inconsistent today, which is what a surface nobody optimizes for looks like.
  • No licence covers the useful path. Because no official Music API exists, there is no terms document that contemplates bulk music-entity extraction — so there is nothing to comply with and nothing to rely on. Legal posture rests on ToS interpretation, and the rights holders are labels, not Google.
  • Metric provenance is unstable. The 125M subscriber and $8B payout figures are advocacy disclosures published when favourable, not periodic reporting; Alphabet's filings never decompose them (Verified Metrics). Expect gaps, not a series.

Inferences

  • Google treats YouTube Music as a client of the video graph rather than an independent corpus. Basis: no sitemap of its own and zero presence across all 23 youtube.com sitemap-index entries, while oEmbed resolves music.youtube.com URLs to youtube.com/embed video payloads.
  • The og layer may thin rather than thicken over time. Basis: /playlist?list=OLAK5uy_… serves no og: tags while /browse/MPRE… serves og:type=music.album — coverage is already inconsistent per entity type.
  • Broad crawl permission coexists with withheld usefulness by design rather than oversight. Basis: the only Disallow rules that bind * are /youtubei/ and /api/ — precisely the machine-readable paths — while every human-facing entity path is permitted.

Open Questions

  • Standalone YouTube Music subscribers and revenue — only the combined Music + Premium figure is ever published; closeable only by an Alphabet disclosure that decomposes the subscriptions line.
  • True catalog composition behind "100M+ songs" — how many are Art Tracks versus user uploads, covers and remixes. Closeable by sampling /search?q= rows at scale and classifying by … - Topic author, then extrapolating.
  • What share of recordings carry two or more video IDs — measurable by resolving a MusicBrainz-seeded sample through search and counting distinct IDs per (title, artist, duration).
  • Whether the OLAK5uy_… album-playlist alias maps deterministically to an MPRE… release ID — needs a paired sample of both identifiers for the same album.
  • The undeclared request-rate ceiling on the og and search paths — measurable only by a deliberate ramp, which was out of scope for this report.
  • Hosting and CDN posture for this host — not measurable from this vantage point (Probe Log); needs a run from an unproxied network.
