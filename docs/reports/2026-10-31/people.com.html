Context

people.com is the web edition of PEOPLE, the US celebrity and human-interest weekly that Time Inc. launched in 1974. Meredith bought Time Inc. in Jan 2018. IAC's Dotdash bought Meredith in Dec 2021, and the publisher later took the magazine's name: People Inc. On Jun 4 2026 the parent itself, formerly IAC, became People Incorporated (NASDAQ: PPLI). Revenue comes from advertising, affiliate commerce and, increasingly, licensing. The publisher has AI content deals with OpenAI (May 2024), Microsoft's pay-per-use marketplace (Nov 2025) and Meta (Dec 2025). Its robots.txt carries those deals: licensees get through and nearly every other AI agent is blocked. For an index builder the delta is that the policy is enforced at the edge. Cloudflare challenges every HTML page and every child sitemap for a self-identified research UA. The same corpus still leaves through side doors on other hosts: a paginated feed API and JW Player's public delivery API.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where people.com departs from the default platform assumptions.

Identifier & dedup

Key on the Mantle docId, not the URL. New URLs end in it (/{slug}-{docId}). Legacy /{section}/{slug}/ URLs do not show it in the URL, but they still have one: the JW record for /tv/derek-hough-hayley-erbert-relationship-timeline/ carries docid 7853408. The ID sequence is shared with Allrecipes, Investopedia and the other brands, so a portfolio-wide index needs (brand, docId) only for display; the ID alone is unique.

Edge policy narrower than robots.txt

robots.txt allows everything under *, yet Cloudflare challenges all HTML and child sitemaps for an honest research UA. Only robots.txt, the sitemap index and the News sitemap pass. An index that relies on robots.txt permissions will discover URLs it cannot fetch. Treat the challenged set as licence-only, not as crawlable.

robots.txt as a licence ledger

Group membership tracks commercial deals. OpenAI (licensed 2024) is allowed everything except images. Meta's agents remain in the blocked group despite the Dec 2025 deal, but with */presented/ and */integrated/ carve-outs. Diff this file over time: a group move means a deal was signed or ended.

Authoritative content field

JSON-LD gives headline, dates, author and image, but the entity layer is in the Parse.ly tags and feed categories (named people), not in schema.org about. datePublished and the sitemap lastmod drift on evergreen pages: the News sitemap carried a 7.5M-ID piece dated this week. Use the docId range for true vintage.

Two URL generations

Dotdash's mid-2023 replatforming split the corpus. About 62% of recovered sitemap URLs are legacy paths with no ID, and their lastmod values look like regeneration stamps rather than publish dates (Inferences). Take vintage from the feed pubDate, which goes back to 1997. Expect legacy → new redirects when old pieces are refreshed.

Commerce content

A visible share of daily output is affiliate shopping posts (e.g. "…Start at $30 on Amazon"; "amazon" in 21 of 429 news slugs). Classify by slug and section, and down-rank them for news intents.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the article (docId), with its lead image, attached video clip and named-entity tags; celebrity entities as hub objects.
  • Primary keys & joins: docId (URL suffix, JW docid); JW mediaid → captions and audio; 32-hex image ID; author URL; entity names from feed categories.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) Feed API pages 1–N daily (only the first few pages change), plus one paced backfill of 7.6K pages for the 381K-item metadata archive: no auth, no body text. 2) News sitemap every few hours for docId and publish time. 3) JW playlists for video, audio and captions, embedding the player for display. 4) Sitemap index to watch part counts; child parts only via Wayback. 5) Body text: People Inc. licence or Microsoft's marketplace; Wayback id_ for research samples only.
  • Recrawl cadence: feed head and News sitemap hourly; JW trending daily; full feed walk monthly to catch re-dated evergreens; robots.txt weekly (deal changes).
  • Derivatives to generate: caption-text embeddings for clips; image embeddings from /thmb/ at small sizes; an entity co-occurrence graph from feed categories.
  • Demo to build: a "celebrity timeline" index: walk the feed archive (1997–2026) by entity category and return dated headlines plus JW clips for any named person, with no article fetch at all.

Risks to the Index

  • inferred The feed side door may close. Basis: the feed path is /v1/rss/google/{uuid}, it accepts an all-zero UUID, and its host has no robots.txt (Probe Log). That looks like a partner feed built for Google rather than a supported public API, so expect it to be keyed or challenged eventually. Store the metadata on first walk.
  • inferred The publisher monetises access, not traffic. Basis: core sessions −22% while licensing grew +23% (Verified Metrics), and the robots.txt groups mirror the deals. Bulk text access will be priced, not opened.
