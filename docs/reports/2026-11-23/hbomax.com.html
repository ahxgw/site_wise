Context

HBO Max is the subscription streaming service built on HBO, Warner Bros. film and TV, DC, Discovery's unscripted networks, CNN, TCM and live sports from TNT. It launched in the US on 2020-05-27, became "Max" on 2023-05-23 and took the HBO Max name back on 2025-07-09. Paramount Skydance closed its purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery on 2026-10-06, so the service now belongs to Skydance. Revenue comes from ad-supported and ad-free subscriptions, plus bundles such as Disney+/Hulu/HBO Max, which is linked from the homepage. For an index builder, the logged-out site is a server-rendered catalogue: every title page carries Movie or TVSeries JSON-LD and a full content record that includes the start and end of the title's licence window. There is no XML sitemap. Discovery runs through HTML sitemap pages per country and language. Playback, and the GraphQL API the pages are built from, are closed. Compare disneyplus.com, which opens XML sitemaps and AI crawlers but hides availability dates.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where HBO Max departs from the default platform assumptions.

The edge, not robots.txt, sets crawler policy

robots.txt allows everyone except /search/, but Akamai refuses ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, CCBot, Bytespider and generic HTTP libraries by UA string, while GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot and Meta's agent get through (Probe Log). Googlebot passes without IP verification. The same page serves every UA, so there is no curated SEO sliver. Treat robots.txt as incomplete and record the UA you crawl as.

The UUID outlives every rename

One hbomaxId per title and per episode has survived HBO Max → Max → HBO Max and the 2025 URL change from /movies/{slug}/{uuid} to /movie/{uuid} (Wayback diff). Key on the UUID, discard slugs, and map max.com, play.hbomax.com and old slug paths onto it. The same UUID is used in every country path.

Licence windows are a first-class signal

Every title and episode states when it arrives and when it leaves, to the minute. This makes recrawling predictable: refetch at endDate to confirm removal, and at startDate for scheduled arrivals. It also supports queries other SVOD sites can't answer, such as "leaving HBO Max this month". End dates in 2099 or 2101 indicate owned or perpetual content (26% of the sample).

Read the record, not the 200KB page

About 95% of a title page is design tokens and shared rails, and the homepage is 2.3MB. The useful object is one JSON string in mappedData that begins {"imageUrlLink". Rails also carry cards with windows for about 100 related titles, which is how 33 pages produced windows for 414 titles. The show page lists only the default season's episodes.

Brand codes beat genres for provenance

The brand array (HBOTV, HBOM, WB, DC, TCM, DSC, CNN, BR, Magnolia…) gives the producing network or licensor. It separates HBO originals from library and third-party titles better than genre does, and it predicts window length. Of the 61 sampled records that carry a brand, 6 of 8 Discovery-network titles (TLC, ID, Magnolia) have 2099+ end dates, against 0 of 10 WB and 0 of 5 TCM titles. A TCM film sampled had a three-month window (calculated by Site Wise).

Country catalogues are not one corpus

Only 147 of 11,946 titles are offered in all 124 countries, and 29% are in exactly one (Verified Metrics). Latin America shares one catalogue of about 4,085 titles, while the US catalogue has 1,714 titles found nowhere else. Index availability as title × country rows, never as one site-wide flag, and run "is X on HBO Max" queries against the user's country.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: the title (movie or series), keyed by UUID, with seasons and episodes as children, each with its own window. A "title × country" availability row is a separate fact table.
  • Primary keys & joins: title UUID ↔ episode UUIDs ↔ trailer UUID ↔ image IDs under BEAM_LWM_DELIVERABLES/{titleId}/. People are plain names, with no person IDs. Join to external databases on title + release year + runtime. brand maps to a network entity.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) the 248 HTML sitemap pages for IDs and country sets (277MB uncompressed; about 30 minutes at 7s per request). 2) One title page per distinct UUID for the record and window: 11.9K pages, about 23 hours at 7s per request from one IP (calculated by Site Wise). 3) Season pages for series with more than one season. 4) Artwork from the image CDN at a fixed width.
  • Recrawl cadence: a daily diff of the sitemap pages; title pages at their window boundaries plus a weekly sweep; episode lists weekly for airing series.
  • Derivatives: embeddings of key art and stills; a licence-window timeline per title and country; normalized cast entities, since only names are given. ASR or OCR has no source here, because no open video or captions were found.
  • Demo to build: a "leaving soon / arriving soon" calendar across countries, built from sitemap diffs plus offeringDates and checked against what actually disappears.

Risks to the Index

  • Ownership churn. Skydance plans to bring HBO Max and Paramount+ together, with a bundle first (Wikipedia). A merged service could change host, IDs or catalogue overnight. Snapshot the UUID map now and watch hbomax.com redirects.
  • The UA wall is selective and can widen. The bot list already excludes Anthropic's and Common Crawl's crawlers. Any crawler that has to present a browser UA to get through would conflict with the terms, so plan for licensed feeds rather than relying on the open path.
  • Terms prohibit database building. Unlike Disney+, the HBO Max terms make no exception for search indexing. Store facts and links; mirroring synopses and art is a rights question.

Inferences

  • End dates of 2099-12-31 or 2101-01-01 mean owned or open-ended rights, not real expiries. Basis: 97 of the 109 far-future windows in the 414-title sample use one of these two values, and the rest are round dates such as 2100-01-01 and 2222-02-03, not a spread of years, and they concentrate on Discovery networks that WBD owned outright (Retrieval Notes).
  • The edge blocklist is a curated UA list, not behavioural detection. Basis: identical request timing got 200 or 403 depending only on the UA string, and an invented UA passed.
