Context

Disney+ is The Walt Disney Company's flagship subscription video service: films and series from Disney, Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars and National Geographic, plus Hulu content, ESPN content in some markets, and licensed titles. It launched on 2019-11-12 on technology from BAMTech, the former MLB Advanced Media spin-off, which is why its image and API hosts still sit under bamgrid.com. Revenue is subscription fees plus ads on the ad-supported tiers in the US, Canada and some Latin American and European markets. Video plays only to paying, logged-in subscribers under DRM. What is open is the marketing layer on www.disneyplus.com: a localized detail page for every title (synopsis, credits, genres, rating, runtime, artwork, episodes) and an editorial "Explore" article section. That layer is unusually open to machines. Every page is server-rendered, the same data is exposed as Next.js JSON, the sitemaps list every title in every region where it is offered, and robots.txt explicitly allows named AI crawlers. For an index builder this is a clean catalogue of a major studio library with region-availability signals. The constraint is legal, not technical: Disney's terms of use ban automated extraction for AI tools or datasets.

Retrieval Notes (site-specific deltas)

Only where this site differs from the default platform assumptions.

Crawl posture

The reverse of the default. robots.txt names 18 AI agents and allows them alongside search engines, and every page serves crawlers the same SSR HTML, unwalled. The block for unnamed agents (User-agent: * / Disallow: /) and the terms of use are the only gates. A generic crawler must identify as one of the named agents, and the terms still exclude non-search uses.

Identifier & dedup

The entity-{uuid} is global: the same UUID appears under every lang-region path where the title is offered. Dedup on UUID; keep per-locale titles and synopses as language variants (the de-at pages carry German titles and labels). Episodes and seasons have their own UUIDs in page JSON but no public URLs.

Retrieval unit

The title entity (film or series), with locale variants and region availability attached. For series, episodes are child records (title, summary, still) but are incomplete from the public surface.

Authoritative content field

Use MediaDetails.summary (full synopsis) and credits. The meta/JSON-LD description is a one-line tagline, and the JSON-LD name carries "Watch … | Disney+". Season numbers in JSON-LD are unreliable; take season order from the Episodes.seasons list.

Freshness & recrawl

No per-URL lastmod, so change detection means diffing entity IDs and hreflang sets between daily sitemap pulls. Additions show up as new UUIDs; licensing exits show up as a locale dropping out of a title's hreflang group.

Region & language

Browser UAs from the "wrong" country are 307-redirected on articles, but title pages answer any locale from a US IP. Missing region/title pairs return 200 soft 404s, so check hasContent, not the status code.

Indexing Plan

  • Retrieval unit: title entity (UUID) with locale variants, region set, credits, genres, rating, runtime, artwork IDs; series carry season IDs and the episode cards visible publicly.
  • Primary keys & joins: entity UUID ↔ lang-region URLs (hreflang) ↔ image IDs (ripcut) ↔ article links (editorial mentions). Cast and crew are display strings only, with no person IDs, so join to external knowledge bases by title + year + director.
  • Ingestion paths, ranked: 1) The daily sitemap pull: 183 files list every title UUID and its region set. Send Accept-Encoding: gzip and retry files that come back uncompressed: at about 150KB per gzip file, a full pass would be about 28MB instead of 3GB (calculated by Site Wise). 2) Page JSON for one locale per language per new or changed UUID. About 62KB per fetch; one locale per UUID is about 13.4K fetches, or roughly 0.8GB, for the whole catalogue (calculated by Site Wise). 3) Static-pages sitemap plus article JSON for editorial text and its entity links.
  • Recrawl cadence: sitemap daily (regenerated about 18:11 UTC); Top 10 daily from one US page; title JSON on first sight and then monthly; articles monthly; artwork on first sight (IDs change when the art does).
  • Derivatives to generate: image embeddings of key art and title logos for visual search; a cross-locale synopsis set as a parallel corpus; a region-availability matrix per title as a "where can I stream X" answer source.
  • Demo to build: a daily "what's new / what's leaving Disney+ by country" feed from consecutive sitemap diffs, joined to title metadata.

Risks to the Index

  • Terms vs robots.txt: robots.txt allows AI agents, but the terms bar automated collection for AI tools or datasets and carve out only search-engine indexing. Index as a search engine, keep display to snippet plus link, and get legal review before any training or dataset use.
  • The public catalogue is a marketing artifact. Series episode lists are incomplete, JSON-LD season numbers are wrong, and anything behind /watch//episode (playback, full episodes, continue-watching) is out of reach.
  • The Hulu integration and the Star-to-Hulu rebrand (Wikipedia: Star phased out 2025-10-08) are still moving content between brands and hosts, so title UUIDs and region sets may churn more than a stable library would.

Inferences

  • The sitemap lists the full public title catalogue per region, not a curated slice. Basis: 14 of 14 randomly sampled UUIDs resolved with content, the hreflang sets match regional storefront splits (KR-only and DACH-only titles), and the KR-only title returns a soft 404 outside its region group.
  • The geo-redirect keys on browser-like user agents, not crawler identity. Basis: on the same URL from the same IP, Chrome got a 307 while curl and an arbitrary custom UA got 200, the same as Googlebot.
